\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%==============================================================================
 
We study a two-stage distributionally robust bilevel optimization problems to model a certain class of two-person zero-sum Stackelberg game under an uncertain environment. The \emph{leader} is the first mover who commits a \emph{binary here-and-now} decision
$x \in \mathcal{X} \subseteq \{0,1\}^{d_x}$.
The \emph{follower} is the second mover who observes $x$ and chooses  a \emph{continuous here-and-now} decision $h$ subject to $Wh \leq w$ for some $W\in \mathbb{R}^{m_{1}\times d_{h}}$ and $w\in \mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}$. After the leader and follower commit decisions $(x,h)$, the random vector $\xi$ is realized, taking values in a support denoted by $\Xi \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{d_{\xi}}$. The follower selects a \emph{continuous wait-and-see} decision $y(\xi) \in \mathbb{R}_+^{d_y}$
subject to $A y(\xi) \leq b_x(\xi) + Bh$ for some $A\in \mathbb{R}^{m_{2}\times d_{y}}$ and $b_{x}(\xi)\in \mathbb{R}^{m_{2}}$, where $b_{x}(\xi)$ is the leader-parameterized right-hand side. Conditional on $x$, the
follower thus solves a two-stage stochastic linear program (2SLP).
The two players share a single linear objective function $d^\top y(\xi)$ and
pursue it in opposite directions.

This setting can be cast to \emph{bilevel optimization
under uncertainty} \citep{beck2023survey}. In any realistic
data-driven setting, the true data-generating distribution
$P^*$ of $\xi$ is not known to the leader perfectly due to
epistemic limitations such as observation noise
and finite samples; she therefore operates on her own estimate $\hat P$ of $\mathbb{P}^*$. The follower likewise commits
to $h$ by solving 2SLP under his own estimate $\tilde P$,
which may or may not coincide with $\mathbb{P}^*$ depending on the problem-specific context. A subtle issue arises that neither $\hat P$ and $\tilde P$
are guaranteed to coincide, nor does the follower have an incentive
to disclose $\tilde P$ to the leader especially in a zero-sum setting, so $\tilde P$ is
inaccessible to the leader. Following the game-theoretic convention, we call $\hat P$ the leader's
\emph{belief}~\citep{harsanyi1995games} and $\tilde P$ the follower's. Consequently, the leader faces distributional ambiguity not only for the true data distribution $\mathbb{P}^*$, but also the follower's belief $\tilde{P}$.

Two distributional ambiguities in $\mathbb{P}^*$ and in
$\tilde P$ can significantly harm the leader's program as follows.
Ambiguity in $\mathbb{P}^*$ renders the leader's risk evaluation
$\mathcal{R}^{\hat P}\big[d^\top y(\xi)\big]$ taken via some risk
functional $\mathcal{R}: L^2(\hat P) \to \mathbb{R}$ under her own
belief $\hat P$ vulnerable to distributional misspecification, the
standard \emph{data uncertainty} concern of the DRO literature. Ambiguity in $\tilde P$, by contrast, propagates into the
follower's lower-level problem whose optimal solution set, parameterized by $\tilde P$, is consequently unknown to the leader,
yielding additional concern termed \emph{decision uncertainty}
\citep{beck2023survey}.

We consider an \emph{ambiguity-averse leader}: she hedges against
both distributional ambiguities --- about $\mathbb{P}^*$ and about
$\tilde P$ --- through an ambiguity set $\mathcal{D}$ of candidate
pairs $(P, \tilde P)$. The follower, by contrast, is a standard
risk-neutral 2SLP player: he maximizes his expected payoff under
his single belief $\tilde P$. When the follower's argmax is not a
singleton, we adopt the \emph{pessimistic} convention, under which
the leader hedges against the response least favorable to her own
objective---a
reasonable assumption against an adversarial follower as in our setting. The resulting distributionally robust bilevel program is formulated as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:DR}
    \min_{x \,\in\, \mathcal{X}}\; \sup_{(P,\, \tilde P) \,\in\, \mathcal{D}}\; \max_{\tilde h } \Big\{ \mathcal{R}^P\!\big[Q(x, \tilde h, \xi)\big] \,:\, \tilde h \in \arg\max_{\bar h \,\in\, H}\, \mathbb{E}^{\tilde P}\!\big[Q(x, \bar h, \xi)\big] \Big\},
\end{equation}
where $Q : \mathcal{X} \times H \times \Xi \to \mathbb{R}$ is the follower's optimal recourse payoff, defined by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Q-def}
    Q(x, h, \xi) \,:=\, \max_{\bar y \,\in\, \mathbb{R}^{d_y}}\big\{ d^\top \bar y \,:\, A\bar y \leq b_x(\xi) + Bh \big\}.
\end{equation}

One natural class of problems to which our framework applies is
that of \emph{network interdiction games} (NIGs) \citep{Wood1993}.
An NIG is a zero-sum Stackelberg game on a directed network in
which the leader (the \emph{interdictor}) selects a subset of arcs
to disable subject to a budget, while the follower (the
\emph{flow player}) routes flow on the perturbed network. Consider
an illicit supply chain disrupted by a law-enforcement agency. The
agency (leader) and the smuggler (follower) rely on different
sensing infrastructure, observational histories, and operational
viewpoints; their internal models of the random arc capacities and
the effects of interdiction diverge even when both are exposed to
the same underlying phenomenon.


Bilevel optimization (BO) has been widely used to model hierarchical
decision-making in operations research, with applications including
pricing and tolls \cite{brotcorne2000bilevel}, energy and gas markets
\cite{ambrosius2020endogenous}, defense of critical
infrastructure \cite{brown2006defending}, and transportation network
design~\cite{ben1992construction}. More recently, BO under
uncertainty has emerged as an active research direction that
integrates hierarchical optimization with stochastic and robust
optimization paradigms \cite{beck2023survey}. The majority of the
methodology and algorithms developed in this area addresses a
\emph{wait-and-see} follower. The stochastic stream originates in the
work of \cite{ patriksson1999stochastic}, who
generalize two-stage stochastic programs to the bilevel setting. One seminal paper of \cite{yanikoglu2018decision} studied both optimistic and pessimistic two-stage stochastic bilevel program with a binary leader
and a \emph{convex} wait-and-see follower, providing exact semi-infinite
reformulations with tractable decision rule approximations under general probability distributions. The stochastic bilevel models have been extended to address more complex structure of the follower's problems~\cite{zhang2021bilevel,tavasliouglu2019solving}, and risk-averse structure of the leader's model~\cite{burtscheidt2020risk}. The robust stream is comparatively less
developed, with the complexity of robust bilevel problems under an uncertain follower's objective characterized by \cite{buchheim2021robust, kis2025robust}. The distributionally robust stream was investigated by \cite{goyal2023decision}, as an extension of \cite{yanikoglu2018decision}, who show that when the
follower's problem is convex and decided in a wait-and-see fashion,
the pessimistic DR-bilevel program admits, under mild assumptions, an
equivalent two-stage DRO problem with uncertainty in the objective, a
structural result we generalize below.

In contrast, BO under uncertainty with a \emph{here-and-now} follower
has received comparatively limited attention. As a general framework,
\cite{beck2023knapsack, beck2026heuristic} developed exact and
heuristic solution methods for so-called \emph{$\Gamma$-robust}
mixed-integer linear bilevel problems with packing-type lower levels.
In the applied literature, the variant has been investigated almost
exclusively within network interdiction. \cite{Lei2018} initiated this
line with a stochastic maximum-flow interdiction model in which the
follower's lower-level problem is a two-stage stochastic program with
a here-and-now capacity recovery and a wait-and-see maximum flow, a
structure that we adopt in our computational study. Of particular
relevance to the present work, the recognition that the leader and
the follower may form different estimates of the same uncertain
network parameters has been framed as a question of \emph{asymmetric
uncertainty} between the two players and studied by
\cite{azizi2024shortest, punla2024shortest}. The closest precedent to our setting is
\cite{ketkov2025data}, in which the leader and the follower each
construct a Wasserstein ambiguity set from their own data and the
leader hedges against incomplete knowledge of the follower's data,
but the follower is itself ambiguity-averse and self-robustifies over
its private ambiguity set, so the lower-level response is a
deterministic mapping of the leader's decision. A unifying feature of
this literature is that the leader is assumed to know either the
follower's distribution or uncertainty set, which is
the natural complete-information benchmark in game-theoretic terms.
The present work relaxes this assumption by modeling the follower's
belief as private to the leader and introducing a leader-side
ambiguity set over plausible follower beliefs, which in turn induces
uncertainty in the follower's lower-level optimal decision.

The literature reviewed above is concerned exclusively with \emph{data
uncertainty}. A separate source identified in the BO literature is
\emph{decision uncertainty}, in which the follower's response is
itself uncertain to the leader even when the data are deterministic.
The survey of \cite{beck2023survey} groups the existing work into two
main strands, namely bounded rationality of the follower
\cite{zare2020inexact, shi2023mixed} and limited observability of
the leader's decision by the follower \cite{beck2021robust}. Data
uncertainty and decision uncertainty have so far been treated in
isolation. The present work lies at the interface between them. Data uncertainty and decision uncertainty have so far been treated as
separate sources. The present work lies at the interface between
them. Ambiguity over the true data-generating distribution of the
shared uncertain parameters gives rise to a follower-side private belief that is ambiguous to the leader, and the
resulting belief-side ambiguity makes the follower's lower-level
response uncertain. Belief-side ambiguity therefore couples decision uncertainty to data
uncertainty within a single modeling primitive, rather than treating
the two sources independently.

We summarize the contributions of this paper as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item  We study a risk-averse two-stage distributionally robust bilevel program with the \emph{here-and-now} follower. The lower-level problem is a 2SLP whose right-hand side is parameterized by both the leader's decision and the follower's ambiguous belief.

\item We analyze the structural properties of the above bilevel program, including the well-definedness of the robust counterpart and the computational complexity. 

\item We derive a computationally viable min--max reformulation in
which, when both ambiguity sets are total-variation balls over finite
scenarios, the inner maximization reduces to a continuous bilinear
program over a polyhedron independent of $x$. Building on this form,
we develop an exact solution approach via Benders decomposition.
\end{enumerate}


In the remainder we states the model primitives and preliminary results in Section~\ref{sec:problem}. In Section~\ref{sec:reformulation} develops two distributionally robust
reformulations. Section~\ref{sec:complexity} establishes the
complexity. Section~\ref{sec:benders} develops the Benders algorithm.
Section~\ref{sec:experiments} reports numerical experiments.
Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.

\paragraph{Notation}
All probability distributions in what follows are Borel
probability measures on $\Xi$, denoted $\mathcal{P}(\Xi)$. For
$P \in \mathcal{P}(\Xi)$, $\mathcal{L}^2(P)$ denotes the space of
$\mathbb{R}^k$-valued $P$-square-integrable Borel functions on $\Xi$,
and $\mathbb{E}^P[\cdot]$ denotes expectation under $P$; for discrete
$P = (p_s)_{s=1}^S$ we write
$\mathbb{E}^p[Z] = \sum_s p_s Z_s$. The probability simplex on $S$
scenarios is $\Delta^S$. Following standard SP convention
\citep{shapiro2014lectures}, we identify the
sample space with $\Xi$ and suppress the underlying
$(\Omega, \Sigma)$. Vectors and matrices are in lowercase and
uppercase Roman type; $\mathbb{R}_+^n$ denotes nonnegative reals.
Hats $\,\hat{}\,$ and tildes $\,\tilde{}\,$ distinguish objects
associated with the leader's belief $\hat P$ and the follower's
belief $\tilde P$, respectively.

\section{Model Assumptions and Nominal Counterpart}
\label{sec:problem}
%==============================================================================
Our main results rest on below assumptions.
\begin{assumption}[Risk measure]\label{asm:coherent}
The leader's risk functional $\mathcal{R}:\mathcal{L}^{2}(P)\to\mathbb{R}$
satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep, label=(\roman*)]
\item $\mathcal{R}$ is a coherent risk measure in the basic sense
of \cite{rockafellar2007coherent} (convexity, monotonicity,
closedness, positive homogeneity, and $\mathcal{R}(C)=C$ for constant
$C$). Equivalently, it admits
the dual representation~\citep[Theorem 3.1]{rockafellar2007coherent}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:envelope}
    \mathcal{R}^{P}[Z] \;=\; \sup_{\rho \,\in\, \mathcal{Q}(P)}\;
    \mathbb{E}^\rho[Z],
\end{equation}
where the \emph{risk envelope} $\mathcal{Q}(P) \subseteq
\mathcal{P}(\Xi)$ is a closed convex set of probability measures
absolutely continuous with respect to $P$
\item $\mathcal{R}$ is an optimized certainty equivalent (OCE)induced by a disutility function $g$ \citep{ben2007old}, i.e., for
any loss $Z$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:OCE}
    \mathcal{R}^{P}[Z] \;=\; \inf_{\tau \,\in\, \mathbb{R}}\;
    \Big\{ \tau + \mathbb{E}^{P}\!\big[\,g(Z-\tau)\,\big] \Big\},
\end{equation}
where the disutility function $g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ is convex
(hence continuous) with $g(0)=0$ and $g(\tau)>\tau$ for all
$\tau\neq 0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

The two representations serve distinct purposes. The risk
envelope~\eqref{eq:envelope} underlies the single-level reformulation
of Lemma~\ref{lem:dual}, while the OCE form~\eqref{eq:OCE} has an inner
expectation that is linear in $P$, which is what allows the worst-case
risk to be recast as a worst-case expectation in
Proposition~\ref{prop:DRO-decomposition}.

A representative instance of the measure satisfying both is the \emph{conditional value-at-risk} $\mathrm{CVaR}_\beta$,
induced by $g(\tau)=\tfrac{1}{1-\beta}\max[\tau,0]$, which we adopt
in numerical experiment; more generally, the two properties hold jointly for the
\emph{spectral risk measures}~\citep{wang2020robust}, i.e.\ convex
combinations of $\mathrm{CVaR}$ at distinct levels of $\beta$.

\begin{remark}[Risk-neutral follower]\label{rem:follower-risk}
The follower's objective could likewise be modeled by a risk measure
satisfying Assumption~\ref{asm:coherent}. Doing so, however, requires
the leader to know the follower's risk-attitude level (e.g.\ the
level $\beta$ for $\mathrm{CVaR}_\beta$), which in practice may be at least
as uncertain as the follower's belief $\tilde P$ itself. We therefore take the
follower to be risk-neutral, which keeps the source of
follower-side ambiguity confined to the belief $\tilde P$. Generalizing to the risk-averse follower with ambiguous risk attitudes is left to future work.
\end{remark}

\begin{assumption}[Linear linking constraints]\label{asm:interdiction}
$b_x(\xi) = u_{0}+(U_0 + U_x)\xi$ with
$u_{0}\in \mathbb{R}^{m_{2}},\;U_0, U_x \in \mathbb{R}^{m_{2} \times d_\xi}$ and
$U_x = \sum_{i=1}^{d_x} U_i x_i$ for
$U_i \in \mathbb{R}^{m_{2} \times d_\xi}$, $i = 1, \ldots, d_x$.
\end{assumption}

Assumption~\ref{asm:interdiction} is standard in linear SP/BO literature~\citep{yanikoglu2018decision, goyal2023decision} in the sense that each uncertainty (leader's decision) is in the right-hand-side of the recourse (lower-level) problem, respectively.

\begin{assumption}[Relatively complete recourse]\label{asm:RCR}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep, label=(\roman*)]
\item $H$ is non-empty and compact.
\item For every $(x, h) \in \mathcal{X} \times H$, the polyhedron
$\mathcal{Y}(x, h, \xi) := \{y \geq 0 : Ay \leq b_x(\xi) + Bh\}$ is
non-empty and bounded for almost every $\xi \in \Xi$ under every
$P \in \hat{\mathcal{D}} \cup \tilde{\mathcal{D}}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

Assumption~\ref{asm:RCR} is also a mild assumption in SP literature, which ensures that $Q(x, h, \xi)$ has finite
optimal value uniformly over $\mathcal{X} \times H \times \Xi$.

\begin{assumption}[Well-definedness of the ambiguity set]\label{asm:ambiguity}
The joint ambiguity set $\mathcal{D}$ satisfies the following.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item (Rectangularity) We construct two ambiguity sets $\hat{\mathcal{D}}, \tilde{\mathcal{D}} \subseteq \mathcal{P}(\Xi)$, both centered at the leader's belief $\hat{P}$, such that $\mathcal{D} = \hat{\mathcal{D}} \times \tilde{\mathcal{D}}$. Equivalently, the candidates $P$ and $\tilde{P}$ vary independently of each other.
        \item (Weakly compactness with finite second moments) Both $\hat{\mathcal{D}}, \tilde{\mathcal{D}}$ are weakly compact in the sense of \cite[Theorem 2.1.]{shapiro2002minimax}, and $\sup_{P \in \hat{\mathcal{D}} \cup \tilde{\mathcal{D}}} \int_\Xi \|\xi\|^2\, \mathrm{d}P(\xi) < \infty$.
    \item (Finite convex reformulations for uncertainty quantification) for every measurable $Z$ bounded on $\Xi$, the worst-case expectation problems $\sup_{P \in \hat{\mathcal{D}}}\;\mathbb{E}^{P}[Z]$ and $\sup_{\tilde{P} \in \tilde{\mathcal{D}}}\;\mathbb{E}^{\tilde{P}}[Z]$ admit finite convex dual reformulations under zero duality gap, in the sense of \cite[Theorems 7 and 11]{zhen2025unified}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

The rectangualrity assumption~\ref{asm:ambiguity} allows the leader to hedge against two distinct
uncertainties --- about nature's $\mathbb{P}^*$ and about the follower's
private $\tilde{P}$ --- in a separate manner.
Without it, the leader would face a single joint ambiguity over
$(P^*, \tilde{P})$, which is generally less tractable; the dependent case is left to future work. Weakly compactness assumption and finite convex reformulation assumption guarantee that the distributionally robust approach~\eqref{eq:DR} is well-defined and computationally tractable, as described in Section~\ref{sec:reformulation}.

\begin{definition}[Explicit scenario form]\label{def:explicit-scenario}
A distribution $P^{0} \in \mathcal{P}(\Xi)$ is in \emph{explicit scenario form} if it is supported on an exogenously specified finite set $\Xi^{0} = \{\xi^{1}, \ldots, \xi^{S}\} \subseteq \Xi$ with weights $q^{0} \in \Delta^{S}$, where the scenario set $\Xi^{0}$ and its cardinality $S$ are part of the problem input.
\end{definition}
\begin{assumption}[Leader-side belief in explicit scenario form]\label{asm:explicit-scenario}
The leader's nominal belief $\hat P$ is in explicit scenario form (Definition~\ref{def:explicit-scenario}), supported on $\hat\Xi = \{\xi^{1}, \ldots, \xi^{S}\}$ with weights $\hat q \in \Delta^{S}$.
\end{assumption}

Assumption~\ref{asm:explicit-scenario} is the standard setting in DRO literature where the center of the ambiguity set is usually assumed to be finitely supported scenarios~\citep{mohajerin2018data}. Further, Definition~\ref{def:explicit-scenario} is used to clarify the computational complexity of any models in our subsequent analysis, since even exact evaluation of the general 2SLP can be $\#$P-hard~\citep{hanasusanto2016comment}, while the case when the distribution is in explicit scenario form yields polynomially-sized LPs.

% Assumption~\ref{asm:explicit-scenario} isolates
% the structural complexity contributed by the distributionally robust
% bilevel layers from the standard $\#P$-hardness of two-stage SLP
% evaluation \citep{hanasusanto2016comment}: under continuous support,
% evaluating follower's stochastic program $\mathbb{E}^{\tilde P}\left[Q(x,h,\xi)\right]$ in \eqref{eq:DR} is already $\#P$-hard for any fixed $(x,h)$, with given any belief $\tilde P$.

Based on Assumptions~\ref{asm:coherent}--\ref{asm:ambiguity}, let us define the leader-side value function $V : \mathcal{X} \times \hat{\mathcal{D}} \times \tilde{\mathcal{D}} \to \mathbb{R}$ for each fixed $(P, \tilde P) \in \mathcal{D}$, by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:V-def}
    V(x; P, \tilde P) \,:=\, \sup_{\tilde h \,\in\, \Psi(x,\tilde P)}\,
    \mathcal{R}^P\!\big[Q(x, \tilde h, \xi)\big],
\end{equation}
where $\Psi(x,\tilde P) := \arg\max_{\bar h \in H} \mathbb{E}^{\tilde P}[Q(x,\bar h,\xi)]$
is the lower-level solution mapping (also known as \emph{rational reaction set}) under belief $\tilde P$, a set-valued map
$\Psi : \mathcal{X} \times \tilde{\mathcal{D}} \rightrightarrows H$.
Then the problem \eqref{eq:DR} can be rewritten as follows:
\begin{align}\label{eq:DR_separate}
    \min_{x\in \mathcal{X}}\sup_{(P,\tilde P) \in \hat{\mathcal{D}} \times \tilde{\mathcal{D}}}\;V(x;P,\tilde P).
\end{align}
We now derive a single-level reformulation of $V(x;\cdot)$ at some fixed (nominal) pair of distributions 
$(P, \tilde P) \in \hat{\mathcal{D}} \times \tilde{\mathcal{D}}$. 

\begin{lemma}[Single-level reformulation of $V(x;\cdot )$]\label{lem:dual}
Under Assumptions~\ref{asm:interdiction}--\ref{asm:RCR}, for any
$(P, \tilde P) \in \hat{\mathcal{D}} \times \tilde{\mathcal{D}}$ and
$x \in \mathcal{X}$,
\begin{subequations}
\begin{align}\label{eq:dual-compact}
    V(x, P, \tilde P) = \sup_{\rho \,\in\, \mathcal{Q}(P)} \inf\Big\{ \mathbb{E}^\rho[b_x(\xi)^\top \hat\pi(\xi)] + \mathbb{E}^{\tilde P}[b_x(\xi)^\top \tilde\pi(\xi) - d^\top \tilde y(\xi)] + w\nu \Big\},
\end{align}
where the infimum is over
$(\hat\pi(\cdot), \tilde\pi(\cdot), \tilde y(\cdot), \tilde h, \lambda, \nu)$
subject to
\label{eq:dual-constraints}
\begin{align}
    & A^\top \hat\pi(\xi) \,\geq\, d,\quad \hat\pi(\xi) \,\geq\, 0,\quad P\text{-a.s.}, \label{eq:CL}\\
    & A^\top \tilde\pi(\xi) \,\geq\, \lambda d,\quad \tilde\pi(\xi) \,\geq\, 0,\quad \tilde P\text{-a.s.}, \label{eq:CF}\\
    & A\tilde y(\xi) \,\leq\, \lambda b_x(\xi) + B\tilde h,\quad \tilde y(\xi) \,\geq\, 0,\quad \tilde P\text{-a.s.},\quad \tilde h \,\geq\, 0, \label{eq:CP}\\
    & W\tilde h \,\leq\, \lambda w,\quad \lambda \,\geq\, 0,\quad \nu \,\geq\, 0, \label{eq:CB}\\
    & \mathbb{E}^\rho[-B^\top \hat\pi(\xi)] + \mathbb{E}^{\tilde P}[-B^\top \tilde\pi(\xi)] + W^\top \nu \,\geq\, 0. \label{eq:CC}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
\end{lemma}
 \begin{proof}
Please see the detailed proof in Appendix~\ref{ch:proof_chap3}.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}[Nominal counterpart]
For a fixed pair $(P, \tilde P) \in \hat{\mathcal{D}} \times \tilde{\mathcal{D}}$, an optimization problem $\min_{x\in \mathcal{X}}V(x;P,\tilde P)$ is the \emph{nominal} stochastic bilevel program counterpart of~\eqref{eq:DR}, in which the leader is taken to perfectly know both the true distribution and the follower's belief. If such a pair, $V(x, P, \tilde P)$ is in explicit scenario form defined above, \eqref{eq:dual-compact} is a deterministic-equivalent linear program while for a general (possibly continuous) pair, the exact evaluation is already $\#$P-hard in general.
\end{remark}
 \begin{remark}
The formulation~\eqref{eq:DR} extends without structural change when the leader objective is augmented to a deterministic cost $c^{\top}x$ and the follower objective is augmented to $d_{0}^{\top}h + d^{\top}y(\xi)$ in both the follower's 2SLP and the leader's risk, since $d_{0}^{\top}\tilde h$ is deterministic given $\tilde h$ and factors out of coherent risk $\mathcal{R}$. We retain $d^{\top}y(\xi)$ alone to keep the essential structure transparent.
\end{remark}
%==============================================================================
\section{Structural Analysis and Reformulations}
\label{sec:reformulation}
%==============================================================================

We develop two reformulations of the distributionally robust value
$V^{*}_\beta(x)$ in~\eqref{eq:DR}, both arising from a single
decomposition of the dual single-level form of
Lemma~\ref{lem:dual}. The first, in
Section~\ref{subsec:representations}, recasts $V^{*}_\beta(x)$ as the
leader's worst-case risk over a set of admissible follower responses.
It separates the effects of the two ambiguity sets, with
$\hat{\mathcal{D}}$ entering the risk objective and $\tilde{\mathcal{D}}$
determining the set of responses against which the leader must hedge. The second, in Section~\ref{subsec:algorithmic},
encodes that admissibility through a Lagrange multiplier rather than
through the response set itself, yielding a min--max formulation whose
feasible set is fixed and independent of $x$. The first reformulation
supplies the interpretation, while the second supplies the form on
which the algorithm of Section~\ref{sec:benders} operates.


\subsection{Set-based representation of decision uncertainty}
\label{subsec:representations}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

The dual single-level form of Lemma~\ref{lem:dual} couples the
leader-side envelope measure $\rho$ and the follower-side measure
$\tilde{P}$ through the single constraint~\eqref{eq:CC}; the remaining
constraints involve one side only. Relaxing~\eqref{eq:CC} with a
multiplier and using the product structure
$\mathcal{D} = \hat{\mathcal{D}} \times \tilde{\mathcal{D}}$ separates
$V^{*}_\beta(x)$ into two independent two-stage distributionally robust
subproblems, one per ambiguity set, coupled only through the
multiplier.

\begin{proposition}[Risk--response decomposition]\label{prop:DRO-decomposition}
Let the assumptions of Section~\ref{sec:problem} hold and fix $x \in \mathcal{X}$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}, nosep]
\item \emph{(Decomposition.)} The leader's worst-case value decomposes into a
leader-side worst-case risk problem and a follower-side worst-case expectation
problem, coupled through a single multiplier $\alpha$,
\begin{subequations}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:V-cartesian-named}
    \sup_{(P, \tilde{P}) \,\in\, \hat{\mathcal{D}} \times \tilde{\mathcal{D}}}
    V(x; P, \tilde{P}) \;=\; \sup_{\alpha \,\in\, H}\;
    \big[\, \bar{V}^{L}(x, \alpha) \;+\; \bar{V}^{F}(x, \alpha)\, \big],
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align*}
    &\bar{V}^{L}(x, \alpha) := \sup_{P \,\in\, \hat{\mathcal{D}}}\;
    \mathcal{R}^{P}\!\big[Q(x, \alpha, \xi)\big]=\inf_{\tau}\Big\{\tau + \sup_{P \in \hat{\mathcal{D}}}
    \mathbb{E}^{P}[g(Q(x,\alpha,\xi)-\tau)]\Big\}\\
    &\bar{V}^{F}(x, \alpha) := \inf_{\substack{\lambda \geq 0,\\ \tilde{h},\, \tilde{\pi}(\cdot),\, \tilde{y}(\cdot)}}\;
    \sup_{\tilde{P} \,\in\, \tilde{\mathcal{D}}}\;
    \mathbb{E}^{\tilde{P}}\!\big[(b_x(\xi)+B\alpha)^{\!\top}\tilde{\pi}(\xi) - d^{\!\top}\tilde{y}(\xi)\big]
    \;: \eqref{eq:CF}\text{--}\eqref{eq:CB}.
\end{align*}
\end{subequations}
The multiplier $\alpha$ is the Lagrange multiplier of the coupling
constraint~\eqref{eq:CC}; the leader-side ambiguity set $\hat{\mathcal{D}}$
enters only $\bar{V}^{L}$, and the follower-side set $\tilde{\mathcal{D}}$
enters only $\bar{V}^{F}$.
\item \emph{(Best-response indicator.)} With the lower-level solution-set mapping
$\Psi(x,\tilde{P})$ of~\eqref{eq:V-def},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:g2-dichotomy}
    \bar{V}^{F}(x, \alpha) =
    \begin{cases}
        0 & \alpha \in \Psi(x, \tilde{P}) \ \text{for some}\ \tilde{P} \in \tilde{\mathcal{D}}, \\
        -\infty & \text{otherwise}.
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Whenever finite, $\alpha$ is itself an optimal lower-level solution under that
$\tilde{P}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
 \begin{proof}
Please see the detailed proof in Appendix~\ref{ch:proof_chap3}.
\end{proof}


Part~(i) separates the two ambiguity sets. The leader-side set $\hat{\mathcal{D}}$
enters only the risk objective $\bar{V}^{L}$, a worst-case risk over data
uncertainty, and the follower-side set $\tilde{\mathcal{D}}$ enters only
$\bar{V}^{F}$. Part~(ii) shows that $\bar{V}^{F}$ contributes nothing beyond a
feasibility restriction, being finite exactly when $\alpha$ is an optimal
lower-level solution under some plausible belief and $-\infty$ otherwise. Although
$\alpha$ enters as the Lagrange multiplier of the coupling
constraint~\eqref{eq:CC}, part~(ii) identifies it with such a solution, so the
outer maximization over $\alpha$ ranges over the follower responses that
$\tilde{\mathcal{D}}$ deems plausible. Maximizing the leader's risk over $\alpha$ is
therefore a hedge against the follower's uncertain decision, the
decision-uncertainty counterpart of the data-uncertainty hedge carried by
$\bar{V}^{L}$. This motivates collecting the uncertain follower decisions over every plausible beliefs into a single
set.
 

 \begin{definition}[Distributionally ambiguous reaction set]\label{def:DRO-admissible-response}
For $x \in \mathcal{X}$, the \emph{distributionally ambiguous reaction set} is the union of the
follower's rational reaction sets over all plausible beliefs,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A-star-D}
    \Psi(x, \tilde{\mathcal{D}}) \;:=\; \bigcup_{\tilde{P} \,\in\, \tilde{\mathcal{D}}}\,
    \Psi(x, \tilde{P}),
\end{equation}
the set of here-and-now decisions optimal for the follower under some plausible
belief $\tilde{P} \in \tilde{\mathcal{D}}$.
\end{definition}

Substituting the indicator~\eqref{eq:g2-dichotomy} into the
separation~\eqref{eq:V-cartesian-named} confines the outer maximization to
$\Psi(x,\tilde{\mathcal{D}})$ and leaves the risk objective, giving the following.

\begin{theorem}[Reaction-set representation]\label{thm:game-theoretic}
Under the assumptions of Section~\ref{sec:problem},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:V-game}
    \sup_{(P, \tilde{P}) \in \hat{\mathcal{D}} \times \tilde{\mathcal{D}}}\!
    V(x; P, \tilde{P})
    \;=\; \max_{\alpha \,\in\, \Psi(x, \tilde{\mathcal{D}})}\;
    \sup_{P \,\in\, \hat{\mathcal{D}}}\; \mathcal{R}^{P}\!\big[Q(x, \alpha, \xi)\big].
\end{equation}
The maximum is attained, $V^{*}(x)$ is finite for every $x \in \mathcal{X}$, and is
weakly increasing in the follower-side radius $\tilde{\varepsilon}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Please see the detailed proof in Appendix~\ref{ch:proof_chap3}.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:game-theoretic} recasts the leader's problem as a robust
optimization over both uncertainty sources. The inner $\sup_{P}$ hedges against
data uncertainty at a fixed response $\alpha$, and the outer $\max_{\alpha}$ hedges
against decision uncertainty over the lower-level solutions that
$\tilde{\mathcal{D}}$ deems plausible. The set $\tilde{\mathcal{D}}$ determines which
$\alpha$ are admissible, and $\hat{\mathcal{D}}$ determines the risk-adjusted
objective at each fixed $\alpha$.

\begin{remark}[Convex pieces, non-convex union]\label{rem:geometry}
Each $\Psi(x,\tilde{P})$ is convex and compact, since $Q(x,\cdot,\xi)$ is the
optimal value of an LP with $\bar{h}$ in the right-hand side and is therefore
concave in $\bar{h}$, so $\mathbb{E}^{\tilde{P}}[Q(x,\cdot,\xi)]$ is concave on the
convex set $H$. The decision-uncertainty set $\Psi(x,\tilde{\mathcal{D}})$ is the
union of these pieces over $\tilde{P} \in \tilde{\mathcal{D}}$, which is compact but
generally non-convex, as Example~\ref{ex:convex-vs-nonconvex} shows. This
non-convexity is what prevents direct optimization of~\eqref{eq:V-game} over
$\alpha$ and motivates the multiplier-based reformulation of
Section~\ref{subsec:algorithmic}.
\end{remark}

\begin{example}[Geometry of $\mathcal{A}^{*}(x, \tilde{\mathcal{D}})$]
\label{ex:convex-vs-nonconvex}
Throughout we fix $x = 0$, $S = 2$, $\tilde q = (\tfrac{1}{2}, \tfrac{1}{2})$, 
and TV radius $\tilde \varepsilon = \tfrac{1}{2}$, so that 
$\tilde{\mathcal{D}} = \Delta^{2}$. Two instances of the maximum-flow 
NIG~\eqref{eq:Q-NIG} are compared.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(a) Standard NIG.} Series network $s \to v \to t$ with 
$|\mathcal{A}| = 2$ interdictable arcs $e_{1}, e_{2}$, recovery budget 
$w = 1$ in the standard polyhedron $H = \{h \in \mathbb{R}_{+}^{2} : h_{1} + h_{2} \leq 1\}$
of Section~\ref{subsec:NIG-model}, and scenario capacities 
$c(\xi^{1}) = (10, 0)$, $c(\xi^{2}) = (0, 10)$. Substituting into~\eqref{eq:Q-NIG}, 
the max-flow value reduces to $Q(0, h, \xi^{s}) = \min\{c_{1}(\xi^{s}) + h_{1},\, c_{2}(\xi^{s}) + h_{2}\}$, 
which gives $Q(0, h, \xi^{1}) = h_{2}$ and $Q(0, h, \xi^{2}) = h_{1}$ 
for $w \leq 10$. Direct computation gives
\begin{equation*}
    \mathcal{A}^{*}(0, e_{1}) = \{(0, 1)\},
    \quad
    \mathcal{A}^{*}(0, e_{2}) = \{(1, 0)\},
    \quad
    \mathcal{A}^{*}(0, \tilde q) = \big\{(t, 1 - t) : t \in [0, 1]\big\},
\end{equation*}
and every other $\tilde p \in \Delta^{2}$ yields one of the two vertices. 
Hence
\begin{equation*}
    \mathcal{A}^{*}(0, \tilde{\mathcal{D}}) 
    = \big\{(t, 1 - t) : t \in [0, 1]\big\},
\end{equation*}
the budget edge of $H$, which is convex 
(Figure~\ref{fig:admissible-geometry}(a)).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(b) Per-arc-capped variant.} This instance replaces 
the single-budget polyhedron of~\eqref{eq:Q-NIG} with the per-arc cap 
$H = [0, 1]^{2}$, retaining the general form $H = \{h \geq 0 : Wh \leq w\}$
of Assumption~\ref{asm:RCR}(ii). The network has two parallel paths 
$s \to v_{k} \to t$, $k = 1, 2$, with $c_{k}(\xi^{s}) = 10\,\mathbb{I}\{s = k\}$
on arc $(s, v_{k})$ and zero pre-recovery capacity on arc $(v_{k}, t)$.
The max-flow value~\eqref{eq:Q-NIG} reduces to 
$Q(0, h, \xi^{1}) = h_{1}$ and $Q(0, h, \xi^{2}) = h_{2}$. Direct 
computation gives
\begin{equation*}
    \mathcal{A}^{*}(0, e_{1}) = \{1\} \times [0, 1],
    \quad
    \mathcal{A}^{*}(0, e_{2}) = [0, 1] \times \{1\},
    \quad
    \mathcal{A}^{*}(0, \tilde p) = \{(1, 1)\}
\end{equation*}
for every $\tilde p \in \mathrm{int}\,\Delta^{2}$. Hence
\begin{equation*}
    \mathcal{A}^{*}(0, \tilde{\mathcal{D}})
    = \big(\{1\} \times [0, 1]\big) \,\cup\, \big([0, 1] \times \{1\}\big),
\end{equation*}
two edges of $H$ meeting at $(1, 1)$ 
(Figure~\ref{fig:admissible-geometry}(b)). The midpoint 
$(\tfrac{1}{2}, \tfrac{1}{2})$ of $(1, 0)$ and $(0, 1)$ lies in the 
interior of $H$ and in no argmax set. The union is connected and 
compact but not convex.
\end{example}

\noindent
Both instances inherit the connectedness and compactness of 
$\mathcal{A}^{*}(x, \tilde{\mathcal{D}})$ from 
Theorem~\ref{thm:game-theoretic}, but their convexity diverges. The 
algorithmic reformulation of Section~\ref{subsec:algorithmic} encodes 
admissibility through the Lagrange multiplier $\alpha \in H$ and Proposition~\ref{prop:pieces}, sidestepping direct 
optimization over the (possibly non-convex) union.


The next result isolates a regime in which the belief heterogeneity collapses.

\begin{proposition}[Reduction under risk neutrality and containment]\label{prop:saturation}
Suppose the leader is risk-neutral, $\mathcal{R}^{P} = \mathbb{E}^{P}$, and
$\hat{\mathcal{D}} \subseteq \tilde{\mathcal{D}}$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:saturation}
    V^{*}(x) \;=\; \sup_{P \,\in\, \hat{\mathcal{D}}}\;
    \max_{\bar{h} \,\in\, H}\;
    \mathbb{E}^{P}\!\big[Q(x, \bar{h}, \xi)\big],
\end{equation}
which is independent of the follower-side radius $\tilde{\varepsilon}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Please see the detailed proof in 
Appendix~\ref{ch:proof_chap3}.
\end{proof}

Under risk neutrality and containment the value depends only on $\hat{\mathcal{D}}$,
and the heterogeneous-belief structure reduces to single-distribution DRO. This
regime includes the symmetric calibration $\hat{\varepsilon} = \tilde{\varepsilon}$,
used as a benchmark in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}. A risk-averse leader
($\mathcal{R}^{P} \neq \mathbb{E}^{P}$) admits no such reduction, since a follower
expectation maximizer need not be optimal for the leader's risk, so
$\tilde{\varepsilon}$ continues to influence $V^{*}(x)$.

Because $\Psi(x,\tilde{\mathcal{D}})$ is generally non-convex
(Remark~\ref{rem:geometry}), the representation~\eqref{eq:V-game} does not by itself
yield a tractable optimization over $\alpha$. We therefore retain the multiplier
$\alpha \in H$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:risk-response}, whose feasible set is fixed
and independent of $x$, and build the min--max reformulation in the next subsection.
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Min--max reformulation}
\label{subsec:algorithmic}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

Theorem~\ref{thm:game-theoretic} expresses $V^{*}(x)$ as a maximization over the
distributionally ambiguous reaction set $\Psi(x,\tilde{\mathcal{D}})$, which is
generally non-convex for given $x$. We therefore work from the multiplier form of
Proposition~\ref{prop:risk-response}, whose feasible set $H$ is fixed and convex,
and dualize the two distributionally robust subproblems $\bar{V}^{L}$ and
$\bar{V}^{F}$ into a single min--sup whose inner feasible set is independent of $x$
and whose objective is convex in $x$.

Before stating the general result, we expose the structure that drives
it. By Proposition~\ref{prop:DRO-decomposition}, $V^{*}(x) =
\sup_{\alpha \in H}[\bar V^{L}(x,\alpha) + \bar V^{F}(x,\alpha)]$, where
the leader block is the worst-case risk $\bar V^{L}(x,\alpha) =
\sup_{P \in \hat{\mathcal{D}}} \mathcal{R}^{P}[Q(x,\alpha,\xi)]$ and the
follower block is the worst-case expectation $\bar V^{F}(x,\alpha)$.
Each block reduces to a single finite-dimensional convex minimization due to Assumption~\ref{asm:ambiguity}. The next theorem
dualizes the two minimizations and combines them with the outer $\sup_\alpha$.

\begin{theorem}[Generic minimax reformulation]\label{thm:structural-minsup}
Under the assumptions in Section~\ref{sec:problem}, the leader's
decision problem admits the reformulation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:abstract-minsup}
    \min_{x \,\in\, \mathcal{X}}\; V^{*}(x)
    \;=\;
    \min_{x \,\in\, \mathcal{X}}\;
    \sup_{(\alpha,\, \zeta^{L},\, \zeta^{F}) \,\in\, \Omega}\;
    F(x, \alpha, \zeta^{L}, \zeta^{F}),
\end{equation}
where $\zeta^{L}, \zeta^{F}$ are the dual variables of the leader and
follower blocks $\bar V^{L}, \bar V^{F}$ in~\eqref{eq:skeleton-blocks}
and $\Omega$ collects their dual-feasibility constraints. The objective
$F$ and the feasible set $\Omega$ satisfy:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep, label=(\roman*)]
\item ($x$-independence of $\Omega$) $\Omega$ is closed and independent
of $x$, since $x$ enters $\bar V^{L}, \bar V^{F}$ only through the
right-hand side $b_x(\xi)$ and hence appears in the duals only as an
objective coefficient.
\item (concavity for fixed $\alpha$) for every fixed $(x, \alpha)$, $F$
is upper semicontinuous and concave in $(\zeta^{L}, \zeta^{F})$ over
the convex section $\{(\zeta^{L}, \zeta^{F}) : (\alpha, \zeta^{L},
\zeta^{F}) \in \Omega\}$.
\item (convexity in $x$) for every fixed $(\alpha, \zeta^{L},
\zeta^{F})$, $F$ is convex in $x$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Please see the detailed proof in Appendix~\ref{ch:proof_chap3}.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:structural-minsup} holds for any pair $(\hat{\mathcal{D}}, \tilde{\mathcal{D}})$ satisfying Assumption~\ref{asm:ambiguity}, but the explicit form of $\Omega$ and $F$ depends on the ambiguity-side strong-duality reformulation. The inner maximization need not be concave in $(\alpha,\zeta)$. The convexity structure with respect to $x$, is what motivates the Benders
decomposition of Section~\ref{sec:benders}.

The reformulation~\eqref{eq:abstract-minsup} is the starting point for a
cutting-plane method in $x$. For fixed $\alpha$ the inner problem is a concave
maximization, but optimizing jointly over $\alpha$ renders the feasible region
nonconvex, and this is the principal source of computational difficulty.
Theorem~\ref{thm:structural-minsup} shows the inner value is convex in $x$, so a
cutting-plane scheme is viable provided a subgradient in $x$ can be obtained at
moderate cost. The cost of doing so is governed by the choice of ambiguity set.

We therefore instantiate the ambiguity sets as total-variation balls on a finite
support, the case for which the inner problem takes its most tractable form. With
$\Xi$ finite, each measure is identified with a vector $p \in \Delta^{S}$, and we
take
\[
    \hat{\mathcal{D}} = \{p \in \Delta^{S} : d_{\mathrm{TV}}(p, \hat{q}) \leq \hat{\varepsilon}\},
    \qquad
    \tilde{\mathcal{D}} = \{\tilde{p} \in \Delta^{S} : d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\tilde{p}, \tilde{q}) \leq \tilde{\varepsilon}\},
\]
with $d_{\mathrm{TV}}(p, q) = \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{s}|p_{s} - q_{s}|$. Total variation
makes both sets polyhedral and each worst-case expectation
$\sup_{p}\mathbb{E}^{p}[\cdot]$ LP-representable \citep{bayraksan2015data}, so the
leader and follower blocks $\bar{V}^{L}_{\beta}, \bar{V}^{F}$ reduce to finite LPs.
Dualizing these and linearizing the binary--continuous products via McCormick
envelopes instantiates Theorem~\ref{thm:structural-minsup} as a continuous bilinear
program
\begin{equation}\label{eq:TV-minsup}
    \min_{x \in \mathcal{X}}\;
    \sup_{(\alpha,\, \zeta^{L},\, \zeta^{F}) \,\in\, \Omega}\;
    F(x, \alpha, \zeta^{L}, \zeta^{F}),
\end{equation}
where the only nonlinearity in $\Omega$ is the bilinear coupling $\alpha_{k} a_{s}$,
$\alpha_{k} d_{s}^{\dagger}$ between the recovery response $\alpha$ and the
worst-case scenario weights. Continuous bilinear programs of this form are solved to
global optimality by current off-the-shelf solvers and admit reformulation as
mixed-integer convex programs, which is what makes this instantiation attractive.
The explicit LPs, the set $\Omega$, and the identification of each dual variable are
deferred to Appendix~\ref{app:algorithmic}.

\section{Complexity Results}
\label{sec:complexity}


The reformulations of Section~\ref{sec:reformulation} reduce~\eqref{eq:DR}
to a single-level program but do not bound its intrinsic difficulty. We
show that the problem sits strictly above standard two-stage
distributionally robust programs in the polynomial hierarchy. The
\emph{decision version} of~\eqref{eq:DR} asks, given a threshold
$\Pi \in \mathbb{Q}$, whether there exists $x \in \mathcal{X}$ with
$V^{*}_\beta(x) \leq \Pi$.

\begin{theorem}[$\Sigma_2^p$-hardness]\label{thm:sigma2-hard}
The decision version of~\eqref{eq:DR} is $\Sigma_2^p$-hard already when the support $\Xi$ is finite and the leader faces no ambiguity over the true
data distribution so that the entire ambiguity is over the follower's belief.
That is, hardness holds even when the data-side set
$\hat{\mathcal{D}} = \{\hat{q}\}$ is the singleton.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    Please see the detailed proof in Appendix~\ref{app:NP-hard}.
\end{proof}


The hardness originates in the interaction between the leader's risk
aversion and the follower-side belief ambiguity, not in the two-stage
stochastic structure alone. The corner adversary forces a binary response
$h^\bullet$, and the leader's $\mathrm{CVaR}$ separates $g(x,h^\bullet) = 0$
from $g(x,h^\bullet) = 1$ at the level of the $(n+1)$-th order statistic. A
risk-neutral leader averages all scenarios and cannot resolve this single
coordinate, which is why the reduction collapses under
$\mathcal{R}^{\hat{q}} = \mathbb{E}$, consistent with
Proposition~\ref{prop:saturation}.

Two consequences follow. The first confirms that the hardness is not an
artifact of the polyhedral $\tilde{\mathcal{D}}$ but persists on the
total-variation instances solved by the algorithm of
Section~\ref{sec:benders}.

\begin{corollary}[Hardness on total-variation instances]\label{cor:tv-hard}
Theorem~\ref{thm:sigma2-hard} persists when $\tilde{\mathcal{D}}$ is replaced
by the total-variation ball
$\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathrm{TV}}(\tilde{\varepsilon}) =
\{\tilde{p} \in \Delta^S : d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\tilde{p}, \hat{q}) \leq
\tilde{\varepsilon}\}$ for any $\tilde{\varepsilon} \geq n/(2n+1)$. In
particular, the instance class on which the algorithm of
Section~\ref{sec:benders} operates is $\Sigma_2^p$-hard.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Each vertex $\tilde{p}^*$ of the polyhedron~\eqref{eq:Dtilde} satisfies
$d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\tilde{p}^*, \hat{q}) = \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_s |\tilde{p}^*_s -
\hat{q}_s| = n/(2n+1)$, so $\tilde{\mathcal{D}} \subseteq
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathrm{TV}}(n/(2n+1))$. The no-instance adversary of
Lemma~\ref{lem:no} is such a vertex and hence remains feasible in the larger
ball, giving $V^{*}_\beta(x) > \Pi$. Since Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform-bound}
bounds the integrand over all of $[0,1]^n$ irrespective of which responses
$\tilde{p}$ induces, the yes-instance bound holds a fortiori over the larger
ball, giving $V^{*}_\beta(x^*) < \Pi$. The remaining data are unchanged.
\end{proof}

The second isolates the difficulty of evaluating the leader's objective at a
fixed decision, which is the per-iteration subproblem of the algorithm.

\begin{corollary}[Hardness of the inner evaluation]\label{cor:eval-hard}
Under the specialization of Theorem~\ref{thm:sigma2-hard}, evaluating
$V^{*}_\beta(\bar{x})$ at a fixed $\bar{x} \in \mathcal{X}$ is NP-hard.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Fix $\bar{x}$ and consider the single-argument formula
$f(\bar{x}, \cdot) : \{0,1\}^n \to \{0,1\}$. Restricting the no-instance
analysis to this $\bar{x}$ yields a polynomial-time reduction from the
complement of \textsc{Sat}: by Lemma~\ref{lem:corner} the corner adversaries
of $\tilde{\mathcal{D}}$ realize every $h^\bullet \in \{0,1\}^n$ as the
follower's unique response, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:binary-cvar} the resulting
leader value exceeds $\Pi$ exactly when some $h^\bullet$ has
$g(\bar{x}, h^\bullet) = 1$, i.e.\ when $f(\bar{x}, \cdot)$ is unsatisfiable.
Deciding $V^{*}_\beta(\bar{x}) > \Pi$ thus decides the complement of
\textsc{Sat}, which is NP-hard. The reduction uses only the follower-side
ambiguity $\tilde{\mathcal{D}}$ and is vacuous when $\tilde{\mathcal{D}}$ is a
singleton.
\end{proof}

\section{Solution Algorithm: A Benders Approach}
\label{sec:benders}

We develop a exact Benders decomposition for $\min_x V^*_\beta(x)$ which is possible due to the minimax formulation~\eqref{eq:abstract-minsup}. The outer master
problem (OMP) maintains a piecewise-linear lower envelope of
$V^*_\beta(\cdot)$ in $x$ and selects a candidate
$\bar{x} \in \mathcal{X}$; the inner subproblem (ISP) evaluates
$V^*_\beta(\bar{x})$ and returns an affine cut in $x$.

The Benders scheme below applies to any ambiguity pair satisfying
Assumption~\ref{asm:ambiguity}, since cut validity and finite termination
use only that $V^*_\beta$ is a pointwise supremum of affine minorants in
$x$. What the geometry governs is whether the inner subproblem~\eqref{eq:ISP}
has an explicit solvable form. We therefore specialize to finite support
with total-variation ambiguity, under which
Corollary~\ref{coro:algorithmic-TV} renders the ISP a continuous bilinear
program over the set $\Omega$ which has bilinear constraints of $\alpha$ and $(\zeta^{L},\zeta^{F})$. We exploit this structure through the
cut-generation modes of Section~\ref{subsec:cut-validity} and the valid
inequalities of Section~\ref{subsec:dual-VI}.

\subsection{Cut generation}
\label{subsec:cut-validity}

The OMP at iteration $K$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:OMP}
    (\mathrm{OMP}_K) \qquad \min_{x \in \mathcal{X},\, t_0 \in \mathbb{R}}\; t_0
    \quad \text{s.t.}\quad t_0 \,\geq\, \ell^{(i)}(x),\; i = 1, \ldots, K-1,
\end{equation}
with $\ell^{(i)} : \mathcal{X} \to \mathbb{R}$ affine cuts specified below.
Let $(\bar{x}^{(K)}, \bar{t}_0^{(K)})$ denote an optimal solution
of~\eqref{eq:OMP}; the value $\bar{t}_0^{(K)}$ is a valid lower bound on
$\min_x V^*_\beta(x)$. The ISP at $\bar{x}^{(K)}$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ISP}
    V^*_\beta(\bar{x}^{(K)}) \;=\; \sup_{(\alpha, \zeta) \in \Omega}\;
    F(\bar{x}^{(K)}, \alpha, \zeta),
\end{equation}
a continuous bilinear maximization in $(\alpha, \zeta)$ whose
complexity is characterized in Section~\ref{sec:complexity}.

We first describe the cut coefficients and intercepts of the Benders cuts.
Let $(\alpha^*, \zeta^*) \in \Omega$ be any feasible point
of~\eqref{eq:ISP}, where $\zeta^* = (\zeta^{L*}, \zeta^{F*})$ collects
the LP duals introduced in Corollary~\ref{coro:algorithmic-TV}. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cut}
    \ell^{(K)}(x) \;:=\; F(x, \alpha^*, \zeta^*)
    \;=\; \mathfrak{a}(\alpha^*, \zeta^*) \;+\;
    \textstyle\sum_{k} \mathfrak{b}_k(\alpha^*, \zeta^*)\, x_k,
\end{equation}
where, reading off the coefficients of $x_k$ and the constant terms
of~\eqref{eq:dual-obj},
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:cut-coeffs}
\begin{align}
    \mathfrak{a}(\alpha^*, \zeta^*) \;&=\;
        \textstyle\sum_{s} d_{0}^{\!\top}\, \hat{y}^{s*}
        \;-\; \pi^{U}\!\!\sum_{k,s}\!\big(\hat{\rho}_{k}^{s,3*} + \tilde{\rho}_{k}^{s,3*}\big)
        \;-\; \lambda^{U}\!\sum_{k} \rho_{k}^{0,3*}, \label{eq:cut-int}\\
    \mathfrak{b}_k(\alpha^*, \zeta^*) \;&=\;
        -\pi^{U}\!\sum_{s}\!\big[(\hat{\rho}_{k}^{s,1*} + \tilde{\rho}_{k}^{s,1*})
        - (\hat{\rho}_{k}^{s,3*} + \tilde{\rho}_{k}^{s,3*})\big]
        \;-\; \lambda^{U}\!\big(\rho_{k}^{0,1*} - \rho_{k}^{0,3*}\big). \label{eq:cut-slope}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
By Corollary~\ref{coro:algorithmic-TV}, $\Omega$ is independent of $x$ and
$F$ is affine in $x$ for fixed $(\alpha^*, \zeta^*) \in \Omega$.
The function $V^*_\beta$ is therefore a pointwise supremum of affine
functions of $x$, hence convex on $\mathrm{conv}(\mathcal{X})$, and
$\ell^{(K)}$ is one of its affine minorants:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cut-valid}
    \ell^{(K)}(x) \;=\; F(x, \alpha^*, \zeta^*)
    \;\leq\; \sup_{(\alpha, \zeta) \in \Omega} F(x, \alpha, \zeta)
    \;=\; V^*_\beta(x) \qquad \forall x \in \mathcal{X}.
\end{equation}
Equality in~\eqref{eq:cut-valid} holds at $\bar{x}^{(K)}$ whenever
$(\alpha^*, \zeta^*)$ attains the supremum in~\eqref{eq:ISP} at
$\bar{x}^{(K)}$. Two observations follow.

First, the cut $\ell^{(K)}$ is an affine function of $x$ with fixed
coefficients $(\mathfrak{a}, \mathfrak{b})$. The quantity
$F(x, \alpha^*, \zeta^*)$ is the value of the Lagrangian dual at the
realized dual iterate $(\alpha^*, \zeta^*)$, not the value of the ISP
itself; the dependence of $\ell^{(K)}$ on the master decision $x$
therefore propagates only through the affine objective coefficients,
and not through the feasible set $\Omega$.

Second, the validity~\eqref{eq:cut-valid} requires no optimality
condition on $(\alpha^*, \zeta^*)$ beyond feasibility in $\Omega$.
Cut extraction is thus decoupled from global optimization of the
bilinear subproblem, which admits three distinct cut-generation modes,
each trading subproblem solve cost for cut tightness at
$\bar{x}^{(K)}$.

The ISP~\eqref{eq:ISP} is a continuous bilinear program, which is
NP-hard in general \citep{chen2009settling}. The subproblem solve
therefore dominates the per-iteration cost, and the choice of solver
trades cut tightness at $\bar{x}^{(K)}$ against subproblem time.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(M\arabic*),leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{Exact cut.} If~\eqref{eq:ISP} is solved to certified global
optimality by a spatial branch-and-bound solver
\citep{al1983jointly}, the returned
$(\alpha^*, \zeta^*)$ attains the supremum at $\bar{x}^{(K)}$. The
resulting cut $\ell^{(K)}$ is tight at $\bar{x}^{(K)}$ by~\eqref{eq:cut-valid},
and the upper bound is updated as
$\overline{V} \leftarrow \min\{\overline{V}, F(\bar{x}^{(K)}, \alpha^*, \zeta^*)\}$.

\item \emph{Local-optimal cut.} If~\eqref{eq:ISP} is solved by a local
nonlinear solver, returning a feasible $(\hat{\alpha}, \hat{\zeta})
\in \Omega$ that is locally optimal but not certified global, the cut
$\ell^{(K)}(x) = F(x, \hat{\alpha}, \hat{\zeta})$ remains valid
by~\eqref{eq:cut-valid}. The cut may be loose at $\bar{x}^{(K)}$ and
the upper bound $\overline{V}$ is not updated; this is the inexact-cut
variant analyzed in the Benders literature
\citep{rahmaniani2017benders}.

\item \emph{Restricted ISP cut.} Given any $\bar{\alpha} \in H$
obtained from a prior bilinear solve, fixing $\alpha = \bar{\alpha}$
in~\eqref{eq:ISP} reduces the residual subproblem to an LP in $\zeta$.
The bilinear couplings $\alpha_k a_s$ and $\alpha_k d_s^\dagger$
become linear in $(a_s, d_s^\dagger) \in \zeta$ once $\bar{\alpha}$
is fixed. Let $\bar{\zeta}_{\bar{\alpha}}$ denote an optimal solution
of this LP at $\bar{x}^{(K)}$. The cut
$\ell^{(K)}(x) = F(x, \bar{\alpha}, \bar{\zeta}_{\bar{\alpha}})$ is
valid: for every $\bar{\alpha} \in H$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:restricted-valid}
    V^*_\beta(\bar{x}^{(K)})
    \;=\; \sup_{(\alpha, \zeta) \in \Omega} F(\bar{x}^{(K)}, \alpha, \zeta)
    \;\geq\; \sup_{\zeta :\, (\bar{\alpha}, \zeta) \in \Omega} F(\bar{x}^{(K)}, \bar{\alpha}, \zeta)
    \;=\; F(\bar{x}^{(K)}, \bar{\alpha}, \bar{\zeta}_{\bar{\alpha}}).
\end{equation}
The cut is tight at $\bar{x}^{(K)}$ only if $\bar{\alpha}$ coincides
with the bilinear-optimal $\alpha$ at $\bar{x}^{(K)}$, but remains
globally valid uniformly in $x \in \mathcal{X}$. The LP solve is
typically two to three orders of magnitude faster than a global
bilinear solve.
\end{enumerate}

The asymmetry between subproblem cost and cut validity, namely that
local and restricted-ISP cuts are substantially cheaper than exact
cuts while remaining globally valid, motivates the two-loop schedule
of Section~\ref{subsec:algorithm}, in which exact cuts are generated
sparingly and restricted-ISP cuts populate a fast inner loop.

\subsection{Valid inequalities}
\label{subsec:dual-VI}

The cuts of Section~\ref{subsec:cut-validity} are LP-derived affine
functions of $x$ that, taken as a family, carry no a-priori information
about the lower-level structure. The OMP at early iterations may therefore
suffer from a poor lower bound, a known limitation of standard Benders
decomposition when the subproblem encodes structure absent from the
master \citep{crainic2021partial}. The remedy is to inject a relaxation of
the subproblem structure directly into the master, a strategy known as
partial Benders decomposition or scenario creation \citep{crainic2021partial}.
We construct such a relaxation by combining the probabilistic lower bound of
Theorem~\ref{thm:game-theoretic} with the LP dual of the lower-level value
function, and present it as the main result of this section.

We first establish a structural lower bound on $V_{\beta}^{*}(x)$. By
Theorem~\ref{thm:game-theoretic},
$V^*_\beta(x) = \max_{\alpha \in \Psi(x, \tilde{\mathcal{D}})}
\sup_{P \in \hat{\mathcal{D}}} \mathcal{R}^{P}[Q(x, \alpha, \xi)]$.
Specializing $P$ to the leader-side center $\hat{q} \in \hat{\mathcal{D}}$
and using $\hat{q} \in \tilde{\mathcal{D}}$, which holds by the construction
of the follower ambiguity set, any
$\bar{h} \in \Psi(x, \tilde{q}) \subseteq \Psi(x, \tilde{\mathcal{D}})$
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dual-VI-bound}
V^*_\beta(x) \;\geq\; \mathcal{R}^{\hat{q}}\!\big[Q(x, \bar{h}, \xi)\big]
\;\geq\; \mathbb{E}^{\hat{q}}\!\big[Q(x, \bar{h}, \xi)\big]
\;=\; \textstyle\sum_{s} \hat{q}_{s}\, Q(x, \bar{h}, \xi^{s}),
\end{equation}
where the second inequality follows from $\hat{q} \in \mathcal{Q}(\hat{q})$,
the risk envelope of $\mathcal{R}^{\hat{q}}$ in~\eqref{eq:envelope}, and the
last equality uses the finite support $\Xi = \{\xi^{s}\}_{s=1}^{|\Xi|}$.

It remains to express each $Q(x, \bar{h}, \xi^{s})$ in a form linear in $x$.
By Assumption~\ref{asm:RCR}, LP duality gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dual-VI-LPdual}
Q(x, \bar{h}, \xi^{s}) \;=\;
\min_{\pi^{s} \geq 0,\; A^{\!\top}\pi^{s} \geq d_{0}}\;
\big(b_x(\xi^{s}) + B\bar{h}\big)^{\!\top}\pi^{s},
\end{equation}
where $\pi^{s} \in \mathbb{R}^{m_{2}}$ is the dual of the lower-level
constraints at scenario $s$. By Assumption~\ref{asm:interdiction},
$b_x(\xi^{s}) = u_{0} + U_{0}\xi^{s} + \sum_{i=1}^{d_x} x_{i}\, U_{i}\xi^{s}$,
so the dual objective separates into an $x$-independent part and the
linear couplings
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dual-VI-bilin}
\big(b_x(\xi^{s}) + B\bar{h}\big)^{\!\top}\pi^{s}
\;=\; \big(u_{0} + U_{0}\xi^{s} + B\bar{h}\big)^{\!\top}\pi^{s}
\;+\; \sum_{i=1}^{d_x} x_{i}\, g_{i}^{s}(\pi^{s}),
\qquad g_{i}^{s}(\pi^{s}) := (U_{i}\xi^{s})^{\!\top}\pi^{s}.
\end{equation}
Each product $x_{i}\, g_{i}^{s}$ is bilinear in the binary $x_{i}$ and the
bounded dual $\pi^{s}$. Since $\pi^{s} \leq \pi^{U}$ by
Assumption~\ref{asm:RCR}, the term $g_{i}^{s}(\pi^{s})$ lies in a computable
interval $[\underline{g}_{i}^{s}, \overline{g}_{i}^{s}]$, and the exact
linearization of $x_{i} g_{i}^{s}$ by an auxiliary variable
$w_{i}^{s} = x_{i}\, g_{i}^{s}$ is the set of inequalities
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dual-VI-mccormick}
\underline{g}_{i}^{s}\, x_{i} \,\leq\, w_{i}^{s} \,\leq\, \overline{g}_{i}^{s}\, x_{i},
\qquad
g_{i}^{s} - \overline{g}_{i}^{s}(1 - x_{i}) \,\leq\, w_{i}^{s} \,\leq\,
g_{i}^{s} - \underline{g}_{i}^{s}(1 - x_{i}),
\end{equation}
which is tight at every $x_{i} \in \{0,1\}$.

\begin{proposition}[Dual-VI]\label{prop:dual-VI}
Under Assumptions~\ref{asm:coherent}--\ref{asm:explicit-scenario} and finite
support $|\Xi| =:S< +\infty$, for any $\bar{h} \in \Psi(x, \tilde{q})$ the
constraints
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:dual-VI}
\begin{align}
    t_{0} &\;\geq\; \textstyle\sum_{s=1}^{S} \hat{q}_{s}\Big[
        \big(u_{0} + U_{0}\xi^{s} + B\bar{h}\big)^{\!\top}\pi^{s}
        + \sum_{i=1}^{d_{x}} w_{i}^{s}\Big], \label{eq:dual-VI-obj}\\
    & A^{\!\top}\pi^{s} \,\geq\, d_{0},\quad
        0 \,\leq\, \pi^{s} \,\leq\, \pi^{U} \quad \forall s=1,\cdots,S, \label{eq:dual-VI-LP}\\
    & g_{i}^{s} = (U_{i}\xi^{s})^{\!\top}\pi^{s},\quad
        w_{i}^{s} \text{ satisfies~\eqref{eq:dual-VI-mccormick}}
        \quad \forall i=1,\cdots,d_{x},\, \forall s=1,\cdots,S, \label{eq:dual-VI-MC}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
are valid for the OMP.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Overall algorithmic procedure}
\label{subsec:algorithm}

The algorithm combines an outer loop of OMP--ISP alternations, in which
the ISP is the full bilinear problem~\eqref{eq:ISP}, with an inner loop
of $\alpha$-restricted LP solves at the dual iterate returned by the
outer loop. Both loops append cuts to the same OMP.

The outer loop alternates between the OMP and the bilinear ISP. At
each outer iteration, the OMP is re-solved to produce a candidate
$\bar{x}^{(K)}$ and a lower bound $\bar{t}_0^{(K)}$, and the bilinear
ISP at $\bar{x}^{(K)}$ returns a feasible dual iterate
$(\bar{\alpha}, \bar{\zeta}) \in \Omega$ from which the affine
cut~\eqref{eq:cut} is constructed. The cut
validity~\eqref{eq:cut-valid} holds at any feasible
$(\bar{\alpha}, \bar{\zeta}) \in \Omega$, which permits matching the
ISP-solver effort to the iteration schedule. A subset of the outer
iterations, taken to be every $m$-th iteration with $m \in \{4, 5\}$
in our experiments, invokes a global spatial branch-and-bound solver
on~\eqref{eq:ISP}; this returns a globally optimal
$(\bar{\alpha}, \bar{\zeta}) = (\alpha^*, \zeta^*)$, produces an exact
cut tight at $\bar{x}^{(K)}$, and updates the upper bound to
$\overline{V} \leftarrow \min\{\overline{V}, F(\bar{x}^{(K)}, \alpha^*, \zeta^*)\}$.
The remaining outer iterations invoke a local bilinear solver, which
returns a locally optimal feasible $(\bar{\alpha}, \bar{\zeta}) \in \Omega$
and yields a local-optimal cut by~\eqref{eq:cut-valid}; the upper
bound is not updated.

After the bilinear cut is appended, the algorithm optionally enters
an inner loop that reuses the $\bar{\alpha}$ returned by the outer
solve. Fixing $\alpha = \bar{\alpha}$ in the ISP reduces the residual
subproblem to an LP in $\zeta$, which is two to three orders of
magnitude faster than the bilinear solve
(Section~\ref{subsec:cut-validity}). The inner loop alternates
between the OMP and this $\alpha$-restricted LP-ISP for at most
$K_{\mathrm{inner}}$ iterations. At each inner iteration, the OMP is
re-solved to produce a new candidate $x_{\mathrm{inner}}$, the
$\alpha$-restricted LP-ISP is solved at $x_{\mathrm{inner}}$ to
obtain $\bar{\zeta}_{\bar{\alpha}}$, and the resulting restricted-ISP
cut is appended to the OMP. Each inner iteration thus produces an
additional valid cut at LP cost, and the OMP lower bound improves
monotonically. The inner loop terminates after $K_{\mathrm{inner}}$
iterations or when the OMP lower bound stalls; we use
$K_{\mathrm{inner}} = 5$ in the experiments.

The OMP is initialized with the dual-VIs of
Section~\ref{subsec:dual-VI} to ensure a non-trivial lower bound from
the first iteration. The outer loop terminates when the relative gap
$(\overline{V} - \bar{t}_0^{(K)}) / \max\{|\overline{V}|, 1\}$ falls
below a prescribed tolerance $\epsilon_{\mathrm{tol}}$, or when a
wall-time budget is exhausted. Algorithm~\ref{alg:benders} summarizes
the procedure.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Two-loop Benders procedure with exact, local-optimal, and
restricted-ISP cuts.}
\label{alg:benders}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State Initialize OMP with dual-VIs (Proposition~\ref{prop:dual-VI})
\State Set $K \gets 1$, $\overline{V} \gets +\infty$; choose
$m$, $K_{\mathrm{inner}}$, $\epsilon_{\mathrm{tol}}$
\Repeat \Comment{outer loop}
    \State Solve $\mathrm{OMP}_K$~\eqref{eq:OMP}, obtain $(\bar{x}^{(K)}, \bar{t}_0^{(K)})$
    \If{$K \bmod m = 0$} \Comment{exact cut}
        \State Global bilinear solve of~\eqref{eq:ISP}, obtain $(\alpha^*, \zeta^*)$
        \State $\overline{V} \gets \min\{\overline{V},\; F(\bar{x}^{(K)}, \alpha^*, \zeta^*)\}$
        \State $(\bar{\alpha}, \bar{\zeta}) \gets (\alpha^*, \zeta^*)$
    \Else \Comment{local-optimal cut}
        \State Local bilinear solve of~\eqref{eq:ISP}, obtain $(\hat{\alpha}, \hat{\zeta}) \in \Omega$
        \State $(\bar{\alpha}, \bar{\zeta}) \gets (\hat{\alpha}, \hat{\zeta})$
    \EndIf
    \State Construct cut $\ell^{(K)}$ from $(\bar{\alpha}, \bar{\zeta})$ via~\eqref{eq:cut-coeffs}; append to OMP
    \For{$j = 1, \ldots, K_{\mathrm{inner}}$} \Comment{$\alpha$-restricted inner loop}
        \State Solve OMP, obtain $x_{\mathrm{inner}}$
        \State Solve $\alpha$-restricted LP-ISP at $(x_{\mathrm{inner}}, \bar{\alpha})$, obtain $\bar{\zeta}_{\bar{\alpha}}$
        \State Construct cut from $(\bar{\alpha}, \bar{\zeta}_{\bar{\alpha}})$; append to OMP
        \If{OMP lower bound has stalled} \textbf{break} \EndIf
    \EndFor
    \State $K \gets K + 1$
\Until{$(\overline{V} - \bar{t}_0^{(K)}) / \max\{|\overline{V}|, 1\} \leq \epsilon_{\mathrm{tol}}$}
\State \Return $(\bar{x}^{(K)}, \overline{V})$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

We establish the finite termination guarantee for the proposed Benders
decomposition algorithm.
\begin{proposition}[Finite termination]\label{prop:finite}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:benders} terminates in finitely many iterations.
\end{proposition}
 
\begin{proof}
Please see the detailed proof in Appendix~\ref{ch:proof_chap3}
\end{proof}
 
 
In our experiments (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}), the local
bilinear solve dominates the outer-loop per-iteration cost. Each
local solve completes in seconds, the global solve is one to two
orders of magnitude slower, and the local-optimal cuts close most of
the OMP--ISP gap. The $\alpha$-restricted inner loop contributes
additional LP-cheap cuts at negligible per-iteration cost. The
periodic global solve serves primarily to validate the incumbent
upper bound and to escape regions in which the local solver returns
local optima far from the global optimum.

\subsection{Maximum-flow network interdiction game}
\label{subsec:NIG-model}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
The maximum-flow network interdiction game (NIG) instantiates the
abstract structure of Assumptions~\ref{asm:RCR}--\ref{asm:interdiction}
in a setting with a deep operations-research history. Introduced by
\cite{Wood1993} to model adversarial disruption of supply networks,
the NIG has served as the canonical benchmark for stochastic and
robust variants of bilevel interdiction
\citep{Cormican1998, Israeli2002, Lei2018, Sadana2022}, with
applications spanning military targeting, infrastructure defense, and
the disruption of illicit logistics. The maximum-flow variant is the
most extensively studied: its lower-level LP is the textbook
$s$--$t$ max-flow problem with min-cut as the LP dual, and the
recovery-budget extension of \cite{Lei2018} introduces the 2SLP
follower we adopt below.
 
The game is played on a directed network $\mathcal{G} = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{A})$
with source $s$ and sink $t$. The arc set is partitioned into the
\emph{interdictable} arcs $\mathcal{A}_I$, on which the leader can commit
effort, and the \emph{non-interdictable} arcs incident to $s$ and $t$,
whose capacities are fixed across scenarios. The leader's feasible set is
$\mathcal{X} = \{x \in \{0,1\}^{|\mathcal{A}|} : \mathbf{1}^\top x \leq \Gamma,\, x_k = 0 \;\forall k \notin \mathcal{A}_I\}$,
where $\Gamma \in \mathbb{Z}_+$ is the interdiction budget. The
follower's recovery polyhedron is
$H = \{h \in \mathbb{R}_+^{|\mathcal{A}|} : \mathbf{1}^\top h \leq w\}$,
where $w$ is the recovery budget. The decision chronology is
$x \to h(x) \to \xi \to y(x, h, \xi)$.
 
The random vector $\xi \in \Xi$ encodes uncertain arc capacities
$u_k(\xi) \geq 0$ and interdiction-effect coefficients
$v_k(\xi) \in [0, 1]$, with $\Xi \subseteq \mathbb{R}_+^{|\mathcal{A}|} \times [0,1]^{|\mathcal{A}|}$.
Writing $N = [N_y \mid N_{ts}]$ for the node-arc incidence matrix with a
dummy $(t,s)$-arc appended \citep{Lei2018}, the lower-level LP value
function~\eqref{eq:Q-def} specializes to the maximum $s$--$t$ flow
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Q-NIG}
    Q(x, h, \xi) \;=\; \max_{\bar{y} \in \mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{A}|},\, \bar{y}_{ts} \in \mathbb{R}}\;
    \big\{\bar{y}_{ts} \,:\, N_y \bar{y} + N_{ts} \bar{y}_{ts} = 0,\;\; 0 \,\leq\, \bar{y}_k \,\leq\, u_k(\xi)\,(1 - v_k(\xi)\, x_k) + h_k \;\;\forall k\big\},
\end{equation}
with $d =  e_{ts}$ (the unit vector picking out the dummy arc) and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bx-NIG}
    b_x(\xi) \;=\; \big(u_k(\xi)\,(1 - v_k(\xi)\, x_k)\big)_{k=1}^{|\mathcal{A}|},
\end{equation}
which instantiates Assumption~\ref{asm:interdiction}.

We instantiate the proposed framework on the maximum-flow network
interdiction game (NIG) of Section~\ref{subsec:NIG-model} and assess
its performance against suitable benchmarks. The maximum-flow NIG is
adopted as the application for two reasons. First, it widely investigated through stochastic/robust bilevel optimization literatures \citep{Lei2018, Sadana2022},
which allows direct comparison with the nominal stochastic approach and
standard DR approach as baselines. Second, the recovery-budget extension of \cite{Lei2018}
adopted in Section~\ref{subsec:NIG-model} produces a follower whose
first-stage decision $h$ is parameterized by the follower's belief, so
the follower-side ambiguity set $\tilde{\mathcal{D}}$ has a substantive
effect on the leader's optimal interdiction.

We evaluate the formulation and the Benders procedure of
Section~\ref{sec:benders} along three measures.
Section~\ref{subsec:exp-compute} reports the wall-clock performance of
three algorithmic variants. Section~\ref{subsec:exp-vor} measures the
in-sample model-implied value of robustness against the
sample-average-approximation (SAA) baseline.
Section~\ref{subsec:exp-oos} reports out-of-sample CVaR performance
under in-distribution and perturbed sampling regimes. All experiments
are implemented in Julia/JuMP and run on an AMD RYZEN 12-Core 3.70 GHz PC with 16GB RAM. The local bilinear subproblem is
solved by Gurobi 13.0 with \texttt{OptimalityTarget = 1}, the local-optimal
mode for non-convex quadratically constrained problems; the global
bilinear subproblem is solved by Gurobi with \texttt{NonConvex = 2},
invoking spatial branch-and-bound. All LP subproblems are solved by
Gurobi in default mode.

\subsection{Experimental setup}
\label{subsec:exp-setup}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 
We use five undirected $s$--$t$ networks of varying size and topology,
comprising a synthetic $5 \times 5$ grid and four real-world backbone
instances (Polska, Abilene, Nobel-US, Sioux Falls). Vertex and arc
counts are reported in the leftmost columns of
Table~\ref{tab:computational}. The leader's interdiction budget is
fixed at $\Gamma = 2$ in all experiments, with $\mathcal{X}$ becomes the constraint
$\sum_{k \in \mathcal{A}_I} x_k \leq \Gamma$ enforced over the
interdictable arc set $\mathcal{A}_I$. For the synthetic Grid
$5 \times 5$, $\mathcal{A}_I$ excludes source-outgoing and
sink-incoming arcs. For the four backbone networks, all arcs are
interdictable, but we impose the cardinality constraints
$\sum_{k \in \delta^{+}(s)} x_k \leq |\delta^{+}(s)| - 1$ and
$\sum_{k \in \delta^{-}(t)} x_k \leq |\delta^{-}(t)| - 1$, which
guarantee that at least one source-outgoing and one sink-incoming arc
remain operational under any interdiction.


The empirical scenario set has cardinality $S = 10$. For each scenario
$s \in [S]$, the baseline arc capacity
$c^{s} \in \mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{A}|}_{+}$ and the
interdiction-success vector $v^{s} \in [0, 1]^{|\mathcal{A}|}$ are
drawn independently from network-specific intervals, following the
factor-model generation of \citet{Sadana2022} as detailed in
Appendix~\ref{app:scenario-gen}. The empirical probability vector on
the $S$ scenarios is uniform, $\hat{q}_{s} = 1 / S$, and we identify
the follower-side belief center with the leader's, $\tilde{q} = \hat{q}$.
The choice $\tilde{q} = \hat{q}$ is the symmetric-ignorance baseline,
in which the leader has no prior reason to displace the follower's
belief center away from her own. To probe sensitivity to
misspecification of this probability vector, we draw $500$ test
probability vectors $q^{(n)}$ from a symmetric Dirichlet
$\mathrm{Dir}(\gamma \cdot \tilde{q}_{\zeta_{\mathrm{dir}}})$, in
which $\tilde{q}_{\zeta_{\mathrm{dir}}}$ is obtained by
multiplicatively perturbing $\hat{q}$ in proportion to a
\emph{Dirichlet-shift level} $\zeta_{\mathrm{dir}} \geq 0$
(Appendix~\ref{ch:proof_chap3}). The concentration
$\gamma \in \{0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 1.0\}$ controls the dispersion of the
draws around the center, with smaller $\gamma$ producing more
dispersed draws. The unperturbed regime $\zeta_{\mathrm{dir}} = 0$
recovers $\tilde{q}_{0} = \hat{q}$, and we evaluate four positive
levels $\zeta_{\mathrm{dir}} \in \{0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 5.0\}$, giving five
Dirichlet-shift regimes in total. The subscript $\mathrm{dir}$
distinguishes the realized shift of the data-generating process from
the modeler's ambiguity radius $\varepsilon$ of the
$\phi$-divergence ambiguity set, which is the assumed shift inside the
modeler's ball. A separate $500$-draw validation set is generated at
$\zeta_{\mathrm{dir}} = 0$ to select the modeler's ambiguity radius,
with the test draws held out for the out-of-sample evaluation of
Section~\ref{subsec:exp-oos}.
 
We evaluate the CVaR risk level $\beta \in \{0.0, 0.05, 0.4, 0.7\}$
and the ambiguity radius
$\hat{\varepsilon} = \tilde{\varepsilon} =: \varepsilon \in \{0.1, 0.2, 0.5\}$.
The out-of-sample comparison fixes $\beta = 0.4$, an intermediate risk
level for which Proposition~\ref{prop:saturation} does not predict
structural saturation and the follower-side ambiguity is nontrivial.
We select $\varepsilon^{*}(\mathrm{net})$ as the smallest
$\varepsilon \in \{0.1, 0.2, 0.5\}$ that minimizes the Full DR's
validation $q_{95}$ on the Phase~A draws, restricted to those
$\varepsilon$ at which the Full DR is strictly distinct from the
Nominal SP. The latter restriction is binding only on Nobel-US,
where Full DR coincides with the Nominal at $\varepsilon = 0.1$
(an instance of the validation-set analogue of the saturation
phenomenon of Proposition~\ref{prop:saturation}), so the
selection on Nobel-US skips to $\varepsilon^{*} = 0.2$. The
remaining four networks satisfy the rule under the smaller-$\varepsilon$
tiebreak alone. This procedure yields
$\varepsilon^{*} = 0.1$ on Grid $5 \times 5$, Polska, and Sioux Falls,
and $\varepsilon^{*} = 0.2$ on Abilene and Nobel-US, both held fixed
for the test phase. We deliberately do not tune $\varepsilon$ per
$(\mathrm{net}, \gamma)$ pair, since doing so would let the selection
rule exploit test-distribution information. Three solutions are
compared on the held-out test set. The \emph{Nominal SP} uses
$\hat{\varepsilon} = \tilde{\varepsilon} = 0$, the \emph{Partial DR}
uses $\hat{\varepsilon} = \varepsilon^{*}$ and $\tilde{\varepsilon} = 0$
(leader-side ambiguity alone), and the \emph{Full DR} uses
$\hat{\varepsilon} = \tilde{\varepsilon} = \varepsilon^{*}$. The
three-way contrast isolates the marginal contribution of follower-side
ambiguity hedging, which is the novel element of our formulation
relative to \citet{Sadana2022, Lei2018}.

\subsection{Computational performance}
\label{subsec:exp-compute}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

We benchmark three variants of the Benders procedure of
Section~\ref{subsec:algorithm}. All three share the outer-loop solver
schedule of Algorithm~\ref{alg:benders}, in which the bilinear ISP
\eqref{eq:ISP} is solved by the global spatial branch-and-bound solver
every $m = 5$-th outer iteration and by the local bilinear solver
otherwise. The variants differ in the two enhancements layered on top
of this base. \textbf{Plain BD} runs the outer loop alone, with no
$\alpha$-restricted inner loop and no min-cut VIs in the OMP.
\textbf{Enhanced BD} adds the $\alpha$-restricted inner loop with
$K_{\mathrm{inner}} = 5$ to each outer iteration, accumulating up to
$K_{\mathrm{inner}}$ additional $\alpha$-restricted cuts per outer
iteration at LP cost. \textbf{Enhanced BD + VIs} additionally
pre-generates the OMP with the min-cut valid inequalities of
Proposition~\ref{prop:dual-VI}. The two enhancements are introduced
cumulatively so that the Plain to Enhanced comparison isolates the
effect of the $\alpha$-restricted inner loop, and the Enhanced to
Enhanced + VIs comparison isolates the effect of the min-cut VIs. All
variants share a $0.5\%$ relative-gap tolerance, a $7{,}200$-second
wall-time limit, and a $1{,}000$-iteration cap.
Table~\ref{tab:computational} reports wall-clock time and
outer-iteration count for each (variant, network) pair, with each
cell aggregated as the geometric mean over the twelve
$(\beta, \varepsilon)$ configurations of
Section~\ref{subsec:exp-setup}.

The $\alpha$-restricted inner loop (Plain BD vs. Enhanced BD) reduces
wall-clock time by a factor of $2.4\times$ on Nobel-US to $6.0\times$
on Polska, with outer-iteration counts compressed by factors of $6$ to
$11$. The mechanism is visible in the cut accounting. Each outer
iteration in Plain BD produces a single bilinear cut, while in
Enhanced BD it produces a bilinear cut plus up to $K_{\mathrm{inner}}$
$\alpha$-restricted cuts that share the same $\alpha^{\dagger}$ but
differ in the OMP iterate at which the LP-ISP is solved. The
accumulated cuts collectively tighten the OMP much faster than a
single bilinear cut per iteration, and the additional cuts are
essentially free because each LP solve is two to three orders of
magnitude cheaper than a bilinear solve. The compression is largest
on Sioux Falls, the densest instance. Plain BD requires $921$ outer
iterations on average, against the $1{,}000$-iteration cap, while
Enhanced BD converges in $82$. Sioux Falls has $|\mathcal{A}| = 76$
and consequently the largest number of bilinear pairs
$\alpha_{k} a_{s}, \alpha_{k} d_{s}^{\dagger}$ in the ISP, so the
savings of the inner loop scale favorably with the bilinearity
dimension.
 
Pre-populating the OMP with the prposed VIs (Enhanced BD vs.
Enhanced BD + VIs) further reduces wall time on all five networks, by
$1.4\times$ on Grid $5 \times 5$, $2.9\times$ on Abilene and Sioux
Falls, $3.4\times$ on Nobel-US, and $4.0\times$ on Polska.
Outer-iteration counts contract correspondingly. Polska decreases
from $54$ to $26$, and Sioux Falls from $82$ to $45$. The benefit is
largest on the four backbone networks ($2.9\times$ to $4.0\times$) and
smallest on the synthetic Grid $5 \times 5$ ($1.4\times$). The pattern
is consistent with the partial-Benders interpretation of
Section~\ref{sec:benders}, in which the min-cut VIs inject
network-structural information into the OMP that is most impactful on
backbones with non-trivial topology, while on the small structured
grid the OMP's combinatorial landscape is already comparatively easy
and the auxiliary McCormick variables added by the VIs partially
offset the iteration-count savings.

\label{subsec:exp-oos}

For each network we fix the per-network ambiguity radius
$\varepsilon^{*}(\mathrm{net})$ selected on the validation set
(Section~\ref{subsec:exp-setup}) and compute three interdiction
solutions at $\beta = 0.4$, namely the Nominal SP, the Partial DR, and
the Full DR (definitions in Section~\ref{subsec:exp-setup}). The
three solutions are evaluated on the held-out test draws
$q^{(n)} \sim \mathrm{Dir}(\gamma \cdot \tilde{q}_{\zeta_{\mathrm{dir}}})$
for $\gamma \in \{0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 1.0\}$ and

$\mathrm{CVaR}_{\beta}^{q^{(n)}}\!\big[Q(x, h^{*}(\tilde{q}^{(n)}), \xi)\big]$,

under the realized $\tilde{q}^{(n)}$.

Nobel-US is the one instance on which the Full DR's $q_{95}$ exceeds

$q_{95} - q_{05}$ spread is still smaller than the Nominal's

worst case. The validation rule, agnostic to the test distribution by
construction, cannot single this instance out, but the in-sample
diagnostic of Section~\ref{subsec:exp-vor} does. At the operative
$\beta = 0.4$, the maximum in-sample $\mathrm{VoR}$ over
$\varepsilon \in \{0.1, 0.2, 0.5\}$ is $4.2\%$ for Nobel-US

networks. Across the full $(\beta, \varepsilon)$ grid, the
corresponding ceilings are $9.0\%$ versus $14.4\%$--$29.4\%$. The
model's own ambiguity set therefore predicts limited DR potential on
Nobel-US at any $\varepsilon$, and the OOS realization is consistent
with that prediction. The small in-sample improvement does not
survive the conservatism-generalization gap once $\varepsilon^{*} = 0.2$
is held fixed in the test phase.
 
The three-solution presentation in Table~\ref{tab:oos-summary} also
isolates the marginal contribution of follower-side ambiguity hedging,
the novel element of our formulation relative to
\citet{Sadana2022, Lei2018}. On Polska, the Partial DR's
columns coincide with the Nominal SP's, so the leader-side optimal
interdiction is structurally invariant to $\hat{\varepsilon}$ over the
relevant range. The $q_{95}$ improvement of Full DR on Polska
($18.03 \to 16.94$) is therefore a direct measurement of the value of
follower-side ambiguity alone.
On Nobel-US, it turned out that the best validation $\epsilon^{*}$ turned out to give same solution among three models. Therefore, we omit the result on Nobel-US in subsequent analysis.
On Sioux Falls the opposite pattern
holds. The Partial DR and Full DR columns coincide, and the
worst-case improvement over the Nominal SP ($20.85 \to 19.52$) is
attributable to leader-side hedging alone. These two patterns
instantiate Proposition~\ref{prop:saturation}, in which one side
saturates and the other carries the protection.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%==============================================================================

We have studied an ambiguity-averse zero-sum Stackelberg game under 
incomplete information about the follower's private belief, 
formulated as a distributionally robust pessimistic bilevel program 
with a two-stage stochastic linear program at the follower's level. 
Heterogeneous beliefs induce a form of follower decision uncertainty 
whose treatment is the central methodological feature of the model. 
We showed that the leader's ambiguity aversion over the follower's 
belief ambiguity set is equivalent to hedging her evaluation over 
the follower's induced decision uncertainty set, which assigns the 
two ambiguity sets structurally distinct roles in the leader's 
worst-case operator. We further established that evaluating the 
leader's worst-case objective is NP-hard and that the full decision 
problem belongs to $\Sigma_{2}^{p}$, placing it strictly above 
standard two-stage distributionally robust programs in the 
polynomial hierarchy. Under mild regularity assumptions we derived 
an exact min-max reformulation, and under the additional restriction 
to finite scenarios with total-variation ambiguity sets, the inner 
maximization specializes to a continuous bilinear program over a 
polyhedron independent of the leader's decision. Exploiting this 
independence, we developed a Benders decomposition with valid inequalities derived from the lower-level LP duality. 
Numerical experiments on a synthetic grid and four real-world 
backbone topologies confirmed the algorithmic benefit of the 
proposed enhancements and showed that the proposed model reduces 
the realized out-of-sample worst-case CVaR relative to a benchmark 
that ignores the follower's belief ambiguity, on four of the five 
tested networks.

Several directions remain for future research. The zero-sum 
assumption is used to obtain the saddle-point representation that 
yields the bilinear single-level program. Extending the model to 
non-zero-sum games requires a KKT-based treatment of the follower's 
first-stage optimality, whose interaction with the two-layer 
ambiguity structure has not been studied. The convex follower 
assumption is used in both the interchangeability argument and the 
dualization of worst-case expectations through LP duality at the 
lower level. Replacing the LP follower with a mixed-integer 
follower, as required in many interdiction applications, breaks 
both steps, since the mixed-integer value function is non-convex 
and the optimal-response correspondence loses single-valuedness in 
the parameters. Recent value-function-based methods for stochastic 
mixed-integer bilevel programs offer a plausible starting point, 
but their combination with two-layer ambiguity remains open.

\subsection{Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:dual}}
\label{app:dual}
We first establish the intermediate reformulation of $V(x,P,\tilde{P})$ as follows, borrowing value-function reformulation idea proposed by \cite{yanikoglu2018decision}:
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:primal-inner}
\begin{align}
V(x;P,\tilde{P})\;=\;\quad \max_{\substack{\tilde h \,\geq\, 0\\ \hat y(\cdot),\, \tilde y(\cdot) \,\geq\, 0}}\;\;
    & \mathcal{R}\!\big[d^\top \hat y(\xi);\, P\big] \\
\textnormal{s.t.}\quad
    & \mathbb{E}^{\tilde P}\!\big[d^\top \tilde y(\xi)\big] \;\geq\; z^*(x, \tilde P), && (\lambda) \label{eq:P-value}\\
    & A\tilde y(\xi) \;\leq\; b_x(\xi)+ B \tilde h ,\quad \tilde P\text{-a.s.}, && (\tilde\pi(\xi)) \label{eq:Pt-feas}\\
     & A\hat y(\xi) \;\leq\; b_x(\xi) + B \tilde h,\quad P\text{-a.s.}, && (\hat\pi(\xi)) \label{eq:rho-feas}\\
    & W\tilde h \;\leq\; w. && (\nu)
\end{align}
\end{subequations}

The policy $\tilde y(\cdot)$ is the recourse in the follower's own problem that defines his optimal $\tilde h$ under his belief $\tilde P$. By interchangeability, $\tilde h \in \Psi(x,\tilde P)$ is equivalent to the existence of $\tilde y(\cdot) \geq 0$ satisfying the feasibility constraint~\eqref{eq:Pt-feas} and the value-function constraint~\eqref{eq:P-value}, which together certify the optimality of $\tilde h$. However, the recourse $\hat y(\cdot)$ requires only the feasibility constraint~\eqref{eq:rho-feas} and no analogous value-function constraint. Since $\hat y(\xi)$ is realized pointwise after $\xi$ is observed and $\mathcal{R}$ is monotone (Assumption~\ref{asm:coherent}(i)), taking a pessimistic approach by maximizing $\mathcal{R}[d^\top \hat y(\xi); P]$ in the objective~\eqref{eq:primal-inner} drives each $\hat y(\xi)$ to the maximizer of $d^\top \bar y$ over $\mathcal{Y}(x,\tilde h,\xi)$, whose value is $Q(x,\tilde h,\xi)$ by~\eqref{eq:Q-def}. Any optimal $\hat y(\cdot)$ therefore attains the follower's optimal recourse value scenario by scenario, so imposing the constraint $\mathbb{E}^{\rho}[d^\top \hat y(\xi)] \geq z^*(x,\tilde h,\rho)$ would leave the optimal value unchanged. The leader's risk being taken under $\rho$ while the follower optimizes a $\tilde P$-expectation is immaterial, since the recovery of $\hat y$ is pointwise in $\xi$ and uses only monotonicity of $\mathcal{R}$.

By the dual representation~\eqref{eq:envelope} of the coherent $\mathcal{R}^{P}$,
\begin{align*}\label{eq:proof-env}
    V(x;P,\tilde P) \,=\, \sup_{\rho \,\in\, \mathcal{Q}(P)}\; \left\{\sup_{\tilde h }\;\left\{\mathbb{E}^{\rho}[Q(x,\tilde h,\xi)]:\;\tilde h \,\in\, \arg\max_{\bar h \in H} \mathbb{E}^{\tilde P}[Q(x,\bar h,\xi)]\right\}\right\}
\end{align*}
By Assumptions~\ref{asm:interdiction}--\ref{asm:RCR}, $Q(x,\bar h,\cdot)$ is the value of a feasible bounded LP with data affine in $\xi$, hence a normal integrand, and $\mathcal{L}^{2}(\rho), \mathcal{L}^{2}(\tilde P)$ are decomposable, so the interchangeability of a risk-neutral expectation with pointwise maximization \citep[Theorem 4]{ruszczynski2006optimization} applies under either measure. Under $\tilde P$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:proof-IC-Pt}
    \mathbb{E}^{\tilde P}[Q(x,\bar h,\xi)] \,=\, \sup_{\tilde y(\cdot) \,\in\, \mathcal{L}^{2}_{+}(\tilde P)}\Big\{\, \mathbb{E}^{\tilde P}[d^{\top}\tilde y(\xi)] \,:\, A\tilde y(\xi) \leq b_{x}(\xi)+B\bar h\;\tilde P\text{-a.e.}\Big\},
\end{equation}
whence, with $z^{*}(x,\tilde P) := \max_{\bar h \in H}\mathbb{E}^{\tilde P}[Q(x,\bar h,\xi)]$, the membership constraint $\tilde h \in \arg\max_{\bar h \in H}\mathbb{E}^{\tilde P}[Q(x,\bar h,\xi)]$ is equivalent to the existence of $\tilde y(\cdot) \in \mathcal{L}^{2}_{+}(\tilde P)$ satisfying $W\tilde h \leq w$, $A\tilde y(\xi) \leq b_{x}(\xi)+B\tilde h$ $\tilde P$-a.e., and $\mathbb{E}^{\tilde P}[d^{\top}\tilde y(\xi)] \geq z^{*}(x,\tilde P)$~\citep[Prop. 2.1]{yanikoglu2018decision}. Under $\rho$, for fixed $\tilde h$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:proof-IC-rho}
    \mathbb{E}^{\rho}[Q(x,\tilde h,\xi)] \,=\, \sup_{\hat y(\cdot) \,\in\, \mathcal{L}^{2}_{+}(\rho)}\Big\{\, \mathbb{E}^{\rho}[d^{\top}\hat y(\xi)] \,:\, A\hat y(\xi) \leq b_{x}(\xi)+B\tilde h\;\rho\text{-a.e.}\Big\}.
\end{equation}
Substituting~\eqref{eq:proof-IC-Pt}--\eqref{eq:proof-IC-rho} into~\eqref{eq:proof-env} yields, for each fixed $\rho$,
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:primal-inner2}
\begin{align}
\begin{alignedat}{2}
\sup_{\tilde h}\qquad
& \mathbb{E}^{\rho}[Q(x,\tilde h,\xi)]
&\quad = \quad&
\max_{\substack{\tilde h \ge 0,\hat y(\cdot),\,\tilde y(\cdot)\ge0}}
\quad \mathbb{E}^{\rho}[d^\top \hat y(\xi)]
\\
\textnormal{s.t.}\qquad
& \tilde h \in \Psi(x,\tilde P)
&&
\textnormal{s.t.}\quad
\mathbb{E}^{\tilde P}[d^\top \tilde y(\xi)]
\ge z^*(x,\tilde P),
\qquad (\lambda)
\\
&&&
A\tilde y(\xi)\le b_x(\xi)+B\tilde h,
\quad \tilde P\text{-a.e.},
\qquad (\tilde\pi(\xi))
\\
&&&
A\hat y(\xi)\le b_x(\xi)+B\tilde h,
\quad \rho\text{-a.e.},
\qquad (\hat\pi(\xi))
\\
&&&
W\tilde h \le w,
\qquad (\nu)
\end{alignedat}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
where the leader-side feasibility appears directly as $\rho$-a.e.~since the envelope precedes the extraction of $\hat y$.

The program~\eqref{eq:primal-inner2} is a feasible bounded LP by Assumption~\ref{asm:RCR}. With the multipliers indicated in~\eqref{eq:primal-inner2}, dual feasibility corresponding to primal variables $\hat y$ and $\tilde y$ gives $A^{\top}\hat\pi(\xi) \geq d$ ($\rho$-a.e.) and $A^{\top}\tilde\pi(\xi) \geq \lambda d$ ($\tilde P$-a.e.), dual of $\tilde h \geq 0$ gives the coupling~\eqref{eq:CC}, and LP strong duality yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:proof-dual-z}
    \eqref{eq:primal-inner2} \;=\; \inf\Big\{\, \mathbb{E}^{\rho}[b_{x}(\xi)^{\top}\hat\pi(\xi)] + \mathbb{E}^{\tilde P}[b_{x}(\xi)^{\top}\tilde\pi(\xi)] + w^{\top}\nu - \lambda\, z^{*}(x,\tilde P)\, \Big\}
\end{equation}
over $(\hat\pi(\cdot), \tilde\pi(\cdot), \nu, \lambda)$ subject to~\eqref{eq:CL}, $A^{\top}\tilde\pi(\xi) \geq \lambda d$ and $\tilde\pi(\xi) \geq 0$ $\tilde P$-a.e., $\lambda, \nu \geq 0$, and~\eqref{eq:CC}. Since $\hat\pi$ enters~\eqref{eq:proof-dual-z} and the coupling~\eqref{eq:CC} only through $\mathbb{E}^{\rho}[\cdot]$, while the feasible set $\{\pi \geq 0 : A^{\top}\pi \geq d\}$ is nonempty by Assumption~\ref{asm:RCR} and independent of $\xi$, the infimum is unaltered when~\eqref{eq:CL} is imposed $P$-a.s.~rather than $\rho$-a.e., which is the form recorded in~\eqref{eq:CL}.


It remains to absorb $-\lambda z^{*}(x,\tilde P)$ into the infimum. From~\eqref{eq:proof-IC-Pt} and the definition of $H$,
\[
    z^{*}(x,\tilde P) \,=\, \sup_{\bar h,\, \bar y(\cdot) \,\geq\, 0}\Big\{\, \mathbb{E}^{\tilde P}[d^{\top}\bar y(\xi)] \,:\, A\bar y(\xi) \leq b_{x}(\xi)+B\bar h\;\tilde P\text{-a.e.},\; W\bar h \leq w\,\Big\},
\]
and the substitution $(\tilde y,\tilde h) := (\lambda\bar y, \lambda\bar h)$ scales every right-hand side by $\lambda$, valid at $\lambda>0$ and extending continuously to $\lambda=0$ since $\{y\geq 0 : Ay \leq 0\} = \{0\}$ under Assumption~\ref{asm:RCR}, giving
\[
    \lambda\, z^{*}(x,\tilde P) \,=\, \sup_{\tilde y(\cdot),\, \tilde h \,\geq\, 0}\Big\{\, \mathbb{E}^{\tilde P}[d^{\top}\tilde y(\xi)] \,:\, A\tilde y(\xi) \leq \lambda b_{x}(\xi)+B\tilde h\;\tilde P\text{-a.e.},\; W\tilde h \leq \lambda w\,\Big\}.
\]
Substituting into~\eqref{eq:proof-dual-z} replaces $-\lambda z^{*}(x,\tilde P)$ by an inner infimum over $(\tilde y(\cdot),\tilde h)$ subject to~\eqref{eq:CP}--\eqref{eq:CB}, which merges with the outer infimum, and together with~\eqref{eq:proof-env} this is~\eqref{eq:dual-compact}.
 
\subsection{Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:dual}}

The proof rests on the following two-distribution strong duality for the 
expectation-case inner LP at an arbitrary reference measure $\rho$.

\begin{lemma}[Strong duality, expectation case]\label{lem:strong_duality_E}
Let Assumption~\ref{asm:RCR} hold, and let $\rho, \tilde{P} \in \mathcal{P}(\Xi)$ 
with $\xi \in \mathcal{L}^2(\rho) \cap \mathcal{L}^2(\tilde{P})$. For the 
expectation-leader inner problem 
$V_E(x, \rho, \tilde{P}) := \sup\{\mathbb{E}^{\rho}[d_0^\top \hat{y}(\xi)] : 
\eqref{eq:P-value}\text{--}\eqref{eq:P-budget}\}$, strong duality holds and 
the LP dual takes the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:DE}
V_E(x, \rho, \tilde{P}) = 
\inf_{\hat{\pi}, \tilde{\pi}, \lambda \geq 0, \nu \geq 0} 
\Big\{ \mathbb{E}^{\rho}[b_x^\top \hat{\pi}] 
+ \mathbb{E}^{\tilde{P}}[b_x^\top \tilde{\pi}] 
- \lambda z^{*}(x, \tilde{P}) + w\nu \;:\; (\dagger) \Big\},
\end{equation}
with
\[
(\dagger): \quad
A^\top \hat{\pi}(\xi) \geq d_0,\;\hat{\pi}(\xi) \geq 0\;\rho\text{-a.s.};\;\;
A^\top \tilde{\pi}(\xi) \geq \lambda d,\;\tilde{\pi}(\xi) \geq 0\;\tilde{P}\text{-a.s.};\;\;
\mathbb{E}^{\rho}[B^\top \hat{\pi}] + \mathbb{E}^{\tilde{P}}[B^\top \tilde{\pi}] + W^\top \nu \geq 0,
\]
and the dual infimum is attained.
\end{lemma}



\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:dual}]
Fix $x \in \mathcal{X}$, $P \in \hat{\mathcal{D}}$, $\tilde{P} \in \tilde{\mathcal{D}}$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 1 (Risk-envelope expansion).}
By \citep[Section~6.3]{shapiro2021lectures}, 
$\mathrm{CVaR}_\beta^{P}[Z] = \sup_{\rho \in \mathcal{R}_\beta(P)} \mathbb{E}^{\rho}[Z]$ 
with $\mathcal{R}_\beta(P) := \{\rho \in \mathcal{P}(\Xi) : \rho \ll P,\; 
\mathrm{d}\rho/\mathrm{d}P \leq (1-\beta)^{-1}\}$. Substituting into the 
objective of Lemma~\ref{lem:value-function} and merging the two suprema 
(decoupled in their respective variables),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:proof-Vrho}
V_\beta(x, P, \tilde{P}) \;=\; \sup_{\rho \in \mathcal{R}_\beta(P)} V_E(x, \rho, \tilde{P}).
\end{equation}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 2 (Strong duality at fixed $\rho$).}
Every $\rho \in \mathcal{R}_\beta(P)$ satisfies $\rho \ll P$ with bounded 
$\mathrm{d}\rho/\mathrm{d}P$, so $\xi \in \mathcal{L}^2(P)$ implies 
$\xi \in \mathcal{L}^2(\rho)$. Lemma~\ref{lem:strong_duality_E} therefore 
applies, yielding the dual~\eqref{eq:DE}. The leader-side constraint 
$A^\top \hat{\pi} \geq d_0$ ``$\rho$-a.s.'' may equivalently be imposed 
$P$-a.s.\ by selecting a $P$-a.s.\ version of $\hat{\pi} \in \mathcal{L}^2(P)$ 
--- a strictly stronger condition that holds simultaneously for every 
$\rho \in \mathcal{R}_\beta(P)$ (since $P\text{-a.s.} \Rightarrow \rho\text{-a.s.}$).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 3 (Embedding $z^{*}$ and rescaling).}
By LP interchangeability \citep[Prop.~2.1]{yanikoglu2018decision} applied 
to the follower's standalone max problem,
\[
z^{*}(x, \tilde{P}) \;=\; \sup_{\bar{y}, \bar{h} \geq 0} 
\Big\{ \mathbb{E}^{\tilde{P}}[d^\top \bar{y}(\xi)] \,:\, 
A\bar{y}(\xi) + B\bar{h} \leq b_x(\xi)\; \tilde{P}\text{-a.s.},\; 
W\bar{h} \leq w \Big\}.
\]
For $\lambda > 0$, the substitution $(\tilde{y}, \tilde{h}) := (\lambda \bar{y}, \lambda \bar{h})$ 
rescales the right-hand side of every constraint by $\lambda$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:proof-rescaled-z}
\lambda\, z^{*}(x, \tilde{P}) \;=\; \sup_{\tilde{y}, \tilde{h} \geq 0} 
\Big\{ \mathbb{E}^{\tilde{P}}[d^\top \tilde{y}(\xi)] \,:\, 
A\tilde{y}(\xi) + B\tilde{h} \leq \lambda\, b_x(\xi)\; \tilde{P}\text{-a.s.},\; 
W\tilde{h} \leq \lambda\, w \Big\},
\end{equation}
extending continuously to $\lambda = 0$ (both sides vanish). Substituting 
\eqref{eq:proof-rescaled-z} into the objective of~\eqref{eq:DE} replaces 
$-\lambda z^{*}$ by 
$\inf_{\tilde{y}, \tilde{h} \geq 0}\{-\mathbb{E}^{\tilde{P}}[d^\top \tilde{y}] 
: \eqref{eq:CP}, \eqref{eq:CB}\}$, which merges into the outer infimum. 
The resulting joint infimum is exactly~\eqref{eq:dual-compact}; combining 
with~\eqref{eq:proof-Vrho} yields the claim. At $\beta = 0$, 
$\mathcal{R}_0(P) = \{P\}$ collapses to a singleton, recovering the 
expected-value coupling.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:DRO-decomposition}}
\label{app:DRO-decomposition}
 We prove (i) first. By Lemma~\ref{lem:dual}, $V(x;P,\tilde P)$ is equivalent to
\[
 \sup_{\rho \in \mathcal{Q}(P)} \inf
\Big\{ \mathbb{E}^{\rho}[b_x(\xi)^{\!\top}\hat{\pi}(\xi)]
+ \mathbb{E}^{\tilde{P}}[b_x(\xi)^{\!\top}\tilde{\pi}(\xi) - d^{\!\top}\tilde{y}(\xi)]
+ w^{\!\top}\nu : \eqref{eq:CL}\text{--}\eqref{eq:CC} \Big\}.
\]
The inner program is a feasible bounded LP by Assumption~\ref{asm:RCR}, so strong
duality applies to the coupling~\eqref{eq:CC}. Relaxing it with a multiplier
$\alpha \geq 0$ adds $B\alpha$ to $b_x(\xi)$ in the $\hat{\pi}$ and $\tilde{\pi}$
terms and gives $\nu$ the coefficient $w - W\alpha$. As
$\inf_{\nu \geq 0}(w-W\alpha)^{\!\top}\nu$ is $0$ on $W\alpha \leq w$ and $-\infty$
otherwise, the supremum over $\alpha$ confines it to $H$ and drops $\nu$. The
relaxed objective separates over the disjoint blocks $\hat{\pi}$
(under~\eqref{eq:CL}) and $(\tilde{\pi}, \tilde{y}, \tilde{h}, \lambda)$
(under~\eqref{eq:CF}--\eqref{eq:CB}), so $V(x;P,\tilde P)$ is equivalent to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:proof-rr-sep}
\sup_{\alpha \in H}
\Big[\; \sup_{\rho \in \mathcal{Q}(P)} \inf_{\hat{\pi} : \eqref{eq:CL}}
\mathbb{E}^{\rho}[(b_x+B\alpha)^{\!\top}\hat{\pi}]
\;+\!\!\! \inf_{\substack{\lambda \geq 0,\ \tilde{h},\ \tilde{\pi}(\cdot),\ \tilde{y}(\cdot) \\ \eqref{eq:CF}\text{--}\eqref{eq:CB}}}\!\!\!
\mathbb{E}^{\tilde{P}}[(b_x+B\alpha)^{\!\top}\tilde{\pi} - d^{\!\top}\tilde{y}] \;\Big].
\end{equation}
The leader bracket equals $\mathcal{R}^{P}[Q(x, \alpha, \xi)]$, since
interchangeability \citep[Prop.~2.1]{yanikoglu2018decision} and LP duality give
$\inf_{\hat{\pi}}\mathbb{E}^{\rho}[(b_x+B\alpha)^{\!\top}\hat{\pi}]
= \mathbb{E}^{\rho}[Q(x, \alpha, \xi)]$, and the risk envelope gives
$\sup_{\rho}\mathbb{E}^{\rho}[Q(x,\alpha,\xi)] = \mathcal{R}^{P}[Q(x,\alpha,\xi)]$.
Taking $\sup_{(P,\tilde{P}) \in \mathcal{D}}$ of~\eqref{eq:proof-rr-sep}, a 
rectangularity assumption sends the leader bracket to
$\bar{V}^{L}(x,\alpha)$ and the follower bracket to its supremum over
$\tilde{P} \in \tilde{\mathcal{D}}$. By the OCE representation~\eqref{eq:OCE} of the same $\mathcal{R}$, this
leader bracket admits the worst-case-expectation form
\[
    \bar V^{L}(x,\alpha) = \sup_{P \in \hat{\mathcal{D}}}
    \mathcal{R}^{P}[Q(x,\alpha,\xi)]
    = \inf_{\tau}\Big\{\tau + \sup_{P \in \hat{\mathcal{D}}}
    \mathbb{E}^{P}[g(Q(x,\alpha,\xi)-\tau)]\Big\},
\]
where the interchange of $\inf_\tau$ and $\sup_P$ holds for OCE risk measure with weakly compact ambiguity set~\citep{kuhn2025distributionally}.

Since $\tilde{\mathcal{D}}$ is convex compact
(Assumption~\ref{asm:ambiguity}), the follower feasible set is convex, and the
objective is affine in $\tilde{P}$ and convex in the remaining variables, Sion's
theorem rewrites that $\sup_{\tilde{P}}\inf$ as the $\inf\sup_{\tilde{P}}$
of the definition of $\bar{V}^{F}(\cdot)$, namely $\bar{V}^{F}(x,\alpha)$. This
gives~\eqref{eq:V-cartesian-named}.

We prove (ii) by evaluating the follower bracket of~\eqref{eq:proof-rr-sep} at a
fixed $\tilde{P}$. Interchangeability turns $\inf_{\tilde{\pi}}$
under~\eqref{eq:CF} into $\lambda\,\mathbb{E}^{\tilde{P}}[Q(x,\alpha,\xi)]$. For
$\lambda > 0$, the rescaling $\bar{y} := \tilde{y}/\lambda$,
$\bar{h} := \tilde{h}/\lambda$ turns~\eqref{eq:CP} into
$A\bar{y} \leq b_x(\xi)+B\bar{h}$ and~\eqref{eq:CB} into $\bar{h} \in H$, so
$\inf_{\tilde{y}}$ contributes $-\lambda\,\mathbb{E}^{\tilde{P}}[Q(x,\bar{h},\xi)]$.
The bracket is therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:proof-rr-lambda}
\inf_{\lambda > 0,\ \bar{h} \in H}
\lambda\big( \mathbb{E}^{\tilde{P}}[Q(x,\alpha,\xi)] - \mathbb{E}^{\tilde{P}}[Q(x,\bar{h},\xi)] \big)
= \inf_{\lambda > 0} \lambda\big( \mathbb{E}^{\tilde{P}}[Q(x,\alpha,\xi)]
- \max_{\bar{h} \in H}\mathbb{E}^{\tilde{P}}[Q(x,\bar{h},\xi)] \big).
\end{equation}
The parenthesized gap is nonpositive and vanishes iff
$\alpha \in \Psi(x,\tilde{P})$, so~\eqref{eq:proof-rr-lambda} is $0$ when
$\alpha \in \Psi(x,\tilde{P})$ and $-\infty$ otherwise; at $\lambda = 0$
the constraint $A\bar{y} \leq 0$ forces $\bar{y} = 0$ by Assumption~\ref{asm:RCR},
giving $0$ and matching the limit. Taking $\sup_{\tilde{P} \in \tilde{\mathcal{D}}}$
yields~\eqref{eq:g2-dichotomy}, and finiteness forces
$\alpha \in \Psi(x,\tilde{P})$ for some $\tilde{P}$, an optimal follower
here-and-now decision under that $\tilde{P}$.
 
\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:pieces}}
\label{app:pieces}
By the interchangeability principle for expectation 
\citep[Prop.~2.1]{yanikoglu2018decision}, the inner infimum 
in~$g_1^{\rho}$ commutes with $\mathbb{E}^{\rho}$:
\[
g_1^{\rho}(x, \alpha, \rho) \;=\; 
\mathbb{E}^{\rho}\Big[\inf_{\hat{\pi} \geq 0,\, A^\top \hat{\pi} \geq d_0}\, 
(b_x(\xi) + B\alpha)^\top \hat{\pi}\Big].
\]
The inner LP is the dual of 
$\max\{d_0^\top \hat{y} : A\hat{y} \leq b_x(\xi) + B\alpha,\, \hat{y} \geq 0\} 
= Q(x, \alpha, \xi)$, so 
$g_1^{\rho}(x, \alpha, \rho) = \mathbb{E}^{\rho}[Q(x, \alpha, \xi)]$. 
Taking $\sup_{\rho \in \mathcal{R}_\beta(P)}$ and applying the 
risk-envelope representation \citep[Sec.~6.3]{shapiro2021lectures} 
yields the predefined result.

\medskip
The variables $(\tilde{\pi}, \tilde{y})$ separate in $Q^{F}$: 
$\tilde{\pi}$ appears in~\eqref{eq:CF} with objective term 
$\mathbb{E}^{\tilde{P}}[(b_x + B\alpha)^\top \tilde{\pi}]$, and 
$\tilde{y}$ appears in~\eqref{eq:CP} with objective term 
$-\mathbb{E}^{\tilde{P}}[d^\top \tilde{y}]$. We treat each block 
separately.

\noindent \emph{$\tilde{\pi}$-block.} By interchangeability,
\[
\inf_{\tilde{\pi}:\,\eqref{eq:CF}}\, 
\mathbb{E}^{\tilde{P}}\!\big[(b_x(\xi) + B\alpha)^\top \tilde{\pi}(\xi)\big] 
\;=\; 
\mathbb{E}^{\tilde{P}}\Big[\inf_{\tilde{\pi} \geq 0,\, A^\top \tilde{\pi} \geq \lambda d}\, 
(b_x(\xi) + B\alpha)^\top \tilde{\pi}\Big].
\]
The inner LP is the dual of 
$\max\{\lambda d_1^\top \bar{y} : A\bar{y} \leq b_x(\xi) + B\alpha,\, 
\bar{y} \geq 0\}$, which equals $\lambda\, Q(x, \alpha, \xi)$ under 
the zero-sum identification $d_0 = d_1$. Hence this block equals 
$\lambda\, \mathbb{E}^{\tilde{P}}[Q(x, \alpha, \xi)]$.

\noindent \emph{$\tilde{y}$-block.}
For $\lambda > 0$, the substitution $\bar{y} := \tilde{y}/\lambda$, 
$\bar{h} := \tilde{h}/\lambda$ rescales~\eqref{eq:CP} to 
$A\bar{y} \leq b_x(\xi) + B\bar{h}$, $\bar{y} \geq 0$. By 
interchangeability,
\[
\inf_{\tilde{y}:\,\eqref{eq:CP}}\, 
\mathbb{E}^{\tilde{P}}\!\big[-d_1^\top \tilde{y}(\xi)\big] 
\;=\; 
-\lambda\, \mathbb{E}^{\tilde{P}}\Big[\sup_{\bar{y} \geq 0,\, A\bar{y} \leq b_x(\xi) + B\bar{h}}\, 
d_1^\top \bar{y}\Big] 
\;=\; 
-\lambda\, \mathbb{E}^{\tilde{P}}[Q(x, \bar{h}, \xi)].
\]

Then for $\lambda >0$, 
\[
Q^{F}(x, \alpha, \tilde{P}, \lambda, h) 
\;=\; 
\lambda \big(\, \mathbb{E}^{\tilde{P}}[Q(x, \alpha, \xi)] 
\,-\, \mathbb{E}^{\tilde{P}}[Q(x, h/\lambda, \xi)]\, \big).
\]
The budget $W h \leq \lambda w$ rescales to $W \bar{h} \leq w$, so 
$\bar{h} \in H$. For fixed $\lambda > 0$, the outer minimization over 
$\bar{h} \in H$ maximizes $\mathbb{E}^{\tilde{P}}[Q(x, \bar{h}, \xi)]$ 
and attains $z^{*}(x, \tilde{P})$. Hence
\[
\inf_{\lambda > 0,\, h:\, W h \leq \lambda w,\, h \geq 0}\, 
Q^{F}(x, \alpha, \tilde{P}, \lambda, h) 
\;=\; 
\inf_{\lambda > 0}\, \lambda\, \Delta(\alpha, x, \tilde{P}),
\]
with $\Delta(\alpha, x, \tilde{P}) := 
\mathbb{E}^{\tilde{P}}[Q(x, \alpha, \xi)] - z^{*}(x, \tilde{P})$. 
By definition of $z^{*}$, $\Delta \leq 0$ for every $\alpha \in H$ 
with equality if and only if $\alpha \in \mathcal{A}^{*}(x, \tilde{P})$. 
The two cases~\eqref{eq:g2-dichotomy} follows: 
$\Delta = 0$ gives $\inf_{\lambda > 0} \lambda \Delta = 0$, while 
$\Delta < 0$ gives $\inf_{\lambda > 0} \lambda \Delta = -\infty$ as 
$\lambda \to +\infty$. The boundary $\lambda = 0$ forces $h = 0$ 
(since $H$ is bounded) and yields $Q^{F} = 0$, consistent with 
$\lim_{\lambda \to 0^+} \lambda\, \Delta = 0$.
\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:game-theoretic}}
\label{app:game-theoretic}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:DRO-decomposition}, $V^{*}(x) = \sup_{\alpha \in H}[\bar{V}^{L}(x,\alpha) + \bar{V}^{F}(x,\alpha)]$, and $\bar{V}^{F}(x,\alpha)$ is $0$ when
$\alpha \in \Psi(x,\tilde{\mathcal{D}})$ and $-\infty$ otherwise. The supremum over
$\alpha \in H$ is therefore confined to $\Psi(x,\tilde{\mathcal{D}})$, where it
reduces to $\bar{V}^{L}(x,\alpha) = \sup_{P \in \hat{\mathcal{D}}}\mathcal{R}^{P}[Q(x,\alpha,\xi)]$,
giving~\eqref{eq:V-game} with a supremum in place of the maximum.

It remains to show the supremum is attained. Under Assumption~\ref{asm:RCR} and
fixed recourse, $Q(x,\bar{h},\xi) = \min_{\pi \in \mathcal{V}}\pi^{\!\top}(b_x(\xi)+B\bar{h})$
for a finite vertex set $\mathcal{V}$ independent of $(\bar{h},\xi)$, so $Q$ is Lipschitz in $\bar{h}$ uniformly in $\xi$, and
$\mathbb{E}^{\tilde{P}}[Q(x,\bar{h},\xi)]$ is jointly continuous in
$(\tilde{P},\bar{h})$, being affine in $\tilde{P}$ and Lipschitz in $\bar{h}$. Each
$\Psi(x,\tilde{P})$ is thus non-empty and compact, and by Berge's maximum theorem
\citep[Thm.~17.31]{aliprantis2006infinite} the map $\tilde{P} \mapsto \Psi(x,\tilde{P})$
is upper hemicontinuous and compact-valued, so its graph is closed in the compact
set $\tilde{\mathcal{D}} \times H$ and hence compact, and its projection onto $H$,
namely $\Psi(x,\tilde{\mathcal{D}})$, is compact. A second application of Berge, with parameter $\alpha$ over the constant
set $\hat{\mathcal{D}}$ and objective $\mathcal{R}^{P}[Q(x,\alpha,\xi)]$, jointly
continuous since $\mathcal{R}$ is Lipschitz on $\mathcal{L}^{2}$ and $Q$ is Lipschitz
in $\alpha$, shows $\bar{V}^{L}(x,\cdot)$ is continuous on $H$. A continuous function
on the compact set $\Psi(x,\tilde{\mathcal{D}})$ attains its maximum, giving the
$\max$ in~\eqref{eq:V-game}. Finiteness follows from $Q$ bounded over
$\mathcal{X} \times H \times \Xi$ (Assumption~\ref{asm:RCR}) and finiteness of
$\mathcal{R}$ (Assumption~\ref{asm:coherent}). Monotonicity holds since
$\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_1 \subseteq \tilde{\mathcal{D}}_2$ implies
$\Psi(x,\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_1) \subseteq \Psi(x,\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_2)$, enlarging the
feasible set of the maximum.
\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:saturation}}
\label{app:saturation}
With $\mathcal{R}^{P} = \mathbb{E}^{P}$, Theorem~\ref{thm:game-theoretic} gives
$V^{*}(x) = \max_{\alpha \in \Psi(x,\tilde{\mathcal{D}})} \sup_{P \in \hat{\mathcal{D}}}
\mathbb{E}^{P}[Q(x,\alpha,\xi)]$. Since $\Psi(x,\tilde{\mathcal{D}}) \subseteq H$ and
the feasible sets of $\alpha$ and $P$ are independent,
\[
    V^{*}(x) \;\leq\; \sup_{P \in \hat{\mathcal{D}}} \max_{\bar{h} \in H}
    \mathbb{E}^{P}[Q(x,\bar{h},\xi)].
\]
For the reverse inequality, $\hat{\mathcal{D}} \subseteq \tilde{\mathcal{D}}$ gives
$\Psi(x,P) \subseteq \Psi(x,\tilde{\mathcal{D}})$ for every $P \in \hat{\mathcal{D}}$,
so $\max_{\bar{h} \in H} \mathbb{E}^{P}[Q(x,\bar{h},\xi)]
= \max_{\alpha \in \Psi(x,P)} \mathbb{E}^{P}[Q(x,\alpha,\xi)]
\leq \max_{\alpha \in \Psi(x,\tilde{\mathcal{D}})} \mathbb{E}^{P}[Q(x,\alpha,\xi)]$;
taking $\sup_{P \in \hat{\mathcal{D}}}$ yields
$\sup_{P} \max_{\bar{h}} \mathbb{E}^{P}[Q(x,\bar{h},\xi)] \leq V^{*}(x)$. The two
bounds give~\eqref{eq:saturation}, whose right-hand side involves only
$\hat{\mathcal{D}}$.
\end{proof}

Under risk neutrality and containment the value depends only on $\hat{\mathcal{D}}$,
and the heterogeneous-belief structure reduces to single-distribution DRO. This
regime includes the symmetric calibration $\hat{\varepsilon} = \tilde{\varepsilon}$,
used as a benchmark in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}. A risk-averse leader
($\mathcal{R}^{P} \neq \mathbb{E}^{P}$) admits no such reduction, since a follower
expectation maximizer need not be optimal for the leader's risk, so
$\tilde{\varepsilon}$ continues to influence $V^{*}(x)$.

Because $\Psi(x,\tilde{\mathcal{D}})$ is generally non-convex
(Remark~\ref{rem:geometry}), the representation~\eqref{eq:V-game} does not by itself
yield a tractable optimization over $\alpha$. We therefore retain the multiplier
$\alpha \in H$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:risk-response}, whose feasible set is fixed
and independent of $x$, and build the min--max reformulation in the next subsection.

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:structural-minsup}}
\label{app:structural-minsup}

The starting point is the decomposition~\eqref{eq:skeleton} of
Proposition~\ref{prop:DRO-decomposition}: $V^{*}(x) = \sup_{\alpha \in
H}[\bar V^{L}(x,\alpha) + \bar V^{F}(x,\alpha)]$, with the two blocks
as in~\eqref{eq:skeleton-blocks}. We first reduce each block to a
finite-dimensional convex minimization, and then take the dual of that
minimization; the variables of the latter dual are $\zeta^{L},
\zeta^{F}$.

\paragraph{Reduction to a convex minimization.}
Consider the leader block. By the OCE representation~\eqref{eq:OCE},
for fixed $(x,\alpha)$,
\[
    \bar V^{L}(x,\alpha) \;=\; \inf_{\tau \in \mathbb{R}}\;
    \Big\{ \tau + \sup_{P \in \hat{\mathcal{D}}}\,
    \mathbb{E}^{P}\big[g(Q(x,\alpha,\xi)-\tau)\big] \Big\}.
\]
The inner $\sup_{P\in\hat{\mathcal{D}}}\mathbb{E}^{P}[\,\cdot\,]$ is a
worst-case expectation over the convex weakly compact set
$\hat{\mathcal{D}}$ (Assumption~\ref{asm:ambiguity}). Taking its strong
dual replaces the supremum over measures by a finite-dimensional
minimization with zero duality gap (Assumption~\ref{asm:ambiguity});
absorbing $\tau$ and these dual variables into a single inner
minimization, $\bar V^{L}(x,\alpha)$ becomes the value of one
finite-dimensional convex program,
\[
    \bar V^{L}(x,\alpha) \;=\; \inf_{u \in \mathcal{U}^{L}(x,\alpha)}\,
    c^{L}(x,\alpha,u),
\]
where the constraint set $\mathcal{U}^{L}$ and objective $c^{L}$ are
those of Lemma~\ref{lem:dual} specialized to $\hat{\mathcal{D}}$. The
follower block is treated identically: its inner $\sup_{\tilde P}$ is
dualized to a finite minimization, and Sion's theorem permits the
interchange $\inf_{\lambda,h}\sup_{\tilde P} =
\sup_{\tilde P}\inf_{\lambda,h}$ ($Q^{F}$ convex in $(\lambda,h)$,
linear in $\tilde P$, $\tilde{\mathcal{D}}$ convex compact), giving
$\bar V^{F}(x,\alpha) = \inf_{v \in \mathcal{V}^{F}(x,\alpha)}
c^{F}(x,\alpha,v)$. In both, $x$ enters the data only through the
right-hand side $b_x(\xi)$ (Assumption~\ref{asm:interdiction}), so it
appears in $c^{L}, c^{F}$ but not in $\mathcal{U}^{L}, \mathcal{V}^{F}$.

\paragraph{Dual of the convex minimization.}
Each block is now a convex minimization. Taking its Lagrangian dual,
\begin{align*}
    \bar V^{L}(x,\alpha) = \sup_{\zeta^{L} \in \mathcal{Z}^{L}}
    F^{L}(x,\alpha,\zeta^{L}),
    \qquad
    \bar V^{F}(x,\alpha) = \sup_{\zeta^{F} \in \mathcal{Z}^{F}}
    F^{F}(x,\alpha,\zeta^{F}),
\end{align*}
where $\zeta^{L}, \zeta^{F}$ are the dual variables of the two
minimizations, $F^{L}, F^{F}$ the dual objectives, and $\mathcal{Z}^{L},
\mathcal{Z}^{F}$ the dual feasible sets; strong duality holds by
Assumption~\ref{asm:RCR} (the minimizations are feasible bounded convex
programs). Since $x$ enters $c^{L}, c^{F}$ only as a right-hand-side
coefficient, in the dual it appears only in the objectives $F^{L},
F^{F}$ and not in $\mathcal{Z}^{L}, \mathcal{Z}^{F}$. 
Substituting into~\eqref{eq:skeleton} and merging the inner suprema
with the outer $\sup_\alpha$,
\begin{align}
    V^{*}(x) \;=\; \sup_{\alpha \in H,\, \zeta^{L} \in \mathcal{Z}^{L},\,
    \zeta^{F} \in \mathcal{Z}^{F}}\,
    \big[ F^{L}(x,\alpha,\zeta^{L}) + F^{F}(x,\alpha,\zeta^{F}) \big].
\end{align}
Set $F := F^{L}+F^{F}$ and let $\Omega$ collect $\alpha \in H$,
$\zeta^{L} \in \mathcal{Z}^{L}$, $\zeta^{F} \in \mathcal{Z}^{F}$ with
the coupling between $\alpha$ and the dual variables; prepending
$\min_x$ gives~\eqref{eq:abstract-minsup}.

We verify the three properties. \emph{(i)} The feasible sets
$\mathcal{Z}^{L}, \mathcal{Z}^{F}$ and the $\alpha$-coupling arise from
the dual of the two minimizations, whose constraints involve the
recourse matrix $A$, the ambiguity sets
$(\hat{\mathcal{D}}, \tilde{\mathcal{D}})$, and the disutility $g$;
none depends on $x$, since $x$ enters the minimizations only through
the right-hand side $b_x(\xi)$. Hence $\Omega$ is closed and
independent of $x$.

\emph{(ii)} Fix $(x,\alpha)$. Each of $F^{L}, F^{F}$ is the objective
of the Lagrangian dual of a convex minimization and is therefore
concave and upper semicontinuous in its dual variables; the section
$\{(\zeta^{L},\zeta^{F}) : (\alpha,\zeta^{L},\zeta^{F}) \in \Omega\}$ is
convex because fixing $\alpha$ removes the $\alpha$-coupling and leaves
the convex constraints of $\mathcal{Z}^{L}, \mathcal{Z}^{F}$. The sum
$F = F^{L}+F^{F}$ inherits both properties.

\emph{(iii)} Fix $(\alpha,\zeta^{L},\zeta^{F}) \in \Omega$. In the
single-level form of Lemma~\ref{lem:dual} the recourse value
$Q(x,\alpha,\xi)$ is replaced by its LP dual $\min_{\hat\pi:\,
\eqref{eq:CL}}(b_x(\xi)+B\alpha)^\top\hat\pi(\xi)$, in which $x$ enters
only through $b_x(\xi)$, paired linearly with $\hat\pi$; the CVaR
envelope absorbs the leader-side worst-case risk into
$\sup_{\rho}\mathbb E^\rho[\cdot]$ without introducing a nonlinear
integrand. Dualizing the leader- and follower-side worst-case
expectations leaves $x$ as an affine coefficient in the dual objective
$F$ and absent from the dual-feasible set $\Omega$, since $b_x(\xi)$ is
affine in $x$ (Assumption~\ref{asm:interdiction}). Hence, with
$(\alpha,\zeta^{L},\zeta^{F})$ fixed, $F$ is affine in $x$. Combining
with~(i), $V^{*}(x) = \sup_{(\alpha,\zeta^{L},\zeta^{F}) \in \Omega} F$
is a pointwise supremum, over the $x$-independent set $\Omega$, of
functions affine in $x$, and is therefore convex in $x$.

 
\subsection{Proof of Corollary~\ref{coro:algorithmic-TV}}
\label{app:algorithmic}


Fix $x \in \mathcal{X}$ and $\alpha \in H$. The proof proceeds in
four steps. Step~1 derives the primal LP forms
\eqref{eq:VbarL-LP}--\eqref{eq:VbarF-LP}. Step~2 takes the LP dual of
each block. Step~3 combines the two duals with the outer
$\sup_{\alpha \in H}$. Step~4 verifies the constraint listing of
$\Omega$.
 
\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 1 (Primal LP forms).}
\textit{Leader block.} By~\eqref{eq:VbarL-def},
\[
\bar{V}^{L}_\beta(x, \alpha)
\;=\; \inf_{\eta_\beta \in \mathbb{R}}\;
\Big\{\,\eta_\beta + \tfrac{1}{1-\beta} \sup_{P \in \hat{\mathcal{D}}}\;
\mathbb{E}^{P}\!\big[(Q(x, \alpha, \xi) - \eta_\beta)^{+}\big]\Big\}.
\]
Under Assumption~\ref{asm:finite-support}, the worst-case TV expectation
admits the LP-dual form \citep{bayraksan2015data}:
for any vector $(Z_{s})_{s=1}^{|S|}$,
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:proof-TV-LP}
\begin{align}
\sup_{p \in \hat{\mathcal{D}}}\; \sum_{s} p_{s} Z_{s}
\;=\; &\min_{\eta^{L}_{TV},\, \mu^{L},\, \sigma_{s}^{L\pm}}\;\;\eta^{L} + 2\hat{\varepsilon}\mu^{L} + \sum_{s} \hat{q}_{s} (\sigma_{s}^{L+} - \sigma_{s}^{L-}) \nonumber \\
\text{s.t. }&\;
\eta^{L}_{TV} + \sigma_{s}^{L+} - \sigma_{s}^{L-} \geq Z_{s},\;
\sigma_{s}^{L+} + \sigma_{s}^{L-} \leq \mu^{L},\;
\sigma_{s}^{L\pm}, \mu^{L} \geq 0,
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
with $\eta^{L}_{TV}$ free. Substituting $Z_{s} = (Q(x, \alpha, \xi^{s}) - \eta_\beta)^{+}$
via $Z_{s} = \vartheta_{s}^{L}$ with $\vartheta_{s}^{L} \geq Q(x, \alpha, \xi^{s}) - \eta_\beta$
and $\vartheta_{s}^{L} \geq 0$, the joint minimization absorbs the positive-part
operator. By Assumption~\ref{asm:RCR}, LP strong duality gives
\[
Q(x, \alpha, \xi^{s})
\;=\; \min_{\hat{\pi}^{s} \geq 0}\;
\big\{(b_x(\xi^{s}) + B\alpha)^{\!\top} \hat{\pi}^{s} \,:\, A^{\!\top} \hat{\pi}^{s} \geq d_{0}\big\}.
\]
Under Assumption~\ref{asm:interdiction}, $(b_x(\xi^{s}) + B\alpha)^{\!\top} \hat{\pi}^{s}
= \sum_{k} (u_{k}^{s} + \alpha_{k}) \hat{\pi}_{k}^{s} - \sum_{k} u_{k}^{s} v_{k}^{s}\, x_{k} \hat{\pi}_{k}^{s}$.
Under Assumption~\ref{asm:MC-bounds}, the binary--continuous product
$x_{k} \hat{\pi}_{k}^{s}$ is exact through McCormick linearization, i.e., the
substitution $\hat{\psi}_{k}^{s} = x_{k} \hat{\pi}_{k}^{s}$ with $\hat{\psi}_{k}^{s} \in
\mathrm{MC}(x_{k}, \hat{\pi}_{k}^{s}; \pi^{U})$ holds at every optimum.
Joint minimization over $(\eta_\beta, \eta^{L}_{TV}, \mu^{L}, \sigma_{s}^{L\pm}, \vartheta_{s}^{L},
\hat{\pi}^{s}, \hat{\psi}^{s})$ yields~\eqref{eq:VbarL-LP}.
 
\textit{Follower block.} By~\eqref{eq:VbarF-def} and Sion's minimax theorem (valid since
$\tilde{\mathcal{D}}$ is convex compact, the inner LP is linear in $\tilde{P}$ and
convex in the primal variables),
\[
\bar{V}^{F}(x, \alpha)
\;=\; \inf_{\lambda, h, \psi^{0}, \tilde{y}, \tilde{\pi}}\;
\sup_{\tilde{P} \in \tilde{\mathcal{D}}}\;
\Big\{\mathbb{E}^{\tilde{P}}\!\big[(b_x(\xi) + B\alpha)^{\!\top} \tilde{\pi}(\xi) - d_{0}^{\!\top} \tilde{y}(\xi)\big]
\,:\, \eqref{eq:CF}, \eqref{eq:CP}, \eqref{eq:CB}\Big\},
\]
where the auxiliary $\psi^{0}_{k} = \lambda x_{k}$ is introduced so that the
product $\lambda x_{k}$ in~\eqref{eq:CP} becomes the linear term
$\lambda u_{k}^{s} - u_{k}^{s} v_{k}^{s} \psi_{k}^{0}$, with $\psi_{k}^{0}
\in \mathrm{MC}(x_{k}, \lambda; \lambda^{U})$ (Assumption~\ref{asm:MC-bounds}).
The inner $\sup_{\tilde{P}}$ over the TV ball $\tilde{\mathcal{D}}$ admits
the dual form analogous to~\eqref{eq:proof-TV-LP}, with dual variables
$(\eta^{F}_{TV}, \mu^{F}, \sigma_{s}^{F\pm})$. Substituting $\tilde{\psi}_{k}^{s} =
x_{k} \tilde{\pi}_{k}^{s}$ via McCormick on the term
$u_{k}^{s} v_{k}^{s} x_{k} \tilde{\pi}_{k}^{s}$ in the dual objective and
collecting all primal variables yields~\eqref{eq:VbarF-LP}.
 
\medskip

Both \eqref{eq:VbarL-LP} and \eqref{eq:VbarF-LP} are feasible bounded LPs
(by Assumption~\ref{asm:RCR}) with finite-dimensional polyhedral feasible
sets, so LP strong duality applies. Table~\ref{tab:dual-map} records each
dual variable with its primal source; the construction is mechanical, and
we summarize the outcome below.

For each primal variable $\geq 0$, the corresponding dual constraint is

The resulting dual constraints, before any rescaling, are
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:proof-dual-raw}
\begin{align}
\text{(from } \hat{\pi}_{k}^{s} \geq 0\text{)}\quad
& (A\hat{y}^{s})_{k} + \hat{\rho}_{k}^{s,2} - \hat{\rho}_{k}^{s,3} \;\leq\; (u_{k}^{s} + \alpha_{k}) a_{s} \quad \forall k, s,
\label{eq:proof-dual-pi-hat}\\
\text{(from } \hat{\psi}_{k}^{s} \geq 0\text{)}\quad
& \hat{\rho}_{k}^{s,1} + \hat{\rho}_{k}^{s,2} - \hat{\rho}_{k}^{s,3} \;\geq\; u_{k}^{s} v_{k}^{s} a_{s} \quad \forall k, s,
\label{eq:proof-dual-psi-hat}\\
\text{(from } \sigma_{s}^{L+}, \sigma_{s}^{L-} \geq 0\text{)}\quad
& |\bar{a}_{s} - \hat{q}_{s}/(1-\beta)| \;\leq\; b_{s}^\dagger,\quad \sum_{s} b_{s}^\dagger \,\leq\, \tfrac{2\hat{\varepsilon}}{1-\beta},
\label{eq:proof-dual-TV-leader}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
plus the analogous follower-side constraints involving $(d_{s}^\dagger, e_{s},
\tilde{\rho}_{k}^{s,i}, \rho_{k}^{0,i}, \tilde{y}^{s,\dagger}, \tilde{\pi}^{s,\dagger}, \kappa)$.
 
\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 3 (Combining with the outer $\sup_{\alpha}$).}
The two LP duals are independent in $(\zeta^{L}, \zeta^{F})$, and both have
the form ``$\max$ subject to (linear constraints) $+ x$-dependent objective''.
Strong LP duality gives $\bar{V}^{L}_\beta(x, \alpha) = \max\{\text{leader dual obj}\}$
and $\bar{V}^{F}(x, \alpha) = \max\{\text{follower dual obj}\}$. Inserting
into~\eqref{eq:V-cartesian-named},
\[
V^{*}_\beta(x) \;=\; \sup_{\alpha \in H}\;
\big[\bar{V}^{L}_\beta(x, \alpha) + \bar{V}^{F}(x, \alpha)\big]
\;=\; \sup_{(\alpha, \zeta^{L}, \zeta^{F}) \in \Omega'}\; F(x, \alpha, \zeta^{L}, \zeta^{F}),
\]
where $\Omega'$ is the union of the two LP-dual feasible sets together with
the membership $\alpha \in H$, and $F$ is the sum of the two dual
objectives. By Step~1, $x$ enters only as right-hand-side data of the
McCormick constraints; therefore $x$ appears only in the dual objective,
not in $\Omega'$.
 
\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 4 (Rescaling and verification of $\Omega$).}
The dual variables $\bar{a}_{s}, b_{s}^\dagger$ from~\eqref{eq:proof-dual-TV-leader}
satisfy $\sum_{s} \bar{a}_{s} = (1-\beta)^{-1}$ and the TV-ball constraints with
right-hand-side $\hat{q}_{s}/(1-\beta)$ and $2\hat{\varepsilon}/(1-\beta)$. The
substitutions $\bar{a}_{s} \mapsto (1-\beta) \bar{a}_{s}$ and $b_{s}^\dagger \mapsto
(1-\beta) b_{s}^\dagger$ normalize $\sum_{s} \bar{a}_{s} = 1$ and convert
\eqref{eq:proof-dual-TV-leader} into the form $|\bar{a}_{s} - \hat{q}_{s}|
\leq b_{s}^\dagger, \sum_{s} b_{s}^\dagger \leq 2\hat{\varepsilon}$, while the envelope
condition $a_{s} \leq \bar{a}_{s}$ becomes $(1-\beta) a_{s} \leq \bar{a}_{s}$.
Neither $\bar{a}_{s}$ nor $b_{s}^\dagger$ appears in the dual objective $F$ or in any
other dual constraint, so the rescaling leaves all other quantities of
$\Omega'$ unchanged. The rescaled constraints match~\eqref{eq:Om-Lball}--\eqref{eq:Om-Lrad}.
 
For the follower block, no rescaling is required: $\sum_{s} d_{s}^\dagger = 1$ and the
TV constraints $|d_{s}^\dagger - \tilde{q}_{s}| \leq e_{s}, \sum_{s} e_{s} \leq 2\tilde{\varepsilon}$
emerge directly, matching~\eqref{eq:Om-Fball}.
 
Constraints~\eqref{eq:proof-dual-pi-hat}--\eqref{eq:proof-dual-psi-hat}
match~\eqref{eq:Om-Lbilin}, \eqref{eq:Om-LMC}; the analogous follower-side
dual constraints match~\eqref{eq:Om-Fbilin}, \eqref{eq:Om-FMC}. The dual
constraint from $\tilde{y}^{s}_{j} \geq 0$ in~\eqref{eq:VbarF-LP} gives
\eqref{eq:Om-Ffeas}; from $\lambda \geq 0$ gives~\eqref{eq:Om-Flambda}; from
$\psi_{k}^{0} \geq 0$ gives~\eqref{eq:Om-psi0}.
 
The dual objective $F$ collects all right-hand-side terms with nonzero
contributions. The only nonzero right-hand-side data are (i) the
RHS of~\eqref{eq:VbarL-CL}, which contributes $\sum_{s} d_{0}^{\!\top} \hat{y}^{s}$,
and (ii) the McCormick constraint right-hand sides, which contribute
$-\pi^{U} x_{k} \hat{\rho}_{k}^{s,1}$, $-\pi^{U}(1 - x_{k}) \hat{\rho}_{k}^{s,3}$
on the leader side, the symmetric terms with $\tilde{\rho}$ on the follower
side, and $-\lambda^{U} x_{k} \rho_{k}^{0,1}$, $-\lambda^{U} (1 - x_{k}) \rho_{k}^{0,3}$
from the $\psi^{0}$-McCormick. Collecting gives~\eqref{eq:dual-obj}.
 
This identifies $\Omega' = \Omega$ and completes the proof.
\hfill$\Box$

We prove Theorem~\ref{thm:sigma2-hard} by a polynomial-time many-one
reduction from \textsc{QSAT}. Let $f : \{0,1\}^p \times \{0,1\}^n \to
\{0,1\}$ be a Boolean formula with $K := O(|f|)$ subterms, and let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qsat}
    \exists\, x \in \{0,1\}^p \;\; \forall\, h \in \{0,1\}^n : \; f(x,h) = 1
\end{equation}
be the $\Sigma_2^p$-complete \textsc{QSAT} instance~\citep{stockmeyer1976polynomial}.
Since the leader minimizes, we work with $g(x,h) := 1 - f(x,h)$. The
reduction uses the corner-forcing scale $M_0 := K + 2$ and the
deactivation constant $M_{\mathrm{deact}} := nM_0 + 1$, both of bit length
$O(\log|f|)$. The instance of \eqref{eq:DR} is fixed at $\mathcal{X} = \{0,1\}^p$, the here-and-now
set $H = [0,1]^n$, the leader's risk measure
$\mathcal{R}^{\hat{q}} = \mathrm{CVaR}_\beta$ at level $\beta = n/(2n+1)$,
and a risk-neutral follower; the leader-side ambiguity is the singleton
$\hat{\mathcal{D}} = \{\hat{q}\}$ with uniform $\hat{q}$, so that all
ambiguity resides in the follower-side polyhedron $\tilde{\mathcal{D}}$
specified in~\eqref{eq:Dtilde} below.

We first construct the follower's wait-and-see linear program. Its recourse
variable is $y = (w, z)$, where $w$ collects one linearization variable per
subterm of $g$ and $z$ is an auxiliary objective variable. The scenario set
is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S-def}
    S := \{L\} \cup \{F_i^+, F_i^- : i = 1, \ldots, n\}, \qquad |S| = 2n+1,
\end{equation}
with each $s \in S$ identified with the basis vector $\xi^s = e_s$, so that
$\xi_s$ indicates scenario $s$ and $\Xi = \{\xi^s : s \in S\}$. Under
Assumption~\ref{asm:interdiction} the linking right-hand side
$b_x(\xi) = u_0 + (U_0 + U_x)\xi$ supplies the scenario data, and the
recourse value is $Q(x,h,\xi) = \max_{y \geq 0}\{z : Ay \leq b_x(\xi) + Bh\}$.
The constraint rows encode the standard big-$M$ linearization of the Boolean
formula $g$~\citep{jeroslow1985polynomial}, each row gated by a scenario
indicator $\xi_s$ so that the linearization is active only at the leader
scenario $L$; the follower scenarios $F_i^\pm$ instead deactivate $w_g$ and
bound the objective $z$ by the individual coordinate $h_i$, and a uniform box
keeps the LP bounded. A direct case check over $\xi^s \in \Xi$ then gives the
per-scenario values
\begin{equation}\label{eq:per-scenario}
    Q(x, h, \xi^s) =
    \begin{cases}
        w_g^{\max}(x, h), & s = L, \\
        nM_0\, h_i, & s = F_i^+, \\
        nM_0\, (1 - h_i), & s = F_i^-,
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $w_g^{\max}(x, \cdot) := \max\{w_g : (w,z)\ \text{feasible at}\ L\}$ is
the optimal value of the Boolean linearization. The reduction invokes the
encoding only through two of its properties, both standard for big-$M$
linearizations~\citep[Lemma~4.1]{jeroslow1985polynomial}: $w_g^{\max}(x, h) =
g(x, h)$ for $x \in \{0,1\}^p$ and $h \in \{0,1\}^n$ (binary exactness); and
$w_g^{\max}(x, \cdot)$ is $\ell_1$-Lipschitz on $[0,1]^n$ with constant at
most $K$, the Lipschitz constant accumulating additively over the $K$
subterms.

We next specify the ambiguity sets and risk functionals. The leader-side set
is the singleton $\hat{\mathcal{D}} = \{\hat{q}\}$ with uniform
$\hat{q}_s = 1/(2n+1)$, and the leader evaluates by
$\mathcal{R}^{\hat{q}} = \mathrm{CVaR}_{\beta}$ at level $\beta = n/(2n+1)$.
By the polyhedral representation of $\mathrm{CVaR}$ under the uniform
$\hat{q}$, this evaluation is the average of the top $n+1$ scenario values,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cvar-top}
    \mathcal{R}^{\hat{q}}\!\big[Q(x, h, \xi)\big]
    \;=\; \frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{r=1}^{n+1} Z_{(r)}(x,h),
\end{equation}
where $Z_{(1)} \geq \cdots \geq Z_{(2n+1)}$ orders the values
$\{Q(x,h,\xi^s)\}_{s \in S}$ of~\eqref{eq:per-scenario}, since
$\beta(2n+1) = n$. The follower-side set is the polyhedron
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Dtilde}
    \tilde{\mathcal{D}} := \Big\{ \tilde{p} \in \Delta^{S} :
    \tilde{p}_L = \tfrac{1}{2n+1},\;\;
    \tilde{p}_{F_i^+} + \tilde{p}_{F_i^-} = \tfrac{2}{2n+1}\;\; \forall i \Big\},
\end{equation}
which is compact, contains $\hat{q}$, and has $2^n$ vertices indexed by the
sign patterns $\{+,-\}^n$. The risk-neutral follower maximizes
$\mathbb{E}^{\tilde{p}}[Q(x,h,\xi)]$ over $h \in H = [0,1]^n$.

We now reduce the adversary's choice to a coefficient vector. With
$\tilde{p}_L$ fixed, the follower's expected payoff is, up to an
$h$-independent constant,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phiF}
    \mathbb{E}^{\tilde{p}}\!\big[Q(x,h,\xi)\big]
    \;=\; \frac{1}{2n+1}\, w_g^{\max}(x,h)
    \;+\; \sum_{i=1}^n c_i(\tilde{p})\, h_i \;+\; \mathrm{const},
    \qquad c_i(\tilde{p}) := nM_0\big(\tilde{p}_{F_i^+} - \tilde{p}_{F_i^-}\big),
\end{equation}
so the adversary affects the follower's $h$-dependent objective only through
the coefficient vector $c(\tilde{p}) = (c_1, \ldots, c_n)$. The block-sum
constraints in~\eqref{eq:Dtilde} make the components of $c$ vary
independently over $[-\tfrac{2nM_0}{2n+1}, \tfrac{2nM_0}{2n+1}]$, and the
vertices of $\tilde{\mathcal{D}}$ correspond to the corner coefficients
$c^* \in \{\pm \tfrac{2nM_0}{2n+1}\}^n$. Specializing the reaction-set
representation of Theorem~\ref{thm:game-theoretic} to this instance, the
leader's worst-case value admits the equivalent form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:V-reform}
    V^{*}_\beta(x) = \sup_{\tilde{p} \in \tilde{\mathcal{D}}}\;
    \sup_{h \in \Psi(x,\tilde{p})}\;
    \mathcal{R}^{\hat{q}}\!\big[Q(x,h,\xi)\big],
    \qquad
    \Psi(x,\tilde{p}) = \arg\max_{h \in [0,1]^n}
    \Big[\tfrac{1}{2n+1} w_g^{\max}(x,h) + \textstyle\sum_i c_i(\tilde{p}) h_i\Big].
\end{equation}

The crux of the reduction is that each corner coefficient forces the
follower's continuous response to a single binary vertex.

\begin{lemma}[Corner-forcing]\label{lem:corner}
For any $x \in \{0,1\}^p$ and any corner coefficient
$c^* \in \{\pm \tfrac{2nM_0}{2n+1}\}^n$, the follower's response is the
unique binary vertex
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hbullet}
    \Psi(x, c^*) = \{h^\bullet\}, \qquad
    h_i^\bullet := \mathbf{1}[c_i^* > 0].
\end{equation}
Consequently, ranging over the vertices of $\tilde{\mathcal{D}}$ selects each
binary vector $h^\bullet \in \{0,1\}^n$ as the follower's unique response.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix a coordinate $i$ and hold the others fixed. The increment of the
follower's objective in~\eqref{eq:phiF} from $h_i = 0$ to $h_i = 1$ is
\[
    \tfrac{1}{2n+1}\big[w_g^{\max}(x, h_i{=}1) - w_g^{\max}(x, h_i{=}0)\big]
    \;+\; c_i^*,
\]
whose first term has absolute value at most $K/(2n+1)$ by the
$\ell_1$-Lipschitz property. At a corner, $|c_i^*| = 2nM_0/(2n+1)$, and since
$M_0 = K+2$, the inequality $2nM_0 > K$ holds for all $n \geq 1$. Hence the
sign of the increment equals the sign of $c_i^*$, so the objective is
strictly monotone in $h_i$ and is maximized at $h_i = \mathbf{1}[c_i^* > 0]$.
Since this per-coordinate maximizer is $\mathbf{1}[c_i^* > 0]$ for every
fixed configuration of the remaining coordinates, the joint maximizer is the
unique binary vector $h^\bullet$. The final claim follows since the $2^n$
corners realize all sign patterns, hence all of $\{0,1\}^n$.
\end{proof}

The threshold separating the two cases is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Pi}
    \Pi := \frac{n^2 M_0 + 1/2}{n+1}.
\end{equation}
We record the value of the leader's $\mathrm{CVaR}$ at a binary response and
a uniform bound over all continuous responses, which together place the
yes- and no-cases on opposite sides of $\Pi$.

\begin{lemma}[CVaR at binary points]\label{lem:binary-cvar}
For any $x \in \{0,1\}^p$ and $h^v \in \{0,1\}^n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:binary-cvar}
    \mathcal{R}^{\hat{q}}\!\big[Q(x, h^v, \xi)\big]
    \;=\; \frac{n^2 M_0 + g(x, h^v)}{n+1}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By~\eqref{eq:per-scenario} and binary exactness, the scenario values at
$h^v$ are $Q(\xi^L) = g(x,h^v) \in \{0,1\}$ and, for each $i$,
$\{Q(\xi^{F_i^+}), Q(\xi^{F_i^-})\} = \{nM_0, 0\}$. The $n$ values equal to
$nM_0$ occupy the top $n$ positions of the order statistic, and since
$g(x,h^v) \leq 1 < nM_0$ the value $g(x,h^v)$ occupies position $n+1$.
Averaging the top $n+1$ values gives~\eqref{eq:binary-cvar}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Uniform CVaR bound under a yes-witness]\label{lem:uniform-bound}
Let $x^*$ be a yes-instance witness of~\eqref{eq:qsat}. Then for every
$h \in [0,1]^n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-bound}
    \mathcal{R}^{\hat{q}}\!\big[Q(x^*, h, \xi)\big]
    \;\leq\; \frac{n^2 M_0}{n+1}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $\delta_i := \min(h_i, 1-h_i) \in [0, 1/2]$. The two follower-scenario
values in block $i$ are $nM_0 h_i$ and $nM_0(1-h_i)$, with maximum
$B_i^{\max} = nM_0(1 - \delta_i)$ and minimum $B_i^{\min} = nM_0 \delta_i$.
The nearest binary point $\hat{h}$ to $h$ satisfies
$\|h - \hat{h}\|_1 = \sum_i \delta_i$ and $g(x^*, \hat{h}) = 0$ since $x^*$
is a witness, so binary exactness and the $\ell_1$-Lipschitz property give
$w_g^{\max}(x^*, h) \leq K \sum_i \delta_i$. As $M_0 = K+2 > K$, the leader
value $w_g^{\max}(x^*, h)$ and every $B_i^{\min}$ are at most
$nM_0/2 \leq B_j^{\max}$, so the top $n$ positions of the order statistic are
the $B_i^{\max}$ and the $(n+1)$-th is
$\tau := \max\{w_g^{\max}(x^*,h),\, \max_j B_j^{\min}\}$. Hence
\[
    \mathcal{R}^{\hat{q}}\!\big[Q(x^*, h, \xi)\big]
    = \frac{\sum_i B_i^{\max} + \tau}{n+1}
    = \frac{n^2 M_0 - nM_0 \sum_i \delta_i + \tau}{n+1},
\]
and~\eqref{eq:uniform-bound} reduces to $\tau \leq nM_0 \sum_i \delta_i$. If
$\tau = w_g^{\max}(x^*,h)$, then $\tau \leq K\sum_i \delta_i \leq nM_0 \sum_i
\delta_i$ since $K \leq nM_0$; if $\tau = nM_0 \max_j \delta_j$, then
$\max_j \delta_j \leq \sum_i \delta_i$ gives the bound.
\end{proof}

These two evaluations yield the correspondence between the two problems.

\begin{lemma}[Yes-instance]\label{lem:yes}
If~\eqref{eq:qsat} is a yes-instance, then there exists $x \in \mathcal{X}$
with $V^{*}_\beta(x) \leq \Pi$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $x^*$ be a witness. By~\eqref{eq:V-reform}, $V^{*}_\beta(x^*)$ is a
supremum over $\tilde{p} \in \tilde{\mathcal{D}}$ and $h \in \Psi(x^*,
\tilde{p}) \subseteq [0,1]^n$ of $\mathcal{R}^{\hat{q}}[Q(x^*, h, \xi)]$. The
uniform bound of Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform-bound} holds for every $h \in
[0,1]^n$ and is independent of $\tilde{p}$, so
\[
    V^{*}_\beta(x^*) \;\leq\; \frac{n^2 M_0}{n+1}
    \;<\; \frac{n^2 M_0 + 1/2}{n+1} \;=\; \Pi. \qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[No-instance]\label{lem:no}
If~\eqref{eq:qsat} is a no-instance, then $V^{*}_\beta(x) > \Pi$ for every
$x \in \mathcal{X}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $x \in \{0,1\}^p$. Since~\eqref{eq:qsat} is a no-instance, there is
$h^\bullet \in \{0,1\}^n$ with $g(x, h^\bullet) = 1$. Take the corner
coefficient $c^*$ with $c_i^* = \tfrac{2nM_0}{2n+1}$ if $h_i^\bullet = 1$
and $c_i^* = -\tfrac{2nM_0}{2n+1}$ otherwise, realized by a vertex
$\tilde{p}^* \in \tilde{\mathcal{D}}$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:corner},
$\Psi(x, \tilde{p}^*) = \{h^\bullet\}$, so by Lemma~\ref{lem:binary-cvar},
\[
    V^{*}_\beta(x) \;\geq\;
    \mathcal{R}^{\hat{q}}\!\big[Q(x, h^\bullet, \xi)\big]
    \;=\; \frac{n^2 M_0 + 1}{n+1}
    \;>\; \frac{n^2 M_0 + 1/2}{n+1} \;=\; \Pi. \qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:sigma2-hard}]
The map from $f$ to the instance data
$(\mathcal{X}, H, Q, \hat{\mathcal{D}}, \tilde{\mathcal{D}}, \beta, \Pi)$ is
computable in polynomial time: the LP has $K+2$ variables and $O(|f|)$ rows,
the scenario set has $2n+1$ elements, and the constants $M_0$,
$M_{\mathrm{deact}}$, $\beta$, and $\Pi$ have bit length polynomial in
$|f|$. By Lemmas~\ref{lem:yes} and~\ref{lem:no},~\eqref{eq:qsat} is a
yes-instance if and only if the decision version of~\eqref{eq:DR} answers
yes at threshold $\Pi$. Since \textsc{QSAT} is
$\Sigma_2^p$-complete~\citep{stockmeyer1976polynomial}, the decision version
of~\eqref{eq:DR} is $\Sigma_2^p$-hard. Every object used above conforms to
Assumptions~\ref{asm:coherent}--\ref{asm:explicit-scenario} and to the
specialization stated in the theorem.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:dual-VI}}
\label{app:dual-VI}
 
The proof proceeds in three steps. Step~1 establishes the probabilistic
lower bound on $V^*_\beta(x)$. Step~2 invokes LP duality to express the
per-scenario value as a minimization over the LP dual. Step~3 applies
McCormick linearization to the bilinearity $x_k \pi_k^j$.
 
\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 1 (Lower bound of the worst-case CVaR).}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:game-theoretic},
$V^*_\beta(x) = \max_{\alpha \in \mathcal{A}^*(x, \tilde{\mathcal{D}})}
\sup_{p \in \hat{\mathcal{D}}} \mathrm{CVaR}_\beta^{p}[Q(x, \alpha, \xi)]$.
Under $\hat{q} = \tilde{q}$, $\hat{q} \in \hat{\mathcal{D}} \cap
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}$, so for any
$\bar{h} \in \mathcal{A}^*(x, \tilde{q}) \subseteq
\mathcal{A}^*(x, \tilde{\mathcal{D}})$, taking $\alpha = \bar{h}$ and
$p = \hat{q}$ in the outer maximization gives
\[
V^*_\beta(x) \;\geq\; \mathrm{CVaR}_\beta^{\hat{q}}\!\big[Q(x, \bar{h}, \xi)\big].
\]
By the risk-envelope representation
$\mathrm{CVaR}_\beta^{\hat{q}}[Z] = \sup_{\rho \in \mathcal{R}_\beta(\hat{q})}
\mathbb{E}^{\rho}[Z]$ and the fact that $\hat{q} \in \mathcal{R}_\beta(\hat{q})$
since $\mathrm{d}\hat{q}/\mathrm{d}\hat{q} \equiv 1 \leq (1-\beta)^{-1}$ for
every $\beta \in [0, 1)$, the supremum dominates the value at $\rho = \hat{q}$:
\[
\mathrm{CVaR}_\beta^{\hat{q}}\!\big[Q(x, \bar{h}, \xi)\big]
\;\geq\; \mathbb{E}^{\hat{q}}\!\big[Q(x, \bar{h}, \xi)\big]
\;=\; \textstyle\sum_j \hat{q}_j\, Q(x, \bar{h}, \xi^j).
\]
 
\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 2 (LP-dual representation).}
By Assumption~\ref{asm:RCR}, LP strong duality applies to the lower-level
LP at every $(x, \bar{h}, \xi^j) \in \mathcal{X} \times H \times \Xi$.
Hence
\[
Q(x, \bar{h}, \xi^j) \;=\; \min_{\pi^j \geq 0,\, A^{\!\top}\pi^j \geq d_0}\;
(b_x(\xi^j) + B\bar{h})^{\!\top}\pi^j.
\]
Under Assumption~\ref{asm:interdiction},
$(b_x(\xi^j))_k = u_k^j(1 - v_k^j x_k)$, so the objective expands as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:proof-dual-VI-step2}
Q(x, \bar{h}, \xi^j) \;=\; \min_{\pi^j \geq 0,\, A^{\!\top}\pi^j \geq d_0}\;
\textstyle\sum_k \big[u_k^j \pi_k^j - u_k^j v_k^j (x_k \pi_k^j) + (B\bar{h})_k \pi_k^j\big].
\end{equation}
By Assumption~\ref{asm:MC-bounds}, the LP-optimal $\pi^j$
satisfies $\pi_k^j \leq \pi^U$ uniformly in $(x, k, j)$, so the constraint
$\pi_k^j \in [0, \pi^U]$ may be appended to~\eqref{eq:proof-dual-VI-step2}
without altering the optimum.
 
\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 3 (McCormick linearization).}
Introduce the auxiliary $w_k^j := x_k \pi_k^j$. With $x_k \in \{0, 1\}$
and $\pi_k^j \in [0, \pi^U]$, the McCormick envelope
$\mathrm{MC}(x_k, \pi_k^j; \pi^U)$ in~\eqref{eq:dual-VI-MC} is exact:
$x_k = 0$ forces $w_k^j = 0$, and $x_k = 1$ forces $w_k^j = \pi_k^j$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:proof-dual-VI-step2} therefore equivalently reads
\begin{equation}\label{eq:proof-dual-VI-step3}
Q(x, \bar{h}, \xi^j) \;=\; \min_{(\pi^j, w^j)}\;
\textstyle\sum_k \big[u_k^j(\pi_k^j - v_k^j w_k^j) + (B\bar{h})_k \pi_k^j\big]
\;\;\text{s.t.}\;\; \eqref{eq:dual-VI-LP},\; \eqref{eq:dual-VI-MC}.
\end{equation}
 
\medskip
Let $(x, t_0)$ be feasible for the OMP with $t_0 \geq V^*_\beta(x)$. By
Step~1, $t_0 \geq \sum_j \hat{q}_j Q(x, \bar{h}, \xi^j)$. By
Steps~2--3, the right-hand side admits the joint LP representation
\[
\textstyle\sum_j \hat{q}_j Q(x, \bar{h}, \xi^j)
\;=\; \min_{\{(\pi^j, w^j)\}_j}\;
\textstyle\sum_j \hat{q}_j \sum_k \big[u_k^j(\pi_k^j - v_k^j w_k^j) + (B\bar{h})_k \pi_k^j\big]
\;\;\text{s.t.}\;\; \eqref{eq:dual-VI-LP},\; \eqref{eq:dual-VI-MC}.
\]
Augmenting the OMP with auxiliary variables $\{(\pi^j, w^j)\}_j$ and the
constraint system~\eqref{eq:dual-VI} is therefore valid: for any $x \in
\mathcal{X}$ and any $t_0 \geq V^*_\beta(x)$, the per-scenario LP minimizers
$(\pi^{j*}, w^{j*})$ are feasible for~\eqref{eq:dual-VI-LP}--\eqref{eq:dual-VI-MC}
and satisfy the objective inequality~\eqref{eq:dual-VI-obj}.
\hfill$\Box$
 

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:finite}}
\label{app:finite}
Both claims rest on the exact cuts, which a global solver computes to
certified optimality, so that an exact cut $\ell$ generated at a candidate
$\bar{x}$ satisfies $\ell(\bar{x}) = V^*_\beta(\bar{x})$ and triggers the
update $\overline{V} \leftarrow \min\{\overline{V}, V^*_\beta(\bar{x})\}$.

We first show finiteness. Suppose the algorithm has not terminated and let
$\bar{x}$ be a candidate that already received an exact cut $\ell$ at an
earlier iteration. Since $\ell$ remains in the OMP and the lower bound is
monotone under the addition of valid cuts, re-selecting $\bar{x}$ gives
$\bar{t}_0 \geq \ell(\bar{x}) = V^*_\beta(\bar{x}) \geq \overline{V}$, so the
gap closes and the algorithm stops. Contrapositively, before termination
every candidate receiving an exact cut is a point of $\mathcal{X}$ not
previously explored by an exact cut. As $\mathcal{X}$ is finite and the
schedule produces an exact cut at least every $m$ iterations, at most
$m\,|\mathcal{X}|$ iterations elapse before either the gap closes or every
$x \in \mathcal{X}$ has received an exact cut; in the latter case the next
OMP solve already satisfies $\bar{t}_0 \geq V^*_\beta(\bar{x}) \geq
\overline{V}$. Intermediate iterations may revisit points carrying only
local or restricted cuts, but this does not affect the count, which is
driven solely by the exact-cut subsequence. Hence the algorithm terminates
after finitely many iterations.

It remains to identify the limit. Every cut is a valid affine minorant of
$V^*_\beta$, which is convex in $x$ by Corollary~\ref{coro:algorithmic-TV};
therefore $\bar{t}_0^{(K)} = \min_{x \in \mathcal{X}} \max_i \ell^{(i)}(x)
\leq \min_{x \in \mathcal{X}} V^*_\beta(x)$ at every iteration, while
$\overline{V} \geq \min_{x \in \mathcal{X}} V^*_\beta(x)$ by definition. At
termination $\bar{t}_0^{(K)} = \overline{V}$, so both equal
$\min_{x \in \mathcal{X}} V^*_\beta(x)$ and the returned $\bar{x}$ is a global
minimizer.

\section{Details on Numerical Experiments and Additional Numerical Results}

\subsection{Scenario generation procedure}
\label{app:scenario-gen}
 

The scenario set $\{\xi^{s}\}_{s=1}^{S}$ encodes per-scenario arc
capacities $c^{s} \in \mathbb{R}_{+}^{|\mathcal{A}|}$ and
interdiction-effect coefficients
$v^{s} \in \{0, 1\}^{|\mathcal{A}|}$. The two components are
drawn independently and held fixed across all experimental
configurations for a given network.

\paragraph{Arc capacities.}
We generate the capacities of the interdictable arcs through an
additive factor model. Let $k$ denote the number of latent factors.
The factor loading matrix
$F \in \mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{A}_I| \times k}$ is drawn entry-wise
from $\mathrm{Uniform}(-a, a)$, sampled once per network and held
fixed across scenarios. For each scenario $s \in [S]$, the factor
realizations $\xi^{s} \in \mathbb{R}_{+}^{k}$ are drawn independently
with $\xi_{j}^{s} \sim \mathrm{Exp}(1)$ for $j = 1, \ldots, k$. The
capacity of an interdictable arc $e \in \mathcal{A}_I$ in scenario
$s$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cap-factor}
c_{e}^{s} \;=\; \max\!\Big(\,\epsilon_{\mathrm{clip}},\
    \bar{c} \,+\, \textstyle\sum_{j=1}^{k} F_{e,j}\, \xi_{j}^{s}\,\Big),
\end{equation}
where $\bar{c}$ is a baseline capacity and $\epsilon_{\mathrm{clip}}$
floors realizations that the negative-loading tail would otherwise
drive non-positive. The additive form differs from the multiplicative
factor model of \citet{Sadana2022} in two respects. The $\bar{c}$
shift keeps capacities away from zero in the absence of factor
activity, and the sign-changing loading $F_{e,j} \in [-a, a]$ allows
the factor realizations to either increase or decrease capacity around
the baseline. Both modifications stabilize the bottleneck-finding
behavior of the lower-level LP at small $S$.

The non-interdictable arcs incident to the source $s$ or the sink
$t$ have a fixed capacity across scenarios, set to
$\max_{e \in \mathcal{A}_I,\, s \in [S]} c_{e}^{s}$. This choice
ensures that the source-outgoing and sink-incoming arcs are not the
binding bottleneck, so that the realized $s$--$t$ max-flow is
determined by the interior interdictable arcs rather than by the
fixed boundary capacities. The auxiliary dummy $(t, s)$-arc
introduced in Section~\ref{subsec:NIG-model} receives the
per-scenario capacity
$\sum_{e \in \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{reg}}} c_{e}^{s}$, where
$\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{reg}}$ denotes the regular arcs. This bound
is large enough that the dummy arc never binds in the lower-level LP.

\paragraph{Interdiction-effect coefficients.}
For each interdictable arc $e \in \mathcal{A}_I$ and each scenario
$s \in [S]$, the interdiction-effect coefficient $v_{e}^{s}$ is
drawn independently from $\mathrm{Bernoulli}(p_{v})$ with
$p_{v} = 0.75$. A draw of $v_{e}^{s} = 1$ represents full capacity
destruction of arc $e$ in scenario $s$ under successful interdiction,
and $v_{e}^{s} = 0$ represents complete interdiction failure.
Non-interdictable arcs have $v_{e}^{s} = 0$ for all $s$. The
binarization of the interdiction outcome at the scenario level is
more conservative than the deterministic $v \equiv 1$ specification
of \citet{Lei2018}, since the leader cannot fully predict the
interdiction effect on any single arc before the scenario realizes,
and is the source of part of the lower-level uncertainty quantified
by the ambiguity set on $\xi$. The realized post-interdiction
capacity in scenario $s$ enters the lower-level LP through
equation~\eqref{eq:Q-NIG} as
$c_{e}^{s} (1 - v_{e}^{s} x_{e}) + h_{e}$.

이중수준 최적화는 리더-팔로워 순차 게임에서 나타나는 위계적 의사결정을 모델링하는 강력한 도구이다. 이러한 게임은 환경의 데이터 불확실성에 노출되는 경우가 많으며, 이 불확실성이 나이티언(Knightian)일 때 강건 이중수준 최적화(robust bilevel optimization)가 자연스러운 선택지 중 하나가 된다. 나아가 팔로워의 의사결정 모형이 추계적 계획법일 경우, 하위수준 해 사상은 (분포로 표상되는) 팔로워의 주관적 믿음에 매개된다. 팔로워의 믿음이 공유지식이 아닐 때 팔로워 의사결정의 불확실성이 추가로 발생하는데, 이 문제는 이중수준 최적화 연구에서 거의 다루어지지 않았다. 본 학위논문은 이러한 공백에 착안하여, 처음 두 부분에서 강건 최적화와 이중수준 최적화를 각각 응용 수준에서 연구하고, 세 번째 부분에서 본격적으로 이 문제를 다룬다.

본 학위논문의 첫 번째 부분은 전력 시장에 참여하는 가상발전소의 순차적 입찰 및 배터리 운영 모델을 대상으로 시나리오 과적합에 강건한 데이터 기반 입찰 모델을 제안한다. 두 번째 부분은 주문형 서비스 시스템에서의 서비스 용량 계획 문제를 다루며, 운영자와 서버 간 그리고 서버 상호 간이라는 두 층위의 위계적 상호작용을 이중수준 최적화로 접근한다. 개별 서버 (팔로워)는 준마르코프 결정과정으로 모델링되고, 운영자는 시스템 수준의 성능을 최적화하기 위해 SMDP의 보상과 자원 제약을 설계한다. 다수의 마르코프 에이전트 간 균형을 유체 근사함으로써, 제안된 틀은 MDP 설계와 이중수준 서비스 용량 계획 문제에 관한 기존 연구를 확장한다.

마지막 부분은 모호한 믿음 하에서 지금-여기 팔로워를 갖는 제로섬 추계적 이중수준 계획에 대한 분포적 강건(distributionally robust) 접근법을 개발한다. 리더와 팔로워는 외생적 불확실성이라는 공통의 원천에 직면하며, 각자 그에 대한 고유의 1차 믿음(first-order belief)을 갖는다. 팔로워의 믿음이 공유지식이 아닐 때, 하위수준 해 사상이 리더가 관측하지 못하는 팔로워의 믿음에 의해 매개변수화되므로 팔로워의 반응 자체가 리더에게 불확실해진다. 이는 결정 불확실성과 데이터 불확실성을 하나의 모델링 요소 안에서 결합하는데, 기존 이중수준 최적화 문헌은 두 불확실성을 대체로 분리하여 다루어 왔다. 리더에게 팔로워의 가능한 믿음에 대한 분포(2차 믿음, second-order belief)를 부여하는 베이지안 게임 대신, 본 연구는 강건 최적화적 철학에 기반, 리더가 가능한 팔로워 믿음들의 집합에 대해 헷징하도록 한다. 그 결과로 도출되는 강건 대응문제(robust counterpart)를 유도하고 이를 처리 가능한 형태로 재정식화한다.
