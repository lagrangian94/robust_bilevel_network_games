section{Introduction}
label{secintro}
%==============================================================================
 
We study a class of zero-sum Stackelberg games in which a leader and a
follower interact through an exogenous uncertain parameter
$xi in mathbb{R}^{d_u}$. The leader commits first to a decision
$x in mathcal{X} subseteq mathbb{R}^{d_l}$. The follower, observing
$x$ but not yet $xi$, commits a emph{here-and-now} decision
$h in H subseteq mathbb{R}^{d_h}$; once $xi$ realizes, the follower
then takes a emph{wait-and-see} decision $y(x, h, xi) in mathbb{R}^{d_y}$.
Conditional on the leader's $x$, the follower's problem is therefore a
two-stage stochastic linear program (2SLP) parameterized by $x$. The two
players' objectives are oppositely aligned, so the leader's commitment to
$x$ must anticipate a worst-case response from the follower.
 
This setting can be positioned within the broader framework of bilevel
optimization under uncertainty citep{beck2023survey}. A central object in
this framework is the follower's here-and-now best response $tilde{h}$,
which the leader must anticipate as a lower-level optimality constraint
$tilde{h}$ lies in the follower's bilevel-feasible set, parameterized
jointly by the leader's $x$ and by the data-generating distribution
$mathbb{P}^$ of $xi$. In most realistic settings $mathbb{P}^$ is not
perfectly known, owing to observation noise, modeling error, and the
finiteness of historical data; the robust and distributionally robust
optimization (RODRO) literatures address this parameter uncertainty by
allowing $mathbb{P}^$ to range over an ambiguity set.
 
A natural follow-up concern, which the existing RODRO bilevel literature
has largely set aside, is the following. If $mathbb{P}^$ is uncertain
to the leader, the same epistemic limitations --- observation noise,
modeling error, finite samples --- apply equally to the follower. Each
player therefore operates on his own subjective probability distribution
over $xi$, which we refer to as a emph{belief} in the game-theoretic
sense of citet{harsanyi1967games} we denote by $hat{P}$ the leader's
belief and by $tilde{P}$ the follower's belief, and assume that neither
need coincide with the true $mathbb{P}^$ or with the other player's
belief. In zero-sum or otherwise adversarial settings the two players
have, moreover, neither the strategic incentive nor a coordination
mechanism to disclose their respective beliefs to each other; from the
leader's perspective, $tilde{P}$ is private and unobservable, while her
own $hat{P}$ may deviate from $mathbb{P}^$. The follower's
bilevel-feasible set is parameterized by $tilde{P}$, not by
$mathbb{P}^$ or $hat{P}$, and the leader faces a emph{compound
ambiguity} in $mathbb{P}^$, which she hedges by an ambiguity set
$hat{mathcal{D}}$ around her own belief $hat{P}$, and in $tilde{P}$,
the follower's private belief, which she represents by a second
ambiguity set $tilde{mathcal{D}}$ centered at $tilde{P}$.
 
To make the compound-ambiguity setting concrete, consider the disruption
of an illicit supply chain by a law-enforcement agency. The agency and
the smugglers rely on different sensing infrastructure, different
observational histories, and different operational viewpoints, so that
their internal models of $xi$ diverge even when both are exposed to the
same underlying phenomenon. The agency has access to historical
interdiction outcomes and centralized analytics that the smugglers do
not, while the smugglers observe operational realities on the ground
that the agency does not. The follower's $tilde{P}$ is therefore not a
stylized abstraction but a structural feature of the adversarial games
we study.
 
The principal class of problems to which our framework applies is that
of emph{network interdiction games} (NIGs) zero-sum Stackelberg games
on a directed network in which the leader (the emph{interdictor})
selects a subset of arcs to disable subject to a budget, and the
follower (the emph{flow player}) routes flow on the perturbed network.
The stochastic variants of citet{Cormican1998} and the recovery-budget
formulation of citet{Lei2018} are particularly relevant, as the latter
introduces the 2SLP follower structure --- a here-and-now arc-capacity
recovery decision $h$ followed by a wait-and-see flow $y$ --- that we
adopt below.

 
This study makes three main contributions as follows.
begin{enumerate}
item First, we formalize a class of distributionally robust zero-sum
Stackelberg games with a here-and-now follower, interdiction-type
lower-level coupling, and emph{compound ambiguity} in both nature's
data-generating distribution and the follower's private belief; the
maximum-flow NIG with a two-stage follower of
citet{Lei2018, Sadana2022} is recovered as the principal special case,
and the formulation accommodates asymmetric risk functionals on the two
players.

item Second, we establish the theoretical foundations of this
class. A Lagrangian decomposition of the heterogeneous-belief coupling,
combined with a Sion-type interchange across the follower-side
ambiguity, yields a structural form in which the follower's ambiguity
enlarges the admissible recovery set of the leader's outer
maximization---the leader's distribution shapes the emph{objective},
while the follower's distribution shapes the emph{feasible set} of that
maximization. A subsequent dualization--linearization chain (TV
dualization, McCormick linearization, inner LP duality) produces a
single-level mixed-integer emph{bilinear} reformulation, and we show
that no finite sequence of LP-duality steps absorbs the bilinear
coupling between the recovery-budget multiplier and the TV dual
variables of $tilde{mathcal{D}}$. The resulting decision problem
belongs to $Sigma_2^p$, one level above the NP-completeness of standard
two-stage DR. We also identify a symmetric expected-value sub-case in
which the follower-side radius becomes inactive, which motivates our
adoption of asymmetric risk functionals throughout. item Third, we design
and evaluate a Benders decomposition algorithm whose cut extraction
remains globally valid under inexact bilinear subproblem solves,
augmented by a three-phase solver schedule grading subproblem effort to
iteration phase and a family of min-cut valid inequalities that inject
network-structural information into the master problem. Numerical
experiments on synthetic grids and four real-world topologies quantify
the value of follower-side ambiguity hedging and identify a
emph{topological-saturation} regime in which the DR solution coincides
with the nominal one on real-world networks with narrow $s$--$t$
min-cuts; we report this as a candid empirical finding rather than a
positive result---distributional robustness is most valuable on networks
with dense $s$--$t$ connectivity and modest on real-world backbones with
sparse cuts.
end{enumerate}
 
The remainder of the chapter is organized as follows.
Section~ref{seclitreview} reviews the related literature and locates
the gap that this study addresses.
Section~ref{secproblem} formulates the general DR zero-sum Stackelberg
game and its maximum-flow NIG instantiation.
Section~ref{secsingle-level} reformulates the nominal model (fixed
beliefs) into a single-level program and derives the structural form.
Section~ref{secdro} extends the analysis to the DR setting and
identifies the saturation phenomenon under symmetric expectation.
Section~ref{seccomplexity} develops the tractable mixed-integer
bilinear reformulation and the complexity result.
Section~ref{secbenders} develops the Benders algorithm.
Section~ref{secexperiments} reports numerical experiments.
Section~ref{secconclusion} concludes.
 
medskipnoindenttextbf{Notation.} Vectors and matrices are in lowercase
and uppercase Roman type. $mathbb{R}_+^n$ denotes nonnegative reals.

%==============================================================================
section{Problem Statement}
label{secproblem}
%==============================================================================
 
 
The leader's decision is $x in mathcal{X}$, a polyhedral binary set
$mathcal{X} subseteq {0,1}^{d_x}$. The random vector takes values in
a closed bounded support $Xi subseteq mathbb{R}^{d_xi}$; we denote
realizations by $xi$. The follower's here-and-now decision is $h in H$
for a polyhedron $H subseteq mathbb{R}_+^{d_h}$, and his wait-and-see
decision is $y in Y$ with $Y = mathbb{R}_+^{d_y}$. The lower-level
coupling between leader, follower, and uncertainty is encoded by a
constraint matrix pair $(A, B) in mathbb{R}^{m times d_y} times mathbb{R}^{m times d_h}$,
a leader-parameterized right-hand side $b_x  Xi to mathbb{R}^m$ (made
explicit in Assumption~ref{asminterdiction} below), and lower-level
objective vectors $d_0, d_1 in mathbb{R}^{d_y}$. The players hold
beliefs $hat{P}$ and $tilde{P}$ over $xi$ in the sense of
Section~ref{secintro}, supported on $Xi$, and the leader's compound
uncertainty about the pair $(hat{P}, tilde{P})$ is modeled by a
emph{joint ambiguity set}
$mathcal{D} subseteq mathcal{P}(Xi) times mathcal{P}(Xi)$
containing it.
 
We consider the class of problems given by
begin{equation}label{eqDR}
    min_{x ,in, mathcal{X}};; sup_{(P,, tilde{P}) ,in, mathcal{D}};; V(x, P, tilde{P}),
end{equation}
where, for any $(x, P, tilde{P}) in mathcal{X} times mathcal{D}$, the inner value $V(x, P, tilde{P})$ is the optimal value of the pessimistic bilevel program
begin{equation}label{eqV-def}
    begin{aligned}
        V(x, P, tilde{P}) ;=;; & sup_{tilde{h} ,in, H}quad mathcal{R}^{L}_{P}!big[Q(x, tilde{h}, xi)big] [3pt]
        text{s.t.}quad & tilde{h} ,in, argmax_{bar{h} ,in, H}; mathcal{R}^{F}_{tilde{P}}!big[Q(x, bar{h}, xi)big],
    end{aligned}
end{equation}
the lower-level LP value function $Q  mathcal{X} times H times Xi to mathbb{R}$ is
begin{equation}label{eqQ-def}
    Q(x, h, xi) ;=; max_{bar{y} ,in, Y}; big{d_1^top bar{y} ,, Abar{y} ,leq, b_x(xi) - Bhbig},
end{equation}
and $mathcal{R}^{L}_{P}, mathcal{R}^{F}_{tilde{P}}$ are risk functionals indexed by the candidate distributions $P, tilde{P}$ used by the leader and the follower to evaluate the random lower-level value $Q(x, h, xi)$.
 
We adopt throughout the chapter the asymmetric specification
begin{equation}label{eqrisk-spec}
    mathcal{R}^{L}_{P}[cdot] ;=; mathrm{CVaR}_beta^{P}[cdot] quad text{for some } beta in [0, 1), qquad mathcal{R}^{F}_{tilde{P}}[cdot] ;=; mathbb{E}^{tilde{P}}[cdot],
end{equation}
where the conditional value-at-risk at level $beta$ is defined by
begin{equation}label{eqCVaR-def}
    mathrm{CVaR}_beta^{P}[Z] ;=; min_{eta in mathbb{R}} ;Big{eta + tfrac{1}{1-beta} ,mathbb{E}^{P}!big[(Z - eta)^{+}big]Big}.
end{equation}
The convention $beta = 0$ gives $mathrm{CVaR}_0^{P}[cdot] = mathbb{E}^{P}[cdot]$, recovering the fully risk-neutral case as a nested special case. The choice~eqref{eqrisk-spec} positions our model as the emph{risk-averse-leader  risk-neutral-follower} combination among the four leader-follower risk pairings of citet{Lei2018}, and matches the DR maximum-flow NIG of citet{Sadana2022}. The asymmetry is not merely a modeling convention the follower's expected lower-level objective is the standard primitive in stochastic optimization, while the leader's risk aversion captures the operationally costly commitment of an interdiction action against an adversary. We will moreover show in Section~ref{subsecsaturation} that under symmetric expectation ($beta = 0$) the follower-side ambiguity radius becomes structurally inactive, making the asymmetry necessary for the compound-ambiguity framework to be nondegenerate.
 
The follower commits to $tilde{h}$ here-and-now by maximizing
$mathcal{R}^{F}_{tilde{P}}[Q(x, bar{h}, xi)] = mathbb{E}^{tilde{P}}[Q(x, bar{h}, xi)]$
under his candidate distribution $tilde{P}$; once $xi$ realizes, he
selects an optimal flow $bar{y} in Y$ for the LP~eqref{eqQ-def}. We
adopt $d_0 = d_1$ throughout to encode the zero-sum structure the
leader minimizes the same lower-level objective the follower maximizes;
the notations are kept distinct to mark the players' roles. The operator $sup_{tilde{h}}$ in~eqref{eqV-def} implements the
emph{pessimistic} convention when the follower's $bar{h}$-argmax set
is not a singleton, the leader hedges against the recovery that is worst
for her own objective
citep{yanikoglu2018decision,goyal2023decision}.
 
The technical development rests on the following four assumptions.
 
begin{assumption}[Relatively complete recourse]label{asmRCR}
For every $x in mathcal{X}$, $h in H$, and every $xi in Xi$, the polyhedron ${bar{y} in Y  Abar{y} leq b_x(xi) - B h}$ is nonempty and bounded.
end{assumption}
 
begin{assumption}[Weakly compact ambiguity set]label{asmcompact}
$mathcal{D}$ is convex and weakly compact, and every $(P, tilde{P}) in mathcal{D}$ admits a uniform second-moment bound on $Xi$.
end{assumption}
 
begin{assumption}[Separability of the ambiguity set]label{asmseparability}
There exist convex weakly compact sets $hat{mathcal{D}}, tilde{mathcal{D}} subseteq mathcal{P}(Xi)$, centered at the leader's belief $hat{P}$ and the follower's belief $tilde{P}$ respectively, such that $mathcal{D} = hat{mathcal{D}} times tilde{mathcal{D}}$. Equivalently, the candidates $P$ and $tilde{P}$ vary independently of each other.
end{assumption}
 
begin{assumption}[Interdiction-type linking]label{asminterdiction}
The lower-level right-hand side $b_x(xi)$ depends on the leader's decision through
begin{equation}label{eqbx}
    b_x(xi) ;=; big(u_{k}(xi), (1 - v_k(xi), x_k)big)_{k=1}^{m}
end{equation}
for some random degradation vector $v(xi) in [0, 1]^{m}$, where $u_{k}(xi)$ denotes the (random) natural-state value of the right-hand side component corresponding to the $k$-th lower-level constraint.
end{assumption}
 Assumption~ref{asmRCR} is standard in DR stochastic optimization and
ensures that~eqref{eqQ-def} has a finite optimal value uniformly over
$(x, h, xi) in mathcal{X} times H times Xi$
citep{goyal2023decision}. Assumption~ref{asmcompact} secures the
strong-duality and Sion-type interchange arguments invoked in
Section~ref{secreformulation}. Assumption~ref{asmseparability} is the
structural counterpart of the compound-ambiguity derivation of
Section~ref{secintro} it asserts that the leader's two distinct
uncertainties --- about nature's $mathbb{P}^$ and about the follower's
private $tilde{P}$ --- can be modeled by independent ambiguity sets.
Without it, the leader would face a single joint ambiguity over
$(mathbb{P}^, tilde{P})$, which is generally less tractable; we view
the rectangular case as both natural for the compound-ambiguity
interpretation and necessary for the structural reformulation we develop
below. Assumption~ref{asminterdiction} formalizes the
emph{interdiction-type} coupling in which the leader's decision
$x$ multiplicatively degrades the lower-level right-hand side; it covers
max-flow network interdiction, shortest-path interdiction, minimum-cost
flow interdiction, and knapsack interdiction as special cases. The
linking is written in terms of $x_k$ without further restricting
$mathcal{X}$; the algorithmic developments of
Sections~ref{secreformulation}--ref{seccomplexity} accommodate
general mixed-integer $mathcal{X}$, and the finite-termination
guarantee of Section~ref{secbenders} additionally invokes a binary
restriction on the linking-active components
(Proposition~ref{propfinite}).
 
Under Assumption~ref{asmseparability}, problem~eqref{eqDR}
specializes to the rectangular form
begin{equation}label{eqDR-separable}
    min_{x ,in, mathcal{X}};; sup_{(P,, tilde{P}) ,in, hat{mathcal{D}} times tilde{mathcal{D}}};; V(x, P, tilde{P}),
end{equation}
which we adopt as the working formulation throughout the remainder of
the chapter.


The marginals $hat{mathcal{D}}, tilde{mathcal{D}}$ are abstract in
Assumption~ref{asmseparability}; we specify them concretely under the following assumptions, which both
simplifies the notation and admits the LP-representable algorithmic
form developed in Section~ref{subsecalgorithmic}.
 
begin{assumption}[finite support]label{asmfinite-support}
The uncertainty space has finite cardinality
$Xi = {xi^{1}, ldots, xi^{S}}$, and the belief center $hat{P}$ is the empirical distributions
$hat{q}, tilde{q} in Delta^{S}$ of the leader's and follower's
observed scenario datasets, respectively. Problem-specific bounds
$hat{pi}_k^{s}, tilde{pi}_k^{s} in [0, pi^{U}]$ and
$lambda in [0, lambda^{U}]$ exist (Appendix~ref{appbounds}) and
are tight enough that McCormick linearization is exact on
$x_k in {0, 1}$.
end{assumption}
 
Under Assumption~ref{asmfinite-support}, every candidate
$P in mathcal{P}(Xi)$ is identified with its probability vector
$p in Delta^{S}$, and the $phi$-divergence ambiguity sets admit the
finite-sum representation of citet{bayraksan2015data}.
 
begin{definition}[$phi$-divergence ambiguity sets]label{defphi-div}
Let $phi mathbb{R}_+ to mathbb{R} cup {+infty}$ be a convex
lower-semicontinuous function with $phi(1) = 0$. For two probability
vectors $p, q in Delta^{S}$, the emph{$phi$-divergence} of $p$
from $q$ is
begin{equation}label{eqphi-div-def}
    I_phi(p, q) ;=; sum_{s = 1}^{S} q_{s}, phi!left(frac{p_{s}}{q_{s}}right),
end{equation}
with the usual conventions $0,phi(00) = 0$ and
$0,phi(a0) = a lim_{t to infty} phi(t)t$ for $a  0$.
Given radii $hat{varepsilon}, tilde{varepsilon} geq 0$, the
emph{leader-side} and emph{follower-side} $phi$-divergence ambiguity
sets are
begin{equation}label{eqphi-div-balls}
    hat{mathcal{D}}; =; {p in Delta^{S}  I_phi(p, hat{q}) leq hat{varepsilon}},
    qquad
    tilde{mathcal{D}}; =; {tilde{p} in Delta^{S}  I_phi(tilde{p}, tilde{q}) leq tilde{varepsilon}},
end{equation}
where the follower's belief center is subscripted as $tilde{q}$
(equivalently $tilde{P}_{!0}$) to disambiguate it from the generic
follower-side candidate $tilde{p}$. Both sets are convex and compact,
and combine into the rectangular
$mathcal{D} = hat{mathcal{D}} times tilde{mathcal{D}}$ of
Assumptions~ref{asmcompact}--ref{asmseparability}.
end{definition}
 
Standard choices of $phi$ include
Kullback--Leibler ($phi(t) = t log t - t + 1$), $chi^{2}$
($phi(t) = (t - 1)^{2}$), and emph{total variation (TV)}
($phi(t) = tfrac{1}{2}t - 1$). In this chapter we are especially
interested in the TV case, $I_phi(p, q) = tfrac{1}{2}sum_{s} p_{s} - q_{s} = d_{mathrm{TV}}(p, q)$,
whose ambiguity sets are
begin{equation}label{eqTV-balls}
    hat{mathcal{D}}; =; big{p in Delta^{S}  d_{mathrm{TV}}(p, hat{q}) leq hat{varepsilon}big},
    qquad
    tilde{mathcal{D}}; =; big{tilde{p} in Delta^{S}  d_{mathrm{TV}}(tilde{p}, tilde{q}) leq tilde{varepsilon}big}.
end{equation}
The TV choice yields polyhedral ambiguity sets and LP-representable
worst-case expectations
citep{benTal2013ambiguous,rahimian2019tvdro}, which we exploit in the
algorithmic specialization of Section~ref{subsecalgorithmic}.
 
begin{remark}[Scope of assumptions]label{remscope}
Assumption~ref{asmfinite-support} and the TV
specialization~eqref{eqTV-balls} are adopted for concrete
notational ease and are invoked operatively only in
Section~ref{subsecalgorithmic} (algorithmic min--max
reformulation), Section~ref{seccomplexity} (complexity), and
Section~ref{secbenders} (Benders algorithm; this additionally
exploits the max-flow  min-cut LP duality specific to network
interdiction). All results preceding
Section~ref{subsecalgorithmic} --- in particular the dual single-level
problem of Section~ref{subsecdual-coupling}, the closed
forms of Proposition~ref{proppieces}, the saddle-point
identity~eqref{eqV-cartesian}, the game-theoretic representation
(Theorem~ref{thmgame-theoretic}), and the
saturation Proposition~ref{propsaturation} --- hold for general
support $Xi$ and any weakly compact $phi$-divergence ambiguity set
under Assumptions~ref{asmRCR}--ref{asminterdiction}, and apply
verbatim to any zero-sum Stackelberg game whose data
$(A, B, b_x, d_0, d_1, H, Y)$ match the abstract structure
of~eqref{eqV-def}--eqref{eqbx}. Other $phi$-divergences
(KL, $chi^{2}$, Wasserstein, etc.) yield analogous algorithmic forms
through their respective conjugate dualization
citep{benTal2013ambiguous}; we leave these specializations to future
work.
end{remark}

subsection{Motivation Maximum-flow network interdiction game}
label{subsecNIG-model}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
The game is played on a directed network $mathcal{G} = (mathcal{V}, mathcal{A})$
with source $s$ and sink $t$. The arc set is partitioned into the
emph{interdictable} arcs $mathcal{A}_I$, on which the leader can commit
effort, and the emph{non-interdictable} arcs incident to $s$ and $t$,
whose capacities are fixed across scenarios. The leader's feasible set is
$mathcal{X} = {x in {0,1}^{mathcal{A}}  mathbf{1}^top x leq Gamma,, x_k = 0 ;forall k notin mathcal{A}_I}$,
where $Gamma in mathbb{Z}_+$ is the interdiction budget. The
follower's recovery polyhedron is
$H = {h in mathbb{R}_+^{mathcal{A}}  mathbf{1}^top h leq w}$,
where $w$ is the recovery budget. The decision chronology is
$x to h(x) to xi to y(x, h, xi)$.
 
The random vector $xi in Xi$ encodes uncertain arc capacities
$c_k(xi) geq 0$ and interdiction-effect coefficients
$v_k(xi) in [0, 1]$, with $Xi subseteq mathbb{R}_+^{mathcal{A}} times [0,1]^{mathcal{A}}$.
Writing $N = [N_y mid N_{ts}]$ for the node-arc incidence matrix with a
dummy $(t,s)$-arc appended citep{Lei2018}, the lower-level LP value
function~eqref{eqQ-def} specializes to the maximum $s$--$t$ flow
begin{equation}label{eqQ-NIG}
    Q(x, h, xi) ;=; max_{bar{y} in mathbb{R}^{mathcal{A}},, bar{y}_{ts} in mathbb{R}};
    big{bar{y}_{ts} ,, N_y bar{y} + N_{ts} bar{y}_{ts} = 0,;; 0 ,leq, bar{y}_k ,leq, c_k(xi),(1 - v_k(xi), x_k) + h_k ;;forall kbig},
end{equation}
with $d_0 = d_1 = e_{ts}$ (the unit vector picking out the dummy arc) and
begin{equation}label{eqbx-NIG}
    b_x(xi) ;=; big(c_k(xi),(1 - v_k(xi), x_k)big)_{k=1}^{mathcal{A}},
end{equation}
which instantiates~eqref{eqbx} of Assumption~ref{asminterdiction}.

section{Saddle-Point Reformulation}
label{secreformulation}
%==============================================================================
 
This section develops two equivalent reformulations of the
distributionally robust value $V^{}_beta(x)$
of~eqref{eqDR-final}. The first reformulation is
emph{game-theoretic} it casts $V^{}_beta(x)$ as the leader's worst-case
CVaR of the lower-level value $Q(x, alpha, xi)$, maximized over those
recoveries $alpha$ that the follower could rationally choose under
some distribution in $tilde{mathcal{D}}$. This form makes the
Stackelberg structure of the original
problem~eqref{eqDR-separable}--eqref{eqV-def} transparent the
follower-side ambiguity set $tilde{mathcal{D}}$ shapes the
emph{feasible set} of admissible follower responses, while the
leader-side ambiguity set $hat{mathcal{D}}$ shapes the
emph{objective} (the CVaR distortion). The asymmetric risk
choice~eqref{eqrisk-spec} is what makes both channels nondegenerate.
 
The second reformulation is emph{algorithmic} it casts the leader's
decision problem $min_{x} V^{}_beta(x)$ as a single min--max
begin{equation}
    min_{x ,in, mathcal{X}}; sup_{(alpha,, zeta) ,in, Omega(x)}; F(x, alpha, zeta),
end{equation}
in which $alpha$ is the Lagrange multiplier of the unique
cross-distribution coupling constraint introduced below, and
$zeta$ collects the LP duals of two standard two-stage DRO subproblems
(one for each ambiguity set). This min--max form, together with
explicit bilinear coupling between $alpha$ and the follower-side TV-dual
variables, is the basis for the Benders algorithm of
Section~ref{secbenders} and the complexity result of
Section~ref{seccomplexity}.
 
Both reformulations originate from the same source dualizing the inner
pessimistic LP of~eqref{eqV-def} produces a single-level dual program
whose unique cross-distribution constraint links a leader-side
expectation under a CVaR risk-envelope element to a follower-side
expectation under the candidate distribution. Lagrangian relaxation of
this coupling constraint with multiplier $alpha$, followed by a
Cartesian separation across the two ambiguity sets, leads to the
saddle-point structure of both reformulations.
 
We work in continuous notation throughout
Sections~ref{subsecdual-coupling}--ref{subsecrepresentations},
with candidate distributions
$(P, tilde{P}) in hat{mathcal{D}} times tilde{mathcal{D}}$ over
the $phi$-divergence ambiguity sets of
Definition~ref{defphi-div}; the
finite-sum form~eqref{eqphi-div-def} is used as a notational
convenience and the structural results hold equally for general support
$Xi$ (cf.~Remark~ref{remscope}). The TV
specialization~eqref{eqTV-balls} together with
Assumption~ref{asmfinite-support} is invoked only in
Section~ref{subsecalgorithmic} for the algorithmic representation.
Throughout we hold $x in mathcal{X}$ fixed.
 
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
subsection{The dual single-level problem}
label{subsecdual-coupling}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 
For any fixed
$(P, tilde{P}) in hat{mathcal{D}} times tilde{mathcal{D}}$,
encoding the follower's arg-max condition in~eqref{eqV-def} as a
primal value constraint yields the lower-level program
begin{subequations}label{eqprimal-inner}
begin{align}
    (mathcal{P})quad max_{substack{tilde{h} ,in, H  hat{y}(cdot),, tilde{y}(cdot) ,geq, 0}};;
    & mathrm{CVaR}_beta^{P}!big[d_0^top hat{y}(xi)big] label{eqP-obj}
    text{s.t.}quad
    & mathbb{E}^{tilde{P}}!big[d_0^top tilde{y}(xi)big] ;geq; z^{}(x, tilde{P}), && (lambda) label{eqP-value}
    & Ahat{y}(xi) + Btilde{h} ;leq; b_x(xi), quad Ptext{-a.s.}, && (hat{pi}(xi)) label{eqP-leader-feas}
    & Atilde{y}(xi) + Btilde{h} ;leq; b_x(xi), quad tilde{P}text{-a.s.}, && (tilde{pi}(xi)) label{eqP-follower-feas}
    & mathbf{1}^top tilde{h} ;leq; w, && (nu) label{eqP-budget}
end{align}
end{subequations}
where the dual variables are indicated in parentheses,
$z^{}(x, tilde{P}) = max_{bar{h} in H}, mathbb{E}^{tilde{P}}[Q(x, bar{h}, xi)]$
is the follower's optimal value.
 
The leader's outer functional $mathrm{CVaR}_beta^{P}$
in~eqref{eqP-obj} is conveniently handled through its
emph{risk envelope} dual representation
citep{rockafellar2002cvar,shapiro2021lectures} for any
$Z in mathcal{L}^{2}(P)$,
begin{equation}label{eqCVaR-envelope}
    mathrm{CVaR}_beta^{P}[Z] ;=; sup_{rho ,in, mathcal{R}_beta(P)}; mathbb{E}^{rho}[Z],
    quad
    mathcal{R}_beta(P) ;=; Big{rho ;; rho ,ll, P,;; 0 ,leq, tfrac{mathrm{d}rho}{mathrm{d}P} ,leq, tfrac{1}{1-beta};;text{$P$-a.s.},;; int mathrm{d}rho = 1Big}.
end{equation}
At $beta = 0$ the envelope collapses to $mathcal{R}_0(P) = {P}$ and
recovers $mathbb{E}^{P}[Z]$. Substituting~eqref{eqCVaR-envelope}
into~eqref{eqP-obj} converts $(mathcal{P})$ into a joint supremum
over the primal variables and the envelope element $rho$, on which LP
duality can be applied at each fixed $rho$.
 
begin{lemma}[Lower-level dual]label{lemdual}
Under Assumption~ref{asmRCR}, strong duality holds for the inner LP
over $(tilde{h}, hat{y}, tilde{y})$ at every fixed
$rho in mathcal{R}_beta(P)$. Substituting
$z^{}(x, tilde{P}) = mathbb{E}^{tilde{P}}[d_1^top tilde{y}^{}(xi)]$
with $(tilde{y}^{}, tilde{h}^{})$ an optimal follower wait-and-see
flow attaining $z^{}(x, tilde{P})$, and rescaling
$(tilde{y}, tilde{h}) leftarrow (lambda tilde{y}, lambda tilde{h})$
to absorb the multiplier $lambda$ of~eqref{eqP-value} into the
follower's primal feasibility, the single-level form of the dual is
begin{equation}label{eqdual-compact}
    V_beta(x, P, tilde{P}) ;=; sup_{rho ,in, mathcal{R}_beta(P)}; inf;Big{, mathbb{E}^{rho}!big[b_x(xi)^{!top} hat{pi}(xi)big] ;+; mathbb{E}^{tilde{P}}!big[b_x(xi)^{!top} tilde{pi}(xi) - d_1^top tilde{y}(xi)big] ;+; wnu ,, {rm (Ctext{-}L), (Ctext{-}F), (Ctext{-}P), (Ctext{-}B), (CC)}^{rho},Big},
end{equation}
where the infimum is taken over
$(hat{pi}(cdot), tilde{pi}(cdot), tilde{y}(cdot), tilde{h}, lambda, nu)$
subject to
begin{subequations}label{eqdual-constraints}
begin{align}
    text{rm (C-L)}quad & A^{!top} hat{pi}(xi) ;geq; d_0, quad hat{pi}(xi) ;geq; 0, quad Ptext{-a.s.}, label{eqCL}
    text{rm (C-F)}quad & A^{!top} tilde{pi}(xi) ;geq; lambda, d_1, quad tilde{pi}(xi) ;geq; 0, quad tilde{P}text{-a.s.}, label{eqCF}
    text{rm (C-P)}quad & Atilde{y}(xi) + Btilde{h} ;leq; lambda, b_x(xi), quad tilde{y}(xi) ;geq; 0, quad tilde{P}text{-a.s.}; ;; tilde{h} ;geq; 0, label{eqCP}
    text{rm (C-B)}quad & mathbf{1}^top tilde{h} ;leq; lambda, w, quad lambda ;geq; 0, quad nu ;geq; 0, label{eqCB}
    text{rm (CC)}^{rho}quad & mathbb{E}^{rho}!big[B^{!top} hat{pi}(xi)big] ;+; mathbb{E}^{tilde{P}}!big[B^{!top} tilde{pi}(xi)big] ;+; nu mathbf{1} ;geq; 0. label{eqCC}
end{align}
end{subequations}
At $beta = 0$, $mathcal{R}_0(P) = {P}$ is a singleton, $rho = P$,
and {rm (CC)}$^{P}$ recovers the expected-value coupling.
end{lemma}
 
The proof is in Appendix~ref{applower-dual}; it combines a product-space
lifting argument with the strong-duality theorem for stochastic LPs
citep{shapiro2021lectures}.
 
Each line of~eqref{eqdual-constraints} has a direct origin.
textbf{(C-L)}--textbf{(C-F)} are the LP duals of the leader-side and
follower-side primal flow
constraints~eqref{eqP-leader-feas}--eqref{eqP-follower-feas}; the
multiplier $lambda$ of~eqref{eqP-value} appears in the right-hand
side of textbf{(C-F)}. textbf{(C-P)} retains the follower's primal
flow feasibility in rescaled form $lambda b_x(xi)$ because the
substitution of $z^{}(x, tilde{P})$ keeps $tilde{y}, tilde{h}$ as
primal variables of $(mathcal{P})$. textbf{(C-B)} collects the
rescaled budget and the sign constraints.
 
The unique constraint linking expectations under leader-side and
follower-side measures is the emph{coupling constraint}
textbf{(CC)}$^{rho}$, which originates from the dual of the
recovery-budget constraint $mathbf{1}^top tilde{h} leq w$. Two
structural features drive the rest of the analysis. First,
textbf{(CC)}$^{rho}$ mixes expectations under emph{two different
measures} ($rho$ and $tilde{P}$). Second, the leader-side measure is
the envelope element $rho$ rather than the data-generating $P$; the
CVaR-leader's risk aversion enters the structural reformulation
emph{at the constraint level} through this single equation.
 
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
subsection{Saddle-point and game-theoretic representation}
label{subsecrepresentations}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 
 
Relaxing textbf{(CC)}$^{rho}$ with a nonnegative per-arc multiplier
$alpha in mathbb{R}_+^{mathcal{A}}$ and optimizing over
$nu geq 0$ produces, for any fixed
$rho in mathcal{R}_beta(P)$ and
$tilde{P} in tilde{mathcal{D}}$,
begin{equation}label{eqV-lagrangian}
    V_beta(x, P, tilde{P}) ;=; sup_{alpha ,in, H_alpha}; Big[,
    sup_{rho ,in, mathcal{R}_beta(P)}, g_1^{rho}(x, alpha, rho)
    ;+; inf_{lambda,, h,, psi^{0}}, g_2(x, alpha, tilde{P}, lambda, h, psi^{0}),Big],
end{equation}
where $H_alpha = {alpha in mathbb{R}_+^{mathcal{A}}  mathbf{1}^top alpha leq w}$
arises from the optimum over $nu$,
$psi^{0}_k = lambda x_k$ is a McCormick auxiliary, and $g_1^{rho}$
and $g_2$ are the leader-side and follower-side blocks separated by the
Lagrangian; full expressions are in
Appendix~ref{applagrangian-CVaR}. The polyhedron $H_alpha$ coincides
exactly with the recovery polyhedron $H$, an observation we sharpen in
Remark~ref{remalpha-h} below.
 
Two closed-form computations dispense with the inner LPs.
 
begin{proposition}[Closed forms of $g_1^{rho}$ and $g_2$]label{proppieces}
For any $x in mathcal{X}$, any $alpha in H_alpha$, and any
candidate distributions $P, tilde{P}$ on $Xi$,
begin{enumerate}
item[(i)] (emph{Leader piece}) $g_1^{rho}(x, alpha, rho) = mathbb{E}^{rho}[Q(x, alpha, xi)]$ for every $rho in mathcal{R}_beta(P)$, and consequently
begin{equation}label{eqg1-closed}
    sup_{rho ,in, mathcal{R}_beta(P)}, g_1^{rho}(x, alpha, rho)
    ;=; mathrm{CVaR}_beta^{P}!big[Q(x, alpha, xi)big];
end{equation}
 
item[(ii)] (emph{Follower piece})
begin{equation}label{eqg2-dichotomy}
    inf_{lambda ,geq, 0,, h ,geq, 0,, mathbf{1}^top h ,leq, lambda w};
    g_2(x, alpha, tilde{P}, lambda, h, psi^{0}) ;=;
    begin{cases}
        0 & alpha in mathcal{A}^{}(x, tilde{P}), 
        -infty & text{otherwise},
    end{cases}
end{equation}
where the emph{admissible recovery set} at $tilde{P}$ is
begin{equation}label{eqA-star-p}
    mathcal{A}^{}(x, tilde{P}) ;=; argmax_{bar{h} ,in, H};
    mathbb{E}^{tilde{P}}!big[Q(x, bar{h}, xi)big].
end{equation}
end{enumerate}
end{proposition}
 
The leader piece~eqref{eqg1-closed} follows from appyling duality $xi$-pointwise inside the $rho$-expectation, followed
by the risk-envelope identity~eqref{eqCVaR-envelope}. The follower
dichotomy reduces, after eliminating the inner LP for $tilde{y}$ via
duality and using positive homogeneity of $Q$ in $h$, to
begin{equation}label{eqlambda-penalty}
    inf_{lambda geq 0}, lambda, Delta(alpha, x, tilde{P}),qquad
    Delta(alpha, x, tilde{P}) ;=; mathbb{E}^{tilde{P}}!big[Q(x, alpha, xi)big] - z^{}(x, tilde{P}) ,leq, 0,
end{equation}
with $Delta = 0$ exactly on the arg-max set. Proofs are in
Appendices~ref{applagrangian-CVaR}--ref{appg2-proof}.
 
 
begin{remark}[The dual identification $alpha equiv h$]label{remalpha-h}
In $(mathcal{P})$ the recovery $h$ is a budget-constrained primal
variable. Dualizing converts it into the
coupling constraint textbf{(CC)}$^{rho}$ with scalar dual $nu$;
Lagrangian relaxation reintroduces a variable dual to it, $alpha$, whose
envelope $H_alpha$ coincides with the recovery polyhedron $H$.
Proposition~ref{propg2} sharpens this correspondence $alpha$ is
admissible iff it is itself an optimal recovery for the follower under
some $tilde{P}$ in $tilde{mathcal{D}}$.
end{remark}
 
Substituting~eqref{eqg1-closed} into~eqref{eqV-lagrangian} and
lifting to the DR sup over
$(P, tilde{P}) in hat{mathcal{D}} times tilde{mathcal{D}}$
(rectangularity of $mathcal{D}$, Assumption~ref{asmseparability})
separates the two ambiguity suprema across the two blocks. On the
follower side, the nested operator
$sup_{tilde{P}} inf_{lambda, h, psi^{0}}$ is interchangeable by
Sion's theorem (Appendix~ref{appsion-proof}). After interchange,
begin{subequations}label{eqV-cartesian}
begin{align}
    V^{}_beta(x) &;=; sup_{alpha ,in, H_alpha};
    Big[,sup_{P ,in, hat{mathcal{D}}}, mathrm{CVaR}_beta^{P}!big[Q(x, alpha, xi)big]
    ;+; inf_{lambda,, h,, psi^{0}}, sup_{tilde{P} ,in, tilde{mathcal{D}}}, g_2(x, alpha, tilde{P}, lambda, h, psi^{0}),Big] label{eqV-cartesian-raw}
    &;=; sup_{alpha ,in, H_alpha};
    big[,bar{V}^{L}_beta(x, alpha) ;+; bar{V}^{F}(x, alpha),big], label{eqV-cartesian-named}
end{align}
where
begin{align}
    bar{V}^{L}_beta(x, alpha) &;=; inf_{eta_beta};
    Big{,eta_beta ;+; tfrac{1}{1-beta},sup_{P ,in, hat{mathcal{D}}};
    mathbb{E}^{P}!big[,(Q(x, alpha, xi) - eta_beta)^{+},big],Big}, label{eqVbarL-def}
    bar{V}^{F}(x, alpha) &;=; inf_{lambda,, h,, psi^{0}};
    sup_{tilde{P} ,in, tilde{mathcal{D}}};
    inf_{tilde{y}(cdot),, tilde{pi}(cdot)};
    Big{,mathbb{E}^{tilde{P}}!big[,b_x(xi)^{!top}tilde{pi}(xi) - d_0^top tilde{y}(xi),big] ,, text{eqref{eqCF}, eqref{eqCP}, eqref{eqCB}},Big}. label{eqVbarF-def}
end{align}
end{subequations}

Identity~eqref{eqV-cartesian-named} reduces the leader's decision
problem~eqref{eqDR} to the single min--max
begin{equation}label{eqV-minmax-block}
    min_{x ,in, mathcal{X}}; V^{}_beta(x) ;=;
    min_{x ,in, mathcal{X}};
    sup_{alpha ,in, H_alpha};
    big[,bar{V}^{L}_beta(x, alpha) ;+; bar{V}^{F}(x, alpha),big],
end{equation}
inside which sit two two-stage DRO subproblems. The leader block
$bar{V}^{L}_beta$ is emph{risk-averse} (CVaR objective on
ambiguity set $hat{mathcal{D}}$), and the follower block
$bar{V}^{F}$ is emph{risk-neutral} (expected objective on
$tilde{mathcal{D}}$), coupled by the multiplier $alpha$.

Applying the dichotomy~eqref{eqg2-dichotomy} to the follower-side
block of~eqref{eqV-cartesian} collapses the inner $inf_{lambda, h, psi^{0}}$
into a membership constraint over the emph{DRO admissible recovery
set}
begin{equation}label{eqA-star-D}
    mathcal{A}^{}(x, tilde{mathcal{D}}) ;=; bigcup_{tilde{P} ,in, tilde{mathcal{D}}}, mathcal{A}^{}(x, tilde{P}).
end{equation}
 
begin{theorem}[Game-theoretic representation]label{thmgame-theoretic}
Under Assumptions~ref{asmRCR}--ref{asminterdiction} and for $beta in [0, 1)$,
begin{equation}label{eqV-game}
    V^{}_beta(x) ;=; max_{alpha ,in, mathcal{A}^{}(x, tilde{mathcal{D}})};
    sup_{P ,in, hat{mathcal{D}}}; mathrm{CVaR}_beta^{P}!big[Q(x, alpha, xi)big].
end{equation}
$V^{}_beta(x)$ is finite for every $x in mathcal{X}$ and is weakly
monotonically increasing in $tilde{varepsilon}$.
end{theorem}
 
The proof is in Appendix~ref{appstructural-proof}.
Theorem~ref{thmgame-theoretic} has a transparent Stackelberg reading.
The leader's optimization is over the recoveries $alpha$ that the
follower could rationally choose under emph{some} distribution in
$tilde{mathcal{D}}$; this is the game-theoretic admissible-recovery
set. Given such an $alpha$, the leader pays the worst-case CVaR of the
lower-level value $Q(x, alpha, xi)$ over her own ambiguity set
$hat{mathcal{D}}$. The two ambiguity sets thus enter through
structurally distinct channels $tilde{mathcal{D}}$ controls the
emph{game-theoretic feasibility} (admissible follower responses),
$hat{mathcal{D}}$ controls the emph{risk-adjusted objective}. The
asymmetric risk choice~eqref{eqrisk-spec} is what keeps both channels
active at $beta = 0$, Proposition~ref{propsaturation} below shows
that the feasibility channel becomes inactive on a structurally natural
regime.
 
 % -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
subsection{Algorithmic min--max reformulation}
label{subsecalgorithmic}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
The representation~eqref{eqV-cartesian} suits computation only after
$bar{V}^{L}_beta$ and $bar{V}^{F}$ are converted into LP-representable
form. To that end, we invoke Assumption~ref{asmfinite-support} (data-driven
finite support with empirical belief centers) together with the TV
ambiguity sets~eqref{eqTV-balls}. Writing $u_k^{s} = u_k(xi^{s})$
and $v_k^{s} = v_k(xi^{s})$, and applying Rockafellar--Uryasev primal
expansion of the CVaR functional, total-variation LP dualization of
each worst-case expectation, and McCormick linearization of the
binary--continuous bilinearities $x_k hat{pi}_k^{s}, x_k tilde{pi}_k^{s}, lambda x_k$,
the two blocks of~eqref{eqV-cartesian} admit the explicit primal LP forms
begin{subequations}label{eqVbarL-LP}
begin{align}
    bar{V}^{L}_beta(x, alpha) ;=; min;; & eta_beta + tfrac{1}{1-beta}Big[eta^{L} + 2hat{varepsilon},mu^{L} + textstylesum_{s} hat{q}_{s},(sigma_{s}^{L+} - sigma_{s}^{L-})Big] label{eqVbarL-obj}
    text{s.t.}quad & sigma_{s}^{L+} - sigma_{s}^{L-} + eta^{L} ,geq, zeta_{s}^{L},quad sigma_{s}^{L+} + sigma_{s}^{L-} ,leq, mu^{L} quad forall s, label{eqVbarL-TV}
    & zeta_{s}^{L} ,geq, textstylesum_{k}!big[(u_k^{s} + alpha_k),hat{pi}_k^{s} - u_k^{s} v_k^{s}, hat{psi}_k^{s}big] - eta_beta quad forall s, label{eqVbarL-RU}
    & A^{!top} hat{pi}^{s} ,geq, d_{0},quad hat{pi}^{s} ,geq, 0 quad forall s, label{eqVbarL-CL}
    & hat{psi}_k^{s} ,in, mathrm{MC}(x_k,, hat{pi}_k^{s};, pi^{U}) quad forall k, s, label{eqVbarL-MC}
    & zeta_{s}^{L}, mu^{L}, sigma_{s}^{Lpm}, hat{psi}_k^{s} ,geq, 0,quad eta_beta, eta^{L} text{ free}, label{eqVbarL-nn}
end{align}
end{subequations}
begin{subequations}label{eqVbarF-LP}
begin{align}
    bar{V}^{F}(x, alpha) ;=; min;; & eta^{F} + 2tilde{varepsilon},mu^{F} + textstylesum_{s} tilde{q}_{s},(sigma_{s}^{F+} - sigma_{s}^{F-}) label{eqVbarF-obj}
    text{s.t.}quad & sigma_{s}^{F+} - sigma_{s}^{F-} + eta^{F} ,geq, textstylesum_{k}!big[(u_k^{s} + alpha_k),tilde{pi}_k^{s} - u_k^{s} v_k^{s}, tilde{psi}_k^{s}big] - d_{0}^{!top}, tilde{y}^{s} quad forall s, label{eqVbarF-TV}
    & sigma_{s}^{F+} + sigma_{s}^{F-} ,leq, mu^{F} quad forall s, label{eqVbarF-TVnorm}
    & A^{!top} tilde{pi}^{s} ,geq, lambda, d_{1},quad tilde{pi}^{s} ,geq, 0 quad forall s, tag{C-F} label{eqVbarF-CF}
    & (Atilde{y}^{s})_k + h_k ,leq, lambda, u_k^{s} - u_k^{s} v_k^{s},psi_k^{0} quad forall k, s, tag{C-P} label{eqVbarF-CP}
    & mathbf{1}^{!top}, h ,leq, lambda, w, tag{C-B} label{eqVbarF-CB}
    & tilde{psi}_k^{s} ,in, mathrm{MC}(x_k,, tilde{pi}_k^{s};, pi^{U}),quad psi_k^{0} ,in, mathrm{MC}(x_k,, lambda;, lambda^{U}) quad forall k, s, label{eqVbarF-MC}
    & tilde{y}^{s}, tilde{pi}^{s}, tilde{psi}_k^{s}, h, lambda, psi_k^{0}, mu^{F}, sigma_{s}^{Fpm} ,geq, 0,quad eta^{F} text{ free}, label{eqVbarF-nn}
end{align}
end{subequations}
where $mathrm{MC}(x, pi; U)$ abbreviates the McCormick envelope of
$psi = x pi$ with $pi in [0, U]$ and $x in {0, 1}$, viz.
$psi leq U x,; psi leq pi,; psi geq pi - U(1-x),; psi geq 0$.
For fixed $(x, alpha)$, both~eqref{eqVbarL-LP}
and~eqref{eqVbarF-LP} are LPs; the products $alpha_k hat{pi}_k^{s}$
and $alpha_k tilde{pi}_k^{s}$ enter the constraint matrix linearly
in $(hat{pi}^{s}, tilde{pi}^{s})$, while $x_k$ enters only as
McCormick right-hand-side parameter. Full derivation is in
Appendix~ref{appformulation}.
 
Taking the LP dual of each minimization and combining with the outer
$sup_{alpha in H_alpha}$ yields the final saddle-point form, which
we record as the main reformulation of this chapter. We adopt the
notation of citet{bayraksan2015data} for the LP-dual variables $a_s, b_s$
(leader-side TV duals), $d_s, e_s$ (follower-side TV duals),
$hatrho_k^{s,i}, tilderho_k^{s,i}$ (McCormick duals for
$hatpsi, tildepsi$), $rho_k^{0,i}$ (McCormick duals for $psi^0$),
and the remaining variables as introduced below.
 
begin{theorem}[Algorithmic min--max reformulation]label{thmalgorithmic}
Under Assumptions~ref{asmRCR}--ref{asmfinite-support} and for
$beta in [0, 1)$,
begin{equation}label{eqsingle-level}
    min_{x ,in, mathcal{X}}; V^{}_beta(x)
    ;=;
    min_{x ,in, mathcal{X}};
    sup_{(alpha,, zeta^{L},, zeta^{F}) ,in, Omega};
    F(x, alpha, zeta^{L}, zeta^{F}),
end{equation}
where the LP-dual variables collected in
$zeta^{L} = (a_{s}, bar{a}_{s}, b_{s}, hat{y}^{s}, hat{rho}_{k}^{s,1}, hat{rho}_{k}^{s,2}, hat{rho}_{k}^{s,3})$
and
$zeta^{F} = (d_{s}, e_{s}, tilde{y}^{s,dagger}, tilde{pi}^{s,dagger}, kappa, tilde{rho}_{k}^{s,1}, tilde{rho}_{k}^{s,2}, tilde{rho}_{k}^{s,3}, rho_{k}^{0,1}, rho_{k}^{0,2}, rho_{k}^{0,3})$
are the LP duals of the primal LPs~eqref{eqVbarL-LP}
and~eqref{eqVbarF-LP}; in particular,
$hat{y}^{s}, tilde{y}^{s,dagger}$ recover the leader-side and
follower-side max-flows, $tilde{pi}^{s,dagger}$ is the dual of the
follower's capacity constraint, and $kappa geq 0$ is the dual of the
recovery-budget constraint $mathbf{1}^{!top} h leq lambda w$. The
dual objective is
begin{equation}label{eqdual-obj}
    F(x, alpha, zeta^{L}, zeta^{F})
    ;=; textstylesum_{s} d_{0}^{!top}, hat{y}^{s}
    ;-; pi^{U}!!sum_{k,s}!big[x_k,(hat{rho}_{k}^{s,1} + tilde{rho}_{k}^{s,1}) + (1 - x_k)(hat{rho}_{k}^{s,3} + tilde{rho}_{k}^{s,3})big]
    ;-; lambda^{U}!sum_{k}!big[x_k, rho_{k}^{0,1} + (1 - x_k), rho_{k}^{0,3}big],
end{equation}
and the feasible set $Omega$ is
begin{subequations}label{eqOmega}
begin{align}
    textstylesum_{s} a_{s} &;=; textstylesum_{s} bar{a}_{s} ;=; 1, qquad (1-beta), a_{s} ,leq, bar{a}_{s} quad forall s, label{eqOm-Lball}
    bar{a}_{s} - hat{q}_{s} &;leq; b_{s} quad forall s, qquad textstylesum_{s} b_{s} ,leq, 2hat{varepsilon}, label{eqOm-Lrad}
    textstylesum_{s} d_{s} &;=; 1, qquad d_{s} - tilde{q}_{s} ,leq, e_{s} quad forall s, qquad textstylesum_{s} e_{s} ,leq, 2tilde{varepsilon}, label{eqOm-Fball}
    (Ahat{y}^{s})_{k} + hat{rho}_{k}^{s,2} - hat{rho}_{k}^{s,3} &;leq; (u_{k}^{s} + alpha_{k}), a_{s} quad forall k, s, label{eqOm-Lbilin}
    (Atilde{y}^{s,dagger})_{k} + tilde{rho}_{k}^{s,2} - tilde{rho}_{k}^{s,3} &;leq; (u_{k}^{s} + alpha_{k}), d_{s} quad forall k, s, label{eqOm-Fbilin}
    hat{rho}_{k}^{s,1} + hat{rho}_{k}^{s,2} - hat{rho}_{k}^{s,3} &;geq; u_{k}^{s} v_{k}^{s}, a_{s} quad forall k, s, label{eqOm-LMC}
    tilde{rho}_{k}^{s,1} + tilde{rho}_{k}^{s,2} - tilde{rho}_{k}^{s,3} &;geq; u_{k}^{s} v_{k}^{s}, d_{s} quad forall k, s, label{eqOm-FMC}
    (A^{!top} tilde{pi}^{s,dagger})_{j} &;geq; d_{0,j}, d_{s} quad forall j, s, label{eqOm-Ffeas}
    textstylesum_{k,s} u_{k}^{s}, tilde{pi}_{k}^{s,dagger} + w,kappa + textstylesum_{k}(rho_{k}^{0,2} - rho_{k}^{0,3}) &;leq; textstylesum_{s} d_{1}^{!top}, tilde{y}^{s,dagger}, label{eqOm-Flambda}
    rho_{k}^{0,3} - rho_{k}^{0,1} - rho_{k}^{0,2} &;leq; textstylesum_{s} u_{k}^{s} v_{k}^{s}, tilde{pi}_{k}^{s,dagger} quad forall k, label{eqOm-psi0}
    alpha in H_{alpha}, quad &zeta^{L}, zeta^{F} ,geq, 0. label{eqOm-sign}
end{align}
end{subequations}
The objective $F$ is linear in $x$ for fixed $(alpha, zeta^{L}, zeta^{F})$ and linear in $(zeta^{L}, zeta^{F})$ for fixed $x$. Constraints~eqref{eqOm-Lbilin} and~eqref{eqOm-Fbilin} are continuous--continuous bilinearities in $alpha_{k} cdot a_{s}$ and $alpha_{k} cdot d_{s}$, respectively; all other constraints of $Omega$ are linear, and $Omega$ does not depend on $x$.
end{theorem}
 
The constraints of $Omega$ have the following structural meaning,
each arising from LP duality applied to a specific primal block
of~eqref{eqVbarL-LP}--eqref{eqVbarF-LP}
begin{itemize}
item eqref{eqOm-Lball}--eqref{eqOm-Lrad} confine the
leader-side scenario weights to the CVaR-distorted TV ball $a_{s}$
is the CVaR-distorted worst-case probability (the dual of the
R--U linking constraint~eqref{eqVbarL-RU}), $bar{a}_{s}$ is the
ambient TV-ball worst-case probability (the dual of the TV
link~eqref{eqVbarL-TV} rescaled to sum to one), and $b_{s}$ is the
TV-norm slack. The CVaR risk-envelope condition
$(1-beta),a_{s} leq bar{a}_{s}$ links the two. At $beta = 0$,
$a_{s} = bar{a}_{s}$ and the formulation collapses to the risk-neutral
case studied in the research notes.
item eqref{eqOm-Fball} is the analogous TV-ball constraint on the
follower-side scenario weight $d_{s}$, the dual of the
follower-side TV link~eqref{eqVbarF-TV}.
item eqref{eqOm-Lbilin} and~eqref{eqOm-Fbilin} are the dual
feasibility constraints for the primal max-flow capacity duals
$hat{pi}_{k}^{s}$ and $tilde{pi}_{k}^{s}$; the bilinear products
$alpha_{k},a_{s}$ and $alpha_{k},d_{s}$ arise from the Lagrangian
relaxation of the recovery-budget constraint and are the only
nonlinearities in $Omega$.
item eqref{eqOm-LMC} and~eqref{eqOm-FMC} are the dual feasibility
constraints for the McCormick auxiliaries $hat{psi}_{k}^{s}$ and
$tilde{psi}_{k}^{s}$.
item eqref{eqOm-Ffeas} is the dual feasibility constraint for the
follower's flow recovery $tilde{y}^{s}$ in~eqref{eqVbarF-CP},
paralleling the leader's max-flow dual~eqref{eqVbarL-CL}.
item eqref{eqOm-Flambda} arises from the dual of $lambda geq 0$
in the follower piece, balancing capacity, budget, and
$psi^{0}$-McCormick contributions.
item eqref{eqOm-psi0} is the dual feasibility constraint for the
McCormick auxiliary $psi_{k}^{0} = lambda x_{k}$.
end{itemize}
Full derivation, with explicit identification of each LP-dual variable
with its primal source, is in Appendix~ref{appformulation}.
 
begin{remark}[Irreducibility of the bilinearity]label{remirreducibility}
In standard two-stage DR with a single ambiguity set, the inner
$sup_{p},mathbb{E}^{p}[Q(x, xi)]$ is one worst-case expectation that
becomes an LP after TV dualization, and the full program reduces to a
polynomial-size MILP. In the present setting, the follower's
best-response condition introduces a emph{second} worst-case operator
that survives the Lagrangian relaxation as the inner $sup_{tilde{P}}$
in $bar{V}^{F}$. After TV dualization this operator becomes the
continuous variable $d_{s}$, and its product with the recovery-budget
multiplier $alpha_{k}$ produces the follower bilinear
coupling~eqref{eqOm-Fbilin}; an analogous mechanism through the
leader-side CVaR R--U layer produces~eqref{eqOm-Lbilin}. No finite
sequence of LP-duality steps absorbs $alpha_k cdot a_{s}$ or
$alpha_k cdot d_{s}$, because in each pair both factors are
continuous. The bilinearity is a structural feature of the
heterogeneous-belief setting and is the source of the complexity
result of Section~ref{seccomplexity}.
end{remark}
 
 
 
%==============================================================================
section{Computational Complexity}
label{seccomplexity}
%==============================================================================
 
The algorithmic representation~eqref{eqsingle-level} of
Theorem~ref{thmalgorithmic} is, under
Assumption~ref{asmfinite-support}, a continuous bilinear maximization
over a polyhedral set $Omega$ with bilinear constraints in
$alpha_k a_{s}$ and $alpha_k d_{s}$, parameterized linearly
by $x in mathcal{X}$. We characterize the complexity of the two
natural decision problems it defines evaluating
$V^{}_beta(bar{x})$ at a fixed $bar{x}$, and solving the leader's
full decision problem $min_{x} V^{}_beta(x)$.
 
paragraph{The inner subproblem.}
For fixed $bar{x} in mathcal{X}$, evaluating
$V^{}_beta(bar{x}) = sup_{(alpha, zeta) in Omega} F(bar{x}, alpha, zeta)$
is a continuous bilinear maximization in the variables
$(alpha, zeta^{L}, zeta^{F})$, with bilinearity arising solely
through the products $alpha_k a_{s}$ and $alpha_k d_{s}$ of
Remark~ref{remirreducibility}. Since the decision form of continuous
bilinear maximization is already NP-complete with a single bilinear
pair citep{alkhayyal1983bilinear}, the decision problem
``$V^{}_beta(bar{x}) geq theta$'' is NP-hard and its complement
``$V^{}_beta(bar{x}) leq theta$'' is coNP-hard.
 
paragraph{The full decision problem.}
The decision version of the leader's problem,
begin{equation}label{eqfull-decision}
    min_{x in mathcal{X}}; V^{}_beta(x) ;leq; theta,
end{equation}
takes the form
$exists, x in mathcal{X};forall, (alpha, zeta) in Omega; F(x, alpha, zeta) leq theta$,
i.e., an existential outer quantifier over the leader's binary
decision followed by a universal inner check over the bilinear
polytope $Omega$. The universal check is coNP-hard by the inner
subproblem discussion above; the alternation places the full problem
in $Sigma_2^{p}$.
 
begin{lemma}[Complexity upper bound]label{lemSigma2}
The decision problem~eqref{eqfull-decision} belongs to $Sigma_2^{p}$.
end{lemma}
 
Membership follows from the $existsforall$-structure the outer
existential certificate $x in mathcal{X}$ has polynomial size, and
by the NP-completeness of continuous bilinear maximization
citep{alkhayyal1983bilinear}, the inner universal check
$forall (alpha, zeta) in Omega F(x, alpha, zeta) leq theta$
is in coNP.
 
begin{remark}[On $Sigma_2^{p}$-hardness]label{remSigma2-hard}
We expect~eqref{eqfull-decision} to be $Sigma_2^{p}$-hard. The
$existsforall$ alternation together with the continuous bilinear
coupling $alpha_k a_{s}, alpha_k d_{s}$ between the outer
existential and inner universal variables is the standard mechanism by
which two-stage problems escape NP, and a natural reduction would be
from $existsforall$-3SAT with bilinear constraints encoding the
clause structure across the two quantifier blocks. A complete proof
is left to future work; absent it, only the upper bound of
Lemma~ref{lemSigma2} is rigorously established here.
end{remark}
 
The $Sigma_2^{p}$ upper bound of Lemma~ref{lemSigma2} by itself
does not rule out a polynomial-size MILP encoding. Under the
$Sigma_2^{p}$-hardness conjecture of Remark~ref{remSigma2-hard},
however, no such encoding can exist unless the polynomial hierarchy
collapses to its first level. Whether or not the conjecture is
established, the continuous bilinear coupling of
Remark~ref{remirreducibility} already rules out a finite-step
LP-duality reduction to MILP any tractable algorithm must therefore
(i) accept exponential worst-case running time, (ii) employ a non-LP
approach to the bilinear coupling, or (iii) exploit problem-specific
structure to keep practical instances tractable. The Benders
decomposition of Section~ref{secbenders} pursues (ii) for the
subproblem and (iii) for the master, generating valid cuts under both
exact (spatial branch-and-bound on the bilinear subproblem) and
$alpha$-restricted (LP-only) solves.


 
 
%==============================================================================
section{Benders Decomposition}
label{secbenders}
%==============================================================================
 
We develop a Benders decomposition for $min_x V^_beta(x)$ tailored to
the bilinear single-level form~eqref{eqsingle-level}. The outer master
problem (OMP) maintains a piecewise-linear lower envelope of
$V^_beta(cdot)$ in $x$ and selects a candidate
$bar{x} in mathcal{X}$; the inner subproblem (ISP) evaluates
$V^_beta(bar{x})$ and returns an affine cut in $x$. Two features of
the algorithm distinguish it from standard Benders (i) cut validity
under emph{inexact} (local-optimal) subproblem solves, which we exploit
to use a fast local bilinear solver for most iterations and reserve the
expensive global solver for a smaller subset (Section~ref{subseccut-validity}); and (ii)
a family of min-cut valid inequalities derived from the LP duality
between max-flow and min-cut (Section~ref{subsecmin-cut-VI}).
 
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
subsection{Cut generation exact and $epsilon$-optimal}
label{subseccut-validity}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
The OMP at iteration $K$ is
begin{equation}label{eqOMP}
    (text{OMP}_K) qquad min_{x in mathcal{X},, t_0 in mathbb{R}}; t_0 quad text{s.t.}quad t_0 geq ell^{(i)}(x),; i = 1, ldots, K-1,
end{equation}
with $ell^{(i)}  mathcal{X} to mathbb{R}$ affine cuts (specified below). Denote an
optimal solution $(bar{x}^{(K)}, bar{t}_0^{(K)})$; $bar{t}_0^{(K)}$ is
a valid lower bound on $min_x V^_beta(x)$. The ISP at $bar{x}^{(K)}$ is
begin{equation}label{eqISP}
    V^_beta(bar{x}^{(K)}) ;=; sup_{(alpha, zeta) in Omega}; F(bar{x}^{(K)}, alpha, zeta),
end{equation}
a continuous bilinear maximization in $(alpha, zeta)$
(Section~ref{seccomplexity}).
 
paragraph{Cut form.}
Let $(alpha^, zeta^) in Omega$ be any feasible point of~eqref{eqISP}, where $zeta^ = (zeta^{L}, zeta^{F})$ collects the LP duals introduced in Theorem~ref{thmalgorithmic}. Define
begin{equation}label{eqcut}
    ell^{(K)}(x) ;=; F(x, alpha^, zeta^) ;=; mathfrak{a}(alpha^, zeta^) ;+; textstylesum_{k} pi_k^{x}(alpha^, zeta^), x_k,
end{equation}
where, reading off the coefficients of $x_k$ and the constant terms
of~eqref{eqdual-obj},
begin{subequations}label{eqcut-coeffs}
begin{align}
    mathfrak{a}(alpha^, zeta^) ;&=; textstylesum_{s} d_{0}^{!top}, hat{y}^{s}
        ;-; pi^{U}!!sum_{k,s}!big(hat{rho}_{k}^{s,3} + tilde{rho}_{k}^{s,3}big)
        ;-; lambda^{U}!sum_{k} rho_{k}^{0,3}, label{eqcut-int}
    pi_k^{x}(alpha^, zeta^) ;&=; -pi^{U}!sum_{s}!big[(hat{rho}_{k}^{s,1} + tilde{rho}_{k}^{s,1}) - (hat{rho}_{k}^{s,3} + tilde{rho}_{k}^{s,3})big]
        ;-; lambda^{U}!big(rho_{k}^{0,1} - rho_{k}^{0,3}big). label{eqcut-slope}
end{align}
end{subequations}
Crucially, the cut $ell^{(K)}$ is an emph{affine function of $x$ with
fixed coefficients} $F(x, alpha^, zeta^)$ is the value of the
emph{Lagrangian dual at the realized dual iterate} $(alpha^, zeta^)$,
not the value of the ISP optimization itself.
 
begin{proposition}[Cut validity]label{propcut-validity}
For any feasible $(alpha^, zeta^) in Omega$ and any
$x in mathcal{X}$,
begin{equation}label{eqcut-valid}
    ell^{(K)}(x) ;leq; V^_beta(x).
end{equation}
If $(alpha^, zeta^)$ is a global optimum of~eqref{eqISP} at
$bar{x}^{(K)}$, then $ell^{(K)}(bar{x}^{(K)}) = V^_beta(bar{x}^{(K)})$.
end{proposition}
 
begin{proof}
$Omega$ does not depend on $x$ by Theorem~ref{thmalgorithmic}, so for
any feasible $(alpha^, zeta^) in Omega$ and any $x in mathcal{X}$,
[
    ell^{(K)}(x) ;=; F(x, alpha^, zeta^) ;leq; sup_{(alpha, zeta) in Omega} F(x, alpha, zeta) ;=; V^_beta(x).
]
Tightness at $bar{x}^{(K)}$ under global optimality is the definition.
end{proof}
 
The validity statement holds for emph{any} feasible
$(alpha^, zeta^) in Omega$, not only globally optimal ones. This
decouples cut extraction from global optimization of the bilinear
subproblem, and is the foundation of the inexact-solver schedule below.
 
paragraph{Exact and $epsilon$-optimality cuts.}
The bilinear ISP is NP-complete in general (Section~ref{seccomplexity})
and is the dominant per-iteration cost. We grade the solver effort
 
smallskip
noindentemph{Exact cut.} If~eqref{eqISP} is solved to global
optimality by a spatial branch-and-bound solver, the resulting
$ell^{(K)}$ is tight at $bar{x}^{(K)}$ and yields a valid upper bound
$overline{V} leftarrow min{overline{V}, F(bar{x}^{(K)}, alpha^, zeta^)}$.
 
smallskip
noindentemph{$epsilon$-optimality cut.} If~eqref{eqISP} is solved
only to a local optimum --- i.e., the solver returns a stationary point
$(hatalpha, hatzeta) in Omega$ satisfying first-order conditions but
not certified global --- then by Proposition~ref{propcut-validity} the
cut $ell^{(K)}(x) = F(x, hatalpha, hatzeta)$ is still valid; only
the global tightness at $bar{x}^{(K)}$ is forfeited, so the cut may be
loose and $overline{V}$ is not updated. We call such a cut an
emph{$epsilon$-optimality cut} it is the cut returned at any
$epsilon$-stationary feasible point, valid uniformly in
$x in mathcal{X}$ and slope-exact at $(hatalpha, hatzeta)$.
 
The asymmetry between cost and validity --- the local solve is
typically one to two orders of magnitude faster than the global solve
while producing a fully valid cut --- motivates the schedule of
Section~ref{subsecalgorithm}.
 
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
subsection{Min-cut valid inequalities for the master a partial Benders construction}
label{subsecmin-cut-VI}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
The cuts of Section~ref{subseccut-validity} are LP-derived linear
functions in $x$ that, taken as a family, contain no a-priori
information about the underlying network. The OMP at iteration $K = 1$
therefore admits the trivial $t_0 = 0$ for arbitrary $x in mathcal{X}$,
and the lower bound remains uninformative for many early iterations.
This is a known limitation of standard Benders decomposition when the
subproblem encodes combinatorial structure absent from the master
citep{rahmaniani2017benders}.
 
The remedy is to inject a relaxation of the subproblem structure
directly into the master, a strategy known as emph{partial Benders
decomposition} or emph{scenario creation}
citep{crainic2021partial} the master is enriched with valid
inequalities derived from a tractable relaxation of the subproblem,
typically obtained by aggregating scenarios or by retaining a tractable
substructure. In the present setting, the maximum-flow specialization
of Section~ref{subsecNIG-model} provides such a substructure the
follower's per-scenario evaluation $Q(x, bar{h}, xi^j)$ is a
max-flow LP, and its min-cut dual offers a clean linear-in-$x$
relaxation once a recovery $bar{h}$ and a probability vector $hat{q}$
are fixed. We exploit this to derive a family of emph{min-cut valid
inequalities} (min-cut VIs), pre-populated into the OMP as a network-aware
cut pool. The construction uses two ingredients specific to network
interdiction the LP duality between max-flow and min-cut, and the
McCormick linearization of the binary--continuous bilinearity
$x_k delta_k^j$.
 
For interdiction $x$, recovery $bar{h} in H$, and scenario $j$, the
residual capacity on arc $k$ is $u_k^j(x, bar{h}) = c_k^j(1 - v_k^j x_k) + bar{h}_k$,
and the max-flow under $u^j(x, bar{h})$ admits the min-cut LP
begin{equation}label{eqmincut-LP}
    Q(x, bar{h}, xi^j) ;=; min_{delta^j, tau^j}; textstylesum_k u_k^j(x, bar{h}),delta_k^j
    ;;text{s.t.};; delta_k^j geq tau_{j(k)}^j - tau_{i(k)}^j,;; tau_s^j = 0,; tau_t^j = 1,; delta_k^j, tau_i^j in [0, 1].
end{equation}
Based on Theorem~ref{thmgame-theoretic}, $V^_beta(x) =
max_{alpha in mathcal{A}^(x, tilde{mathcal{D}})} sup_{p in hat{mathcal{D}}} mathrm{CVaR}_beta^{p}[Q(x, alpha, xi)]$.
Specializing $p$ to the leader-side center $hat{q} in hat{mathcal{D}}$
and using the data-driven identification $hat{q} = tilde{q}$ so that
$hat{q} in tilde{mathcal{D}}$, any $bar{h} in H$ that is optimal for
the nominal follower problem under $tilde{q}$ satisfies
$bar{h} in mathcal{A}^(x, tilde{q}) subseteq mathcal{A}^(x, tilde{mathcal{D}})$,
and hence
begin{equation}label{eqmincut-bound}
    V^_beta(x) ;geq; mathrm{CVaR}_beta^{hat{q}}[Q(x, bar{h}, xi)] ;geq; mathbb{E}^{hat{q}}[Q(x, bar{h}, xi)] - tau_beta,
end{equation}
where $tau_beta geq 0$ is an explicit bound on the CVaR--expectation
gap (Appendix~ref{appmincut-bound}). Substituting~eqref{eqmincut-LP}
for each scenario and linearizing $x_k delta_k^j$ via auxiliary
$w_k^j = x_k delta_k^j$ under McCormick envelopes yields the following
inequality.
 
begin{proposition}[Min-cut VI]label{propmincut-VI}
Under the data-driven specification $hat{q} = tilde{q}$, for any
$bar{h} in H$ that is optimal for the nominal follower problem under
$tilde{q}$, the constraint system
begin{align}
    t_0 &;geq; textstylesum_j hat{q}_j sum_k big[c_k^j (delta_k^j - v_k^j w_k^j) + bar{h}_k delta_k^jbig] - tau_beta, label{eqmincut-VI-obj}
    & delta_k^j geq tau_{j(k)}^j - tau_{i(k)}^j,;; tau_s^j = 0,; tau_t^j = 1,;; delta_k^j, tau_i^j in [0,1] quad forall k, j,
    & 0 leq w_k^j leq x_k,;; delta_k^j - (1 - x_k) leq w_k^j leq delta_k^j quad forall k, j,
end{align}
is valid in the MP.
end{proposition}
 
The proof is in Appendix~ref{appmincut-VI-proof}. Two recovery choices
are useful in practice $bar{h} = 0$ (follower does not recover) and
$bar{h} = alpha^{(ell)}$ where $alpha^{(ell)}$ is the
$alpha$-iterate from the $ell$-th subproblem (which lies in $H_alpha = H$ by
Remark~ref{remalpha-h} and is admissible for the nominal follower
under $tilde{q}$). The latter dominates the former by monotonicity of
$Q(x, bar{h}, xi^j)$ in capacity.
 
We initialize the OMP with $L leq 3$ such VIs, including
$bar{h} = 0$, and add subsequent VIs on demand when the OMP--ISP gap
fails to close at the rate suggested by the cut schedule.
 
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
subsection{Overall algorithm procedure}
label{subsecalgorithm}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
The algorithm alternates between the OMP and the bilinear ISP. At each
iteration, the OMP is re-solved to produce a candidate $bar{x}^{(K)}$
and a lower bound $bar{t}_0^{(K)}$; the ISP at $bar{x}^{(K)}$ then
returns a feasible dual iterate $(alpha^, zeta^) in Omega$ from
which the affine cut~eqref{eqcut} is constructed and appended to the
OMP. Since Proposition~ref{propcut-validity} guarantees validity at
any feasible $(alpha^, zeta^)$, we grade the ISP solver effort to
the iteration
begin{itemize}
item emph{Most iterations} use a emph{local} bilinear solver
(stationary-point search; in our implementation Gurobi's local-optimal
mode for non-convex quadratically constrained problems). The returned
$(hatalpha, hatzeta) in Omega$ is $epsilon$-stationary and yields
an $epsilon$-optimality cut by Proposition~ref{propcut-validity}.
The upper bound $overline{V}$ is emph{not} updated in these iterations
since the local solve produces no certificate of global optimality.
 
item emph{A subset of iterations} (every $m$-th, with $m in {4, 5}$
in our experiments) invokes a emph{global} spatial branch-and-bound
solver on~eqref{eqISP}. The exact cut at the global optimum is added
to the OMP and the upper bound is updated to
$overline{V} leftarrow min{overline{V}, F(bar{x}^{(K)}, alpha^, zeta^)}$.
end{itemize}
The OMP is pre-populated with min-cut VIs (Section~ref{subsecmin-cut-VI})
to ensure the lower bound is non-trivial from the first iteration. The
iteration terminates when the relative gap
$(overline{V} - bar{t}_0^{(K)})  overline{V}$ falls below a
prescribed tolerance $epsilon_{mathrm{tol}}$ (or a wall-time budget is
exhausted). The full procedure is summarized in
Algorithm~ref{algbenders}.
 
begin{algorithm}[htbp]
caption{Benders procedure with $epsilon$-optimality and exact cuts}
label{algbenders}
begin{algorithmic}[1]
State Initialize OMP with min-cut VIs (Proposition~ref{propmincut-VI})
State Set $K gets 1$, $overline{V} gets +infty$, and tolerance $epsilon_{mathrm{tol}}$
Repeat
    State Solve OMP$_K$~eqref{eqOMP} to obtain $(bar{x}^{(K)}, bar{t}_0^{(K)})$
    If{$K bmod m = 0$} Comment{exact cut (every $m$-th iteration)}
        State Global solve of ISP~eqref{eqISP} $Rightarrow;(alpha^{}, zeta^{}) in Omega$ (certified)
        State $overline{V} gets min{overline{V},; F(bar{x}^{(K)}, alpha^{}, zeta^{})}$
        State $(alpha^{dagger}, zeta^{dagger}) gets (alpha^{}, zeta^{})$
    Else Comment{$epsilon$-optimality cut (cheap local solve)}
        State Local solve of ISP~eqref{eqISP} $Rightarrow;(hatalpha, hatzeta) in Omega$ ($epsilon$-stationary)
        State $(alpha^{dagger}, zeta^{dagger}) gets (hatalpha, hatzeta)$
    EndIf
    State Construct cut
        $ell^{(K)}(x) = mathfrak{a}(alpha^{dagger}, zeta^{dagger}) + sum_{k} pi_k^{x}(alpha^{dagger}, zeta^{dagger}), x_k$
        from~eqref{eqcut-int},~eqref{eqcut-slope}
    State Append $ell^{(K)}$ to OMP and set $K gets K + 1$
Until{$(overline{V} - bar{t}_0^{(K)})  max{overline{V}, 1} leq epsilon_{mathrm{tol}}$}
State Return $(bar{x}^{(K)}, overline{V})$
end{algorithmic}
end{algorithm}
 
begin{proposition}[Finite termination]label{propfinite}
Suppose the components of $x$ participating in the linking
constraint~eqref{eqbx} are restricted to ${0, 1}$ --- i.e., the
linking projection
$mathcal{X}_{mathrm{link}} = {(x_1, ldots, x_m) in {0, 1}^{m}  x in mathcal{X}}$
is a finite subset of the binary set, with
$mathcal{X}_{mathrm{link}} leq 2^{m}$. Then the algorithm
terminates in finitely many iterations.
end{proposition}
 
begin{proof}
Suppose, for contradiction, that the algorithm does not terminate in
finitely many iterations. Then it produces an infinite sequence
${bar{x}^{(K)}}_{K geq 1}$ in $mathcal{X}_{mathrm{link}}$, and
by pigeonhole some $bar{x}^{star} in mathcal{X}_{mathrm{link}}$
recurs infinitely often. Because the global solver is scheduled at
every $m$-th iteration, $bar{x}^{star}$ is encountered at a
global-solve iteration at least once, producing an exact cut
$ell^{(K^{star})}(bar{x}^{star}) = V^{}_beta(bar{x}^{star})$
and the upper-bound update
$overline{V} leq V^{}_beta(bar{x}^{star})$. Thereafter, any
iteration at which the OMP selects $bar{x}^{star}$ satisfies
$bar{t}_0^{(K)} geq ell^{(K^{star})}(bar{x}^{star}) = V^{}_beta(bar{x}^{star}) geq overline{V}$,
so the gap closes at $bar{x}^{star}$ and the algorithm terminates ---
a contradiction.
end{proof}
 
The argument hinges on the finiteness of $mathcal{X}_{mathrm{link}}$,
not on bounding the cut-slope multiplicity the binary linking is what
makes the leader's decision space ISP-discriminating, and the periodic
global solve ensures that each recurring binary configuration
eventually receives an exact evaluation. The hypothesis is local to
this section --- the structural and complexity results of
Sections~ref{secreformulation}--ref{seccomplexity} require no such
restriction --- and is satisfied by the network-interdiction
applications of Section~ref{secexperiments}. For applications in
which $mathcal{X}_{mathrm{link}}$ is general mixed-integer or
continuous, the algorithm remains valid as a primal--dual procedure
that produces a monotone lower bound and, when global solves succeed,
a valid upper bound; only the finite-termination guarantee is lost.
 
The local solver dominates the per-iteration cost in our experiments
(Section~ref{secexperiments}). Each local solve completes in seconds,
the global solve is one to two orders of magnitude slower, and the
$epsilon$-optimality cuts produced by the local solver close most of
the OMP--ISP gap; the global solve serves primarily to validate the
incumbent upper bound and to break out of regions where the local
solver returns stationary points far from the global optimum.

section{Numerical Experiments}
label{secexperiments}
%==============================================================================
 
We assess the proposed model and the Benders procedure of
Section~ref{secbenders} along three dimensions. Section~ref{subsecexp-setup}
specifies the test instances and the sampling protocol;
Section~ref{subsecexp-compute} reports the wall-clock performance of
three algorithmic variants;
Section~ref{subsecexp-vor} quantifies the in-sample, model-implied
value of robustness against the SAA baseline; and
Section~ref{subsecexp-oos} reports out-of-sample CVaR performance
under in-distribution and perturbed sampling regimes. All experiments
are implemented in JuliaJuMP~citep{lubin2023jump} and run on
[hardware specs to be inserted]. Both the local and global bilinear
subproblem solves use Gurobi the local solver invokes
texttt{OptimalityTarget = 1} (first-order $varepsilon$-stationary
mode) and the global solver invokes texttt{NonConvex = 2} (spatial
branch-and-bound). LP subproblems are solved by Gurobi in default mode.
 
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
subsection{Experimental setup}
label{subsecexp-setup}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 
paragraph{Networks.} We consider five undirected $s$--$t$ networks of
varying size and topology a synthetic $5 times 5$ grid and four
backbone instances (Polska, Abilene, Nobel-US, Sioux Falls). Their
vertex and arc counts are reported in
Table~ref{tabcomputational}.
 
paragraph{Interdictable arcs and budget.} The leader's budget is set
to $Gamma = 2$ across all networks, with linking constraint
$sum_{k in mathcal{A}_I} x_k leq Gamma$. For Grid $5 times 5$,
the interdictable set $mathcal{A}_I$ excludes all source-outgoing and
sink-incoming arcs. For the four backbone networks, $mathcal{A}_I$
contains all arcs subject to the cardinality constraints
$sum_{k in delta^{+}(s)} x_k leq delta^{+}(s) - 1$ and
$sum_{k in delta^{-}(t)} x_k leq delta^{-}(t) - 1$ (at least
one source-outgoing and one sink-incoming arc must remain operational).
 
paragraph{Scenario generation.} The empirical scenario set has
cardinality $S = 10$. For each scenario $s in [S]$, the baseline arc
capacity $c^{s} in mathbb{R}^{mathcal{A}}_{+}$ and the
interdiction-success vector $v^{s} in [0, 1]^{mathcal{A}}$ are
drawn independently from network-specific intervals; the generation
procedure is detailed in Appendix~ref{appscenario-gen}. The empirical
distribution on these $S$ scenarios is uniform,
$hat{q}_{s} = 1  S$, and the follower-side belief center is identified
with the leader's, $tilde{q} = hat{q}$.
 
paragraph{Validation and test sampling.}label{parsampling}
To assess sensitivity to misspecification of the scenario probability
vector, we draw $N_{mathrm{val}} = N_{mathrm{test}} = 500$ probability
vectors $q^{(n)}$ from a symmetric Dirichlet
$mathrm{Dir}(gamma , mathbf{1}_{S})$, with concentration parameter
$gamma in {0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 1.0}$ controlling the dispersion around
$hat{q}$ (smaller $gamma$ produces draws further from the uniform
center). The 500 validation draws are used to select the ambiguity
radius (described below); the 500 test draws are held out for the
out-of-sample evaluation of Section~ref{subsecexp-oos}. To assess
robustness to perturbations of the Dirichlet center itself, an
additional 500 perturbed test draws are generated for each perturbation
level $nu in {0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 5.0}$, where the Dirichlet center is
multiplicatively perturbed from $hat{q}$ in proportion to $nu$
(Appendix~ref{appperturbation}). We use $nu$ rather than $zeta$ to
avoid collision with the LP-dual variables $zeta^{L}, zeta^{F}$ of
Section~ref{subsecalgorithmic}.
 
paragraph{Risk levels, ambiguity radii, and selection rule.}
We evaluate CVaR risk levels $beta in {0.0, 0.05, 0.4, 0.7}$ and
ambiguity radii $hat{varepsilon} = tilde{varepsilon} = varepsilon in {0.1, 0.2, 0.5}$.
For the out-of-sample comparisons of Section~ref{subsecexp-oos} we
fix $beta = 0.4$. The radius $varepsilon^{}(mathrm{net})$ is
selected per network on the validation set, jointly across all
$gamma in {0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 1.0}$, by minimizing the 95th-percentile
of the validation out-of-sample CVaR (rule details in
Appendix~ref{appselection}). The selection is held fixed in the test
phase. We deliberately do not tune $varepsilon$ per $(mathrm{net}, gamma)$
to prevent the selection from using test-distribution information.

subsection{Computational performance}
label{subsecexp-compute}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 
We benchmark three algorithmic variants of the Benders procedure of
Section~ref{subsecalgorithm}
textbf{Plain BD} solves the global bilinear ISP~eqref{eqISP} at every
iteration;
textbf{Enhanced BD} uses $varepsilon$-optimality cuts from the local
bilinear solver at every iteration, with a global solve scheduled at
every $m = 5$-th iteration;
textbf{Enhanced BD + VIs} augments Enhanced BD with the min-cut valid
inequalities of Proposition~ref{propmincut-VI} pre-populated into the
OMP. All variants share a $10^{-4}$ relative-gap tolerance, a
6{,}000-second wall-time limit, and a 1{,}000-iteration cap. We fix
$beta = 0.4$ and $varepsilon = 0.2$.

The schedule of local-solve $varepsilon$-optimality cuts with periodic
global solves (Plain BD $to$ Enhanced BD) reduces wall-clock time by a
factor of $2.4 times$ to $9.6 times$ across the five networks, with
iteration counts compressed from several hundred (or the cap) to fewer
than $80$ in every case. The reduction is largest on Polska
($9.6 times$) and Grid $5 times 5$ ($4.6 times$), where the
per-iteration cost of the global bilinear solver dominates and is most
readily replaced by the local solver. The improvement on Plain BD

converge in $70$--$78$ iterations. Sioux Falls has the densest arc set

($1.2 times$), Nobel-US ($3.3 times$), Sioux Falls ($1.4 times$),

auxiliaries that encode the min-cut VIs inflate the per-OMP-solve cost
beyond the iteration savings. For all four backbone instances, the
VIs deliver a strict improvement in wall time.
 
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
subsection{Value of robustness}
label{subsecexp-vor}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 
For an interdiction $x$ and ambiguity radius $varepsilon$, define the
model-implied worst-case CVaR
begin{equation}label{eqPhi-VoR}
    Phi_beta(x; varepsilon) ;=; max_{(P, tilde{P}) ,in, hat{mathcal{D}}(varepsilon) times tilde{mathcal{D}}(varepsilon)} mathrm{CVaR}_beta^{P}!big[Q(x, alpha^{}(x), xi)big],
end{equation}
where $alpha^{}(x) in mathcal{A}^{}(x, tilde{mathcal{D}})$ is
the follower's worst-case admissible recovery under
$tilde{mathcal{D}}(varepsilon)$. Let $x^{}(varepsilon)$ denote
the DRO optimal interdiction at radius $varepsilon$ and let
$x^{mathrm{nom}} = x^{}(hat{varepsilon} = tilde{varepsilon} = 0)$
denote the SAA (nominal) optimal. The emph{value of robustness} at
$(beta, varepsilon)$ is
begin{equation}label{eqVoR}
    mathrm{VoR}_beta(varepsilon) ;=; frac{Phi_beta(x^{mathrm{nom}}; varepsilon) - Phi_beta(x^{}(varepsilon); varepsilon)}{Phi_beta(x^{}(varepsilon); varepsilon)} times 100 %.
end{equation}
$mathrm{VoR}_beta(varepsilon)$ quantifies the worst-case-CVaR
improvement that the DRO solution promises over the SAA solution, both
evaluated against the modeler's own ambiguity set of radius
$varepsilon$. It is, by construction, an in-sample, model-implied
measure --- distinct from the realized out-of-sample improvement
studied in Section~ref{subsecexp-oos}.
 
input{tables/vor_table.tex}

Table~ref{tab:vor} reports $mathrm{VoR}_beta(varepsilon)$ for every
(network, $beta$, $varepsilon$) combination. Across the table, three
patterns emerge.

First, $mathrm{VoR}_beta(varepsilon)$ is weakly non-decreasing in
$varepsilon$ for virtually every (network, $beta$) row: enlarging the
ambiguity set cannot reduce the worst-case gap between SAA and DRO, a
built-in model-consistency check. Second,
$mathrm{VoR}_beta(varepsilon)$ is broadly non-decreasing in $beta$,
reflecting that higher risk aversion places more weight on the upper
tail of the cost distribution where SAA and DRO interdictions diverge
most. For example, on Grid $5 times 5$ with $varepsilon = 0.5$,
$mathrm{VoR}$ rises from $3.0%$ at $beta = 0$ to $29.4%$ at
$beta = 0.4$; on Sioux~Falls the same column grows from $0.0%$ to
$18.4%$ at $beta = 0.7$.

Third, the magnitudes vary substantially across networks. The largest
values occur on Grid $5 times 5$ ($29.4%$ at $beta = 0.4$,
$varepsilon = 0.5$), Polska ($14.9%$), Abilene ($14.5%$), and
Sioux~Falls ($18.4%$ at $beta = 0.7$, $varepsilon = 0.5$), while
Nobel-US remains modest ($leq 9.0%$). The cross-network variation
correlates with the topology of the $s$--$t$ min-cut: networks whose
min-cut consists of a small number of arcs admit interdiction plans
that are largely invariant to the probability distribution, dampening
the in-sample $mathrm{VoR}$. We do not formalize this pattern; it is
reported as an empirical observation that motivates the cross-network
analysis of Section~ref{subsecexp-oos}.

An important caveat is that $mathrm{VoR}_beta(varepsilon)$ is an
in-sample, model-implied quantity: it states that the DRO solution
dominates the SAA solution under the modeler's own worst case, not
under the realized distribution. In our experiments the in-sample
$mathrm{VoR}$ is largest at $varepsilon = 0.5$, but the
validation-selected radius (Section~ref{subsecexp-setup}) is typically
$varepsilon in {0.1, 0.2}$, consistent with the standard
conservatism--generalization trade-off of DRO. We therefore use the
in-sample $mathrm{VoR}$ as an instance-level diagnostic for
identifying networks on which the full DRO formulation is most likely
to deliver realized improvement, rather than as a proxy for the OOS
gain itself.
 
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
subsection{Out-of-sample performance}
label{subsecexp-oos}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 
For each network we fix the per-network ambiguity radius
$varepsilon^{*}(mathrm{net})$ selected on the validation set
(Section~ref{subsecexp-setup}) and compute three interdictions at
$beta = 0.4$: the nominal (SAA) solution
$x^{mathrm{nom}} = x^{*}(hat{varepsilon} = tilde{varepsilon} = 0)$,
the partial-DRO solution
$x^{mathrm{par}} = x^{*}(hat{varepsilon} = varepsilon^{*}, tilde{varepsilon} = 0)$
(leader-side ambiguity only), and the full-DRO solution
$x^{mathrm{full}} = x^{*}(hat{varepsilon} = tilde{varepsilon} = varepsilon^{*})$.
The validation-selected radii are $varepsilon^{*} = 0.1$ for
Grid~$5 times 5$, Polska, and Sioux~Falls, and $varepsilon^{*} = 0.2$
for Abilene and Nobel-US. All three solutions are evaluated on a
held-out test set (seed~43, independent of the validation draws) under
two regimes:
begin{itemize}
item emph{In-distribution test:} $500$ draws
$q^{(n)} sim mathrm{Dir}(gamma , mathbf{1}_{S})$ for
$gamma in {0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 1.0}$.
item emph{Out-of-distribution test:} $500$ draws from
$mathrm{Dir}(gamma cdot tilde{q}_{zeta})$ for
$gamma in {0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 1.0}$ and perturbation level
$zeta in {0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 5.0}$, where $tilde{q}_{zeta}$ is a
multiplicatively perturbed center (Appendix~ref{appperturbation}).
end{itemize}
For each test draw $q^{(n)}$, the realized CVaR is
$mathrm{CVaR}_beta^{q^{(n)}}!big[Qbig(x, h^{*}(tilde{q}^{(n)}), xibig)big]$,
where $h^{*}(tilde{q}^{(n)})$ is the follower's optimal recovery
under the realized $tilde{q}^{(n)}$. The empirical distribution of
these $500$ realized CVaRs is summarized by box plots in
Figure~ref{fig:oos-boxplot}; the box spans the interquartile range
(IQR), whiskers mark the 5th and 95th percentiles, and diamonds
indicate the mean.

paragraph{Tail-risk reduction.}
The primary design target of the full-DRO formulation is the upper
tail of the realized CVaR distribution, measured by the 95th percentile
(upper whisker). On Grid~$5 times 5$ at $gamma = 0.1$ --- the most
dispersed in-distribution regime --- the 95th-percentile CVaR drops from
$43.7$ (Nominal) to $35.4$ (Full), a $19.0%$ reduction. Under the
strongest perturbation $zeta = 5.0$ the reduction persists: the
95th-percentile falls from $38.9$ to $35.4$ ($8.9%$). On
Sioux~Falls the reduction is consistent at $5.2$--$5.7%$ across all
$gamma$ values. On Abilene the Full solution reduces the
95th-percentile by $7.2%$ at $gamma = 0.1$. On Polska the
reduction reaches $10.9%$ at $gamma = 0.1$ but narrows to $< 1%$
at $gamma = 1.0$, where the Dirichlet draws concentrate near the
uniform $hat{q}$ and robustness provides little additional protection.

paragraph{Median-cost trade-off.}
The tail-risk reduction does not come for free in the median. On
Grid~$5 times 5$ the Full solution's median ($28.4$) exceeds the
Nominal's ($25.8$) by approximately $10%$; a similar pattern appears
on Polska ($14.8$ vs.\ $13.9$) and Abilene ($11.3$ vs.\ $10.1$).
This is the standard conservatism--generalization trade-off of DRO: the
robust interdiction targets arcs that perform well under worst-case
distributions, which may not be the arcs that minimize cost under the
most likely distribution. On Sioux~Falls, by contrast, the median is
nearly identical across solutions ($13.6$--$13.7$), indicating that
the DRO interdiction achieves tail protection without inflating
typical-case cost.

paragraph{Partial vs.\ Full DRO.}
On three of the five networks (Polska, Nobel-US, Sioux~Falls), the
Partial and Nominal solutions coincide: the validation-selected radius
$varepsilon^{*} = 0.1$ or $0.2$ does not perturb the leader-side
distribution enough to change the interdiction at $tilde{varepsilon} = 0$.
The Full solution, which additionally hedges the follower's belief,
selects a different interdiction on all five networks (see
Table~ref{tab:oos-interdictions} below). On Abilene, the Partial
solution actually raises the 95th percentile relative to Nominal
($17.3$ vs.\ $15.6$ at $gamma = 0.1$), while the Full solution
reduces it to $14.5$ --- illustrating that leader-side ambiguity alone
can be counterproductive when follower-side uncertainty is not
simultaneously hedged.

paragraph{Cross-network variation.}
The OOS improvement varies with network topology, paralleling the
in-sample $mathrm{VoR}$ of Section~ref{subsecexp-vor}. The largest
tail-risk gains are on Grid~$5 times 5$ (up to $19%$) and
Sioux~Falls ($approx 5.5%$); the most modest gains are on Nobel-US,
where the Full solution's 95th percentile can exceed the Nominal's
under concentrated $gamma$ (a consequence of the over-conservatism
discussed above). Sparse-min-cut backbones admit interdiction plans
that are largely invariant to the realized distribution, narrowing the
OOS gap between SAA and DRO. The full formulation delivers the
largest realized gains on instances in which the interdiction budget
is comparable to the bottleneck width and the realized distributional
shift exceeds the calibration of $hat{q}$.
 
%==============================================================================
section{Conclusion}
label{secconclusion}
%==============================================================================
 
We have studied distributionally robust zero-sum Stackelberg games under
incomplete information about the follower's distribution, formulated as
a pessimistic bilevel program with two ambiguity sets and a 2SLP follower
under interdiction-type lower-level coupling. The principal structural
finding is that the second worst-case operator introduced by the
heterogeneous-belief structure couples bilinearly with the
recovery-budget Lagrange multiplier; the coupling is irreducible under
LP duality, and the resulting program lies in $Sigma_2^p$ (with
$Sigma_2^p$-hardness conjectured), one level above standard two-stage
DR. A Lagrangian--Sion chain produces a structural form in which the
follower's ambiguity enters the leader's maximization through an
enlarged admissible recovery set, distinct from the way the leader's
ambiguity enters the objective. A dualization chain produces a
single-level mixed-integer bilinear reformulation on which a Benders
algorithm operates with $varepsilon$-optimality cuts from a local
bilinear solver at most iterations and periodic exact cuts from a global
solver, supplemented by min-cut valid inequalities derived from the LP
duality between max-flow and min-cut.
 
Three limitations of the framework deserve emphasis. First, the
symmetric expected-value sub-case is degenerate
(Proposition~ref{propsaturation}) under
$rho_L = rho_F = mathbb{E}$ and
$hat{mathcal{D}} subseteq tilde{mathcal{D}}$, the follower-side
radius is inactive, and the model collapses to a single-distribution
DR. This motivates our asymmetric CVaR-leader formulation but raises
the question of follower-side coherent risk, which we leave to future
work. Second, the empirical value of the full DR formulation
(Section~ref{secexperiments}) varies substantially with network
topology on backbones whose $s$--$t$ min-cut admits few comparable
targets, the nominal interdiction is already near-optimal under most
plausible distributions, and the DR improvement is modest. The
framework delivers the largest realized gains on instances in which
the interdiction budget is comparable to the bottleneck width and the
realized distributional shift exceeds the calibration of the empirical
$hat{q}$. Third, the exact bilinear subproblem remains the dominant
computational cost for the densest instances; a scenario-decomposed
bilinear solver, of the form developed for non-DR multistage SLPs in
citet{kang2024multistage}, is a natural algorithmic extension.
 
Two further directions extend the structural results of this chapter.
Wasserstein-type ambiguity sets in place of TV balls would replace the
LP-representable inner minimizations with second-order cone problems,
preserving the Lagrangian--Sion chain but at substantially higher
per-subproblem cost; type-$infty$ Wasserstein offers a reasonable
intermediate. The generalization beyond zero-sum to non-zero-sum
Stackelberg games (in which the leader and the follower have distinct
objective functionals, not just distinct distributions) would
substantially complicate the strong-duality argument of
Section~ref{subsecdual-coupling}; we do not anticipate that the
structural form of Theorem~ref{thmgame-theoretic} extends without
modification.
